Constructive Access: A Unified Reference for Inference under Restricted Physical Access
Abdinoor / 2D Labs / 13 October 2026

Scientific conclusions depend on the distinctions an apparatus preserves. This
monograph and research toolkit organize inference around an access contract:
the target, admissible mechanisms, preparation, interventions, detector,
calibration, error criterion and physical resources. Accessible record laws
provide a common language for classical and quantum instruments, statistical
decisions and thermodynamic inference. Exact certificates, distinguishing
protocols, finite-data confidence bounds and countermodels make the framework
constructive.

A central result concerns reversible scalar observations that nevertheless
require positive entropy production from every compatible realization within
a specified ordinary smooth compact hidden-diffusion class. The attained
minimum extends to an unobserved stochastic drive with zero mean angular
current. A replicated polynomial hierarchy connects these ideal-law results
to finite detector records, propagating uncertainty in diffusion scale, gain
and exposure under an explicit pointwise geometric relation and generator
bounds. Rare-region constructions show why those global assumptions cannot
be inferred without further structure.

The reference develops these results alongside hidden-state requirements,
intervention design, sampling ambiguities and resource tradeoffs. Reproducible
simulations retain calibration costs and unsuccessful regimes; published
measurement reanalysis remains exploratory. Established mathematical ingredients
are attributed separately from the constructions presented here. External
expert review, historical priority and new experimental validation
remain open.
