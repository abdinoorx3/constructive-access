1. Scope and overall assessment

The main population result is a mathematical theorem for stationary, smooth,
compact, uniformly elliptic hidden diffusions using ordinary full-state time
reversal, with a smooth deterministic detector. The ordinary-reversal entropy
rate is E[v^T D^-1 v]. That class is explicit and essential. The statement is
not a new universal physical law, a quantum result, or a claim of determining
every hidden rate from a scalar trajectory.

I independently derived the conditional identity with a=Sf, c=Af:
 g^2-K=E[c^2|U]-Var(a|U), U=(f,Gamma_f).
Pointwise metric Cauchy gives c^2<=Gamma_f e. Conditioning, taking the positive
part, dividing on Gamma_f>=epsilon and monotone convergence establish the
bound. At Gamma_f=0, positive definiteness forces c=0 and the numerator's
positive part is zero. No unsupported division or observed-Markov assumption
is used. Uniform semigroup remainders justify the L-infinity conditional
limits; all derivatives are bounded in the compact smooth model class.

The path-law invariance argument is sound within that class: dyadic realized
quadratic variation converges to an observable path functional, whose
continuous time derivative is 2Gamma_f. Although the construction reads a
time neighborhood, its ideal limit equals a function of the central complete
state, so it does not invalidate conditional past/future independence. A
finite-window volatility estimate is a different object and cannot be
substituted without new dependence and calibration analysis.

2. Attainment, coordinate changes and extensions

For the uniform cosine circle, a=-gamma Y, Gamma=gamma(1-Y^2), and
 K=gamma^2 Y^2-omega^2(1-Y^2).
The bound is exactly omega^2/gamma and the circle attains it. Every admissible
hidden diffusion with the same complete observed path law has the same local
conditional functional, which proves the claimed compatible-class minimum
without fixing hidden dimension or topology. This is a complete solution to
that specified population realization problem. It is not a finite-data proof
of exact path-law compatibility.

For even positive smooth rho and D on a circle with stationary flux J, Sf is
even and therefore measurable through cos(phi). Conditional reversible-drift
variance vanishes. One-dimensional metric Cauchy is equality away from the two
zero-probability detector critical points. Thus J^2 integral 1/(rho D) is the
attained minimum for every observed law in the stated family. Reflection
converts the hidden generator into its adjoint while fixing the scalar record,
so these positive-minimum scalar laws are reversible. This does not contradict
record-level data processing: the restricted realization class carries extra
physical assumptions.

The exact slack formula follows from m-[m-w]_+=min(m,w). The three stated
equality conditions are necessary and sufficient almost surely. Scalar smooth
invertible recalibration has its Ito correction cancel pointwise in g^2-K,
and Gamma scales by the same squared derivative. Conditioning refinement
monotonicity follows from total conditional variance and convexity of the
positive part. The dual completed square is valid for bounded functions and
truncated inverse volatility, but is not permission for unaccounted same-data
optimization. The matrix extension's positive-trace variational inequality and
metric pseudoinverse projection also hold, including singular observed G;
general singular-vector coordinate invariance is correctly not asserted.

3. Finite-resolution argument

The drift bias h(M2+ + M2-)/4 and product bias
h(M1- M2+ + M1+ M2-)/2 are valid. The latter need not contain an extra h^2 term,
because integrated semigroup increment quotients remain bounded by M1+/M1-.
The frozen score is the completed-square linear witness with both biases
subtracted before weighting. A pointwise upper envelope a>=Gamma is enough;
it need not equal Gamma. A conditional average is insufficient.

The score range uses -(q-u)(q-v)<=(u-v)^2/4 with the same central reading,
and is tighter than treating the two increments as unrelated ranges. The
conditional-Ito second-moment option uses genuine conditional past/future
independence. The empirical-Bernstein implementation uses the Maurer-Pontil
sample-variance theorem, not an illicit replacement of population variance
inside known-variance Bernstein. Exact sums and sums of squares, range inflation
for bounded score perturbation, and the final perturbation penalty are sound.
Logarithm and square-root arithmetic were independently checked against
100-decimal calculations.

Two clarifications were requested and incorporated in the proof text:
- Bounded unbiased detector noise must have zero conditional endpoint cross-
  covariances (conditional independence is sufficient); the central rule cannot
  be chosen from those noisy readings. Shared central noise then adds the
  favorable negative center-variance contribution to the expected score.
- External calibration failure can be added to alpha only when the theorem
  holds conditionally on pre-test information, e.g. independent calibration,
  or when a separate joint guarantee is supplied. Same-data calibration cannot
  silently preserve iid confidence. The frozen numerical bank has fixed rules.

For a deterministic rounded-center rule, q, retention and a are instantaneous
state functions. If |z0-Y0|<=delta, the circle envelope
 a(z0)=1-max(0,|z0|-delta)^2
dominates 1-Y0^2 pointwise. Keeping q, retention and a identical in ideal and
stored scores gives the reading perturbation bound
4(R/h+Q)delta/(h a_min). No random conditional-average substitution is hidden
in this construction. Output flooring requires a full grid-width penalty,
not the half-grid penalty for nearest rounding.

4. Counterexamples and restrictions actively checked

Connected variable-diffusivity torus: replacing Gamma by E[Gamma|Y] falsely
certifies 68.303... when sigma=64; the rational lower bound 19619/288>64
independently confirms the failure. Retaining Gamma restores the exact value.

Smooth nonlinear circle detector: an uncorrected finite-h conditional ratio
has a nonintegrable internal pole at finite true sigma=1. A fixed positive
volatility cutoff still gives a rigorously certified false bound above 1.
An invertible scalar recalibration gives a separate finite, nonsingular
counterexample: the naive finite-h integral is 11.540... at true sigma=1.
Thus exact volatility closure and invertible detector calibration do not remove
the time-step bias obligation. The valid zero-step theorem is invariant; its
uncorrected finite-step substitute is not.

Generalized parity can produce zero physical entropy despite positive ordinary
entropy, even for an even observed scalar. Independent sensor noise causes raw
realized quadratic variation to diverge with sampling frequency. Exposure
averaging gives a smooth trace with zero quadratic variation and violates the
instantaneous smooth detector contract. These are correctly presented as
scope restrictions and not suppressed benchmark failures.

8. Constructive consistency and numerical enclosure repair

The final population-to-finite-observation consistency theorem passes review.
For h=h0*m^-3, epsilon=gamma*m^-2, n=m^12, the circle tail loss is at most
sigma/m. The corrected score expectation lies between its ideal truncated
target minus 2Ch/epsilon and that target. With the stated fixed calibration,
this bias is O(m^-1). Its second moment is at most H^2*m^10 and score range
at most B0*m^8. Known-variance Bernstein therefore gives a sampling radius
at most H*sqrt(2 log(2/alpha))/m + 2B0*log(2/alpha)/(3m^4).

The two tails each receive alpha/2, proving the stated joint validity and
accuracy sandwich with failure at most alpha. Fixed-alpha consistency is
proved separately from Var(empirical mean)<=H^2/m^2 and the deterministic
population/radius limits; it is not incorrectly inferred from a fixed success
probability. The optional summable alpha_m gives almost-sure convergence by
Borel-Cantelli without assuming independence between successive experiments.
The conservative exponents n^-1/12 and declared-time T^-1/9 are sufficient,
not minimax or demonstrations of affordable apparatus operation. Successive
levels incur cumulative costs. Stored mean/score errors must vanish; changes
to a rounded detector's selector or geometry require their own proof.

The independent audit found an important arithmetic qualification: the existing
finite-resolution square-root helper has fixed absolute 90-bit rounding. A
positive final radius can bottom out at 2^-90, so a literal fixed-grid output
does not converge exactly to sigma at arbitrarily large m. Its finite-level
coverage remains valid; the asymptotic implementation claim needed repair.
The author independently identified the same issue. No frozen inference code
was changed. The new consistency wrapper instead encloses the fixed constants
H, B0, log(2/alpha) and sqrt(2 log(2/alpha)) once, and divides their rational
coefficients exactly by m and m^4. Its conservative radius tends to zero.
This also avoids comparing rounded per-m moment bounds with an unenclosed
real constant at a possible equality case.

Two additional independent tests establish exact protocol inequalities in
twelve rational square-root fixtures, check the wrapper's envelope against

floor. All deterministic design records, resource exponents, confidence
