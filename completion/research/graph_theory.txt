CONSTRUCTIVE CURRENT ACCESS ON PHYSICAL CHANNEL GRAPHS
Abdinoor / 2D Labs / 12 October 2026
Agent scope: graph structure, exact completion, independently checkable cuts.

STATUS AND ATTRIBUTION
The statements below are proved finite-model results. Their algebraic ingredients
are established conservation, linear identifiability, max-flow/min-cut duality,
and maximum spanning forests. They are not presented as newly discovered graph
theorems or physical laws. The potentially useful integration is an explicit
classification of physical-channel versus aggregate-current ambiguities, an
exact access design, and a bridge to calibrated physical intervention sensors.
The physical sensor bridge is developed separately by completion_physics; the
precision/energy extension by completion_robust. No laboratory data are used.

1. ACCESS CONTRACT

Let G=(V,E) be a finite undirected multigraph with a fixed reference direction
u_e -> v_e on every physical transition channel e. Self-loops are excluded.
Parallel edges represent distinct physical mechanisms between the same pair of
states. B has column -1 at u_e and +1 at v_e. The native stationary channel
current is j in R^E, and conservation is B j=0. Currents may have either sign.
Unless explicitly imposed, there is no upper activity, rate, affinity, or current
bound and no fixed thermodynamic driving constraint.
The cut formulas require an ordinary graph incidence matrix. A general chemical
stoichiometric balance matrix need not have that structure; use generic linear
identifiability and robust recovery rather than assuming the cut formulas apply.

An individual sensor reports j_e or a number z_e obeying |z_e-j_e|<=epsilon_e.
This sensor contract does NOT assume that the entire state trajectory is visible.
Full-state jump observation already identifies aggregate state-to-state rates
and their aggregate currents. The sparse-sensor problem applies to instruments
that supply only selected current estimates. Every lower bound here refers to
this retained-data contract; richer raw records may carry extra information.

Exact aggregate access reports the signed sum k_b of channel currents in a
specified parallel bundle b. Choose one canonical direction per bundle. If a
channel's reference orientation disagrees, its coefficient in the sum is -1.
Writing k=A j gives B=bar(B) A for the simple underlying graph. The supplied
graph and assignment of physical channels to bundles are assumed correct.

Available exact aggregate readings are treated as already acquired, with no
additional cost in graph completion. Their uncertainty cannot be silently set
to zero in a real application. The general robust linear-observation method is
needed when those readings also have uncertainty.

2. SINGLE TARGET: EXACT NECESSITY AND MINIMUM ACCESS

Fix an unmeasured target channel e with endpoints u,v, and an exact sensor set
S subset E\{e}. The following conditions are equivalent:

 (a) Every pair j,j' in ker B with j_S=j'_S has j_e=j'_e.
 (b) e is a bridge in the residual multigraph G\S.
 (c) S separates u from v in G\{e}.
 (d) There exists U subset V containing u and excluding v for which every
     crossing edge other than e belongs to S.

Proof. If there is a u-to-v path in G\(S union {e}), that path together with e
supports a signed circulation h with h_e=1 and h_S=0. Thus a is false. If there
is no such path, take U to be u's component after deleting S and e. Summing
conservation over U yields

    j_e = - sum_{f != e crossing U} sigma_U(f) j_f,

where sigma_U(f)=+1 for a reference arrow leaving U and -1 for one entering U.
All those currents are measured. The bridge and separation conditions are the
same graph statement. This proves both directions without a generic-rank or
nonzero-current assumption. In particular, an original bridge has j_e=0.

Give every eligible nontarget sensor a nonnegative additive cost c_f. Ineligible
edges have infinite acquisition cost. Every successful set contains the crossing
edges of some admissible u-v cut. Conversely, those crossing edges alone suffice.
Therefore the minimum added exact sensing cost is the u-v minimum cut in G\e.
Parallel edges must be retained: every parallel alternative to e crosses every
admissible cut and must be observed. If direct measurement of e is allowed, the
optimum is the smaller of its direct cost and the indirect minimum-cut cost.
The public target_completion function deliberately excludes direct measurement.

3. EXACT DETERMINISTIC MINIMAX PRECISION

Suppose S is a fixed nontarget sensor set and epsilon_s >= 0. The observation is
z_S=j_S+eta with |eta_s|<=epsilon_s; errors may be correlated or adversarial.
Define

 R = inf_f sup_{j in ker B, |eta_s|<=epsilon_s}
             | f(j_S+eta)-j_e |.

Then

 R = min_{u-v cuts C in G\e, C subset S} sum_{s in C} epsilon_s,

where the minimum is +infinity if no admissible cut exists. The infimum ranges
over all estimators, including nonlinear ones. The corresponding signed cut-sum
estimator attains the bound globally. No averaging can improve this particular
worst-case box model.

Proof of upper bound. Apply the exact cut identity to the measured z_s. The
error is a signed sum of eta_s, bounded by the sum of epsilon_s on the cut.

Proof of lower bound. Give sensor edges capacities epsilon_s and unsensed edges
infinite capacities in G\e. By undirected max-flow/min-cut there is a signed
flow of value R from v to u, bounded in absolute value by epsilon_s on every
sensor edge. Add current R on e (u to v). The result is a conserved vector h,
with h_e=R and |h_s|<=epsilon_s. Both current vectors h and -h produce the same
record z=0 by errors -h_S and h_S. Every estimate at z=0 has absolute error at
least R for one of those two possibilities. If the cut is absent, an entirely
unsensed path plus e produces arbitrarily scalable ambiguity, so R=infinity.

The theorem does not claim that every stochastic estimator is limited by this
radius under a specified noise distribution. It is a deterministic guarantee
for the full box of permissible errors. Known parameter bounds, correlations
excluding box corners, activity limits, or additional priors can reduce it.

INDEPENDENT CERTIFICATE. A finite certificate contains a cut U and a circulation
h. A checker verifies B h=0, h_e=R, |h_s|<=epsilon_s, absence of unmeasured cut
edges, and R=sum_cut epsilon_s. These establish matching upper and lower bounds
without rerunning any optimizer. An infinite certificate contains B h=0,
h_e=1, and h_S=0. In code, unsensed infinite capacities are safely replaced by
1+sum_sensor epsilon only after checking absence of an all-unsensed path.

4. MIXED AGGREGATE AND PHYSICAL-CHANNEL ACCESS

Let T be the bundles whose signed aggregate currents are known exactly. Let S
be the individually sensed channels. Write r_b for the number of unsensed
channels in bundle b. Form H on V with one edge for every bundle b not in T
with r_b>0. Isolated vertices are retained. Then the dimension of the homogeneous
current ambiguity is exactly

 dim ker [B; A_T; E_S]
   = sum_b max(r_b-1,0) + |E(H)|-|V|+components(H).

The first term is unresolved internal channel splitting. The second is a
network-level circulation ambiguity in the unknown aggregate currents.

Proof. Set sensed variations h_S=0. For each nonempty unsensed bundle, taking
its signed sum maps R^(r_b) onto one aggregate variable with a kernel of
dimension r_b-1. This map is a direct product across bundles. Known aggregate
variations are zero. The remaining aggregate variations must lie in the cycle
space of H, of dimension |E(H)|-|V|+components(H). Choose a representative
channel in each nonempty bundle to lift any feasible aggregate variation.
This proves surjectivity onto that cycle space, so dimensions add. Choosing
representatives gives a direct-sum decomposition; the dimension and obstruction
classification do not depend on that choice.

An unsensed target e in bundle b is identifiable exactly when

    r_b=1 AND (b in T OR b is a bridge of H).

If r_b>=2, offsetting signed currents on two unsensed channels in b preserves
every aggregate current and every conservation equation. If r_b=1, its current
variation equals the bundle variation up to orientation, so the bridge result
applies. A sensed target is of course identifiable, regardless of r_b.

KEY NEGATIVE RESULT. If all bundle totals are known, ker A is already a subset
of ker B. Conservation supplies no further information about the internal
channel split. The residual dimension is sum_b(r_b-1)_+. Macroscopic balance
equations cannot resolve how opposing microscopic channels share the same
aggregate current. A balanced additional state-level channel common to two
models with the same complete generator likewise cannot distinguish their
different decompositions. This obstruction requires access that differentiates
physical channels, not merely more processing of the same aggregate records.

5. MINIMUM COMPLETION WITH AGGREGATE ACCESS

For an unmeasured target e, every other channel in its bundle must be sensed.
Those sensors are forced by the two-channel ambiguity just proved. If that
bundle's aggregate is known, this completes recovery. Otherwise remove known
aggregate bundles from the ambiguity graph, retain one edge for each remaining
bundle, delete the target bundle, and find a minimum cut between its endpoints.
Removing an alternative bundle from the residual ambiguity requires sensing all
its channels, at cost equal to the sum of their sensor prices. Thus use these
bundle sums as cut capacities. Forced parallel measurements plus the bundle
minimum cut give a globally minimum added exact sensor set. A partial reading
of an alternative bundle cannot remove that bundle's aggregate ambiguity and
therefore cannot improve the minimum cost for this single target.

For simultaneous recovery of all physical-channel currents, every bundle can
retain at most one unsensed channel. In each known-total bundle, retain one
maximum-price channel unsensed; its current follows from the total. In each
unknown-total bundle, a retained channel makes that bundle an edge of H. These
edges must form a forest. Let each unknown bundle's weight be its maximum
channel price. A maximum-weight spanning forest maximizes saved sensing cost.
Therefore the global minimum is

 sum_allchannels c_e
   - sum_knownbundles max_channel c_e
   - maximum_forest_weight(unknown bundles, weights max_channel c_e).

These are graph/matroid optimization consequences, not claims of new max-flow
or spanning-tree theory. With all aggregates known, exactly one channel per
nonempty bundle can be left unmeasured. With no aggregates known, this reduces
to the familiar cotree measurement criterion on a multigraph.

6. PHYSICAL REALIZABILITY AND THE LIMIT OF THE MINIMAX CLASS

Take any conserved finite vector j and any strictly positive probability vector
pi. For each oriented physical channel choose traffic t_e>|j_e|/2 and set

 q_e(u_e,v_e) = (t_e+j_e/2)/pi[u_e],
 q_e(v_e,u_e) = (t_e-j_e/2)/pi[v_e].

Both rates are strictly positive. Their stationary net current is exactly j_e.
Summing outgoing minus incoming stationary flux gives -B j=0, so pi is
stationary for the sum generator. A connected graph yields an irreducible CTMC.
Thus signed-current ambiguity witnesses are realizable by positive finite-rate
Markov models with the same prescribed stationary occupations.

For finitely many bounded witness currents, one can choose the same t_e for
every witness. In particular, +h and -h in the minimax lower bound have common
pi and common edge traffic. Unbounded impossibility uses arbitrarily large
traffic and rates across the model class, not infinite rates in any one model.
If a physical problem specifies a rate, fuel, activity, or affinity constraint,
that must enter the model class and can invalidate an unbounded claim or reduce
the sharp minimax radius. Conservation alone is not a complete thermodynamic
description. Local detailed balance with freely chosen edge reservoirs can
realize these rate ratios, but fixed chemical stoichiometry or fixed reservoir
affinities impose additional conditions not proved here.

7. REPRODUCIBLE EXAMPLE AND NEGATIVE CONTROLS

code/graph_access.py uses four states and six physical channels: target A-B,
a parallel hidden A-B channel, and A-C, B-C, C-D, D-A. It supplies exact cut
certificates, a physical rate realization of the worst-case ambiguity, the
mixed-access dimension, and the minimum sensor designs. Results are written to
results/graph_access.json. Rationals are serialized as strings, never floats.

With uncertainties 1/100 on the parallel hidden A-B channel, 2/100 on A-C and
3/100 on D-A, the target radius is 3/50. All three measurements are required
for the relevant cut under that access. The matching circulation certificate
shows this is a sharp worst-case radius, not just a triangle-inequality bound.
When all aggregate bundle currents are known, exactly one internal degree of
freedom remains: the split between the two A-B channels. Revealing the other
A-B channel removes it. Conservation never removed it by itself.

The tests include ten named checks, including 160 seeded graphs against every
vertex cut, 240 mixed-observation configurations against independent rational
matrix ranks, 32 single-target designs against all sensor subsets, 32 all-target
designs against all sensor subsets, malformed/forged certificates, reversed
reference orientations, parallel channels, zero-price sensors, zero-radius
bridges, and positive physical rate reconstruction. The seeded graphs include
disconnected cases; connected physical irreducibility is a separate assumption.

Commands:
 python code/graph_access.py
 python -m unittest discover -s tests -p test_graph_access.py -v

Execution result: ten tests passed; the exact arithmetic result certificate is
valid. The graph tests use their own Gaussian elimination and do not call the
production graph classification to establish expected ranks or brute-force
optimality. Independent internal red-team review is separate from these author
checks. This is not external peer review.

8. DEPENDENCIES AND NEXT SCIENTIFIC BRIDGE

The graph solution is useful only if physical apparatus can deliver the assumed
local channel-current estimates without already revealing all currents. The
physical intervention work supplies a promising route: selectively scale both
directions of one channel, prepare the native stationary distribution, and read
the initial response of a local endpoint occupation. A finite-window response
requires controlled transient bias, calibration and preparation guarantees.
Its complete resource cost must include repeated preparation, control, sensing,
reset, acquisition, calibration and support. A graph sensor cost is a declared
accounting input, not by itself a first-principles energy value.

The combined deliverable should therefore prove one continuous implication:
physical access and calibration assumptions -> certified local current sensors
-> a minimum sufficient cut -> a target estimate with a declared precision
guarantee, with counterexamples showing which assumption failures restore the
ambiguity. That chain, not a renamed graph primitive, is the research target.
