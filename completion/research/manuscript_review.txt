1. Composition chapter

The joint outcome/interface defect has the correct 1/2 factor. The adaptive
bound uses unnormalized concrete branch probabilities, includes stopped leaves
and all outcomes, and propagates both output and hidden-update errors. Its
preparation term TV(mu C,nu) is necessary and present. The proof is a valid
branchwise telescoping/contraction argument. The source correctly presents
intertwining as sufficient rather than a characterization of all trace
equivalence.

The tensor defect epsilon1+epsilon2-epsilon1*epsilon2 is valid for product
instruments and product interfaces, via independent maximal local couplings.
Arbitrary added couplings require a separately checked joint defect. The
correlated-preparation warning is correct: matching local marginals alone
does not imply matching joint interface preparations.

The refinement algorithm and forced-split proof correctly establish the
coarsest DETERMINISTIC Markov interface refining the supplied partition, for
all declared instruments and all initial preparations. The text appropriately
avoids identifying this with the smallest linear realization, physical access
to hidden blocks, or a globally cheapest sensor. The CNOT example changes
permitted operations and therefore does not contradict passive equivalence.

2. Classical/quantum signs and protected coarse detectors

The classical convention is consistent: gamma_cl=-(1-r)J, so the endpoint
displacement Delta p_j has sign -J under suppression, while the separately
defined baseline-minus-endpoint response D_e has sign +J. The quantum section
uses Delta_B=p_B(t)-1/2 and Delta_B'(0)=-(1-r)J. Thus its short negative and
late positive responses genuinely violate the classical same-sign consequence
under the stated contract; there is no sign-convention mismatch.

The gateway corollary requires every post-intervention path from i into B to
first visit j. Its first-hit proof and strict positivity follow because there
is no occupation of B before that hitting time. This is a sufficient condition,
not a characterization of every coarse detector retaining positivity.

The explicit four-state counterexample is consistent: native selected-edge
current is 10/121, the half-suppression initial B displacement derivative is
-5/121, and the stationary B displacement is 10/2299>0. It violates the gateway
condition. The manuscript correctly warns that a coarse classical detector can
reverse and that the response crossing alone is not device-independent
quantumness. Delayed readout after release likewise needs a separate contract.

3. Calibrated quantum benchmark and experimental interpretation

The common gain 9/10 multiplies the certified ideal responses at t1=1/2 and
t2=9/4. Exact Taylor certificates establish

 - (9/10) Delta_B(t1) > 3/100,
   (9/10) Delta_B(t2) > 13/1000.

These are the gaps used by the statistical design. With all setting radii
1/2000, the smaller robust power margin is 13/1000-2(1/1000)=11/1000,
as stated. The exported count is 110192 per arm, 330576 total; active pulse
exposure is 110192*(1/2+9/4)=303028 time units. Preparation, detector and other
apparatus costs are explicitly additional. No energy optimum or measured
energy advantage is claimed.

The independent three-arm design avoids assuming a non-disturbing initial
quantum measurement. A shared affine detector maps both contrasts by the same
gain, preserving their sign product even for negative gain; the benchmark power
calculation specifically uses positive gain. A fixed shared offset cancels.
The actual records are assumed independent Bernoulli samples with fixed
probabilities under valid setting-level calibration envelopes. This is narrower
than arbitrary drifting/memory-bearing data and is correctly disclosed.

The rejection conclusion is rejection of the declared classical contract.
The text does not infer the singleton/gateway structure, physical channel
selectivity or preparation stationarity from the same two response means.
Inconclusive data do not establish classicality, and coherent below-threshold
models supply a relevant negative control. The matched incoherent example
shares stationary populations/current inventory, not full temporal records;
the quantum section explicitly preserves that distinction.

5. Final scope amendments

The quantum setup now explicitly assumes g>=0 without loss of generality.
Conjugation by Z sends X to -X, leaves Z populations invariant, and multiplies
each jump operator by -1 without changing its dissipator. The stated threshold
q=g/gamma is therefore valid for the declared convention. This fixes the
missing hypothesis identified by the independent red-team review; no physical
parameter family is lost and the already nonnegative-g code is unchanged.

The classical same-sign null also holds when arm s uses a different constant
retention factor 0<=r_s<1, as long as both arms suppress the same native
physical channel and satisfy the protected detector condition. Each underlying
endpoint population displacement is -(1-r_s)J_e H_s with H_s>0, so their
product is nonnegative. Native preparation and other dynamics must still agree.
Random positive timings independent of the process preserve this conclusion
by averaging fixed-time same-sign responses. Quantitative power needs a bound
on the actual contrast and does not follow from qualitative sign invariance.
