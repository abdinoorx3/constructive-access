1. Target recovery with conservation and shared calibration nuisance

Let j in R^m obey Bj=0. B is a known real matrix; an incidence matrix is the
physical network case. Observations are

    y = H j + A eta + e,       |e_i| <= epsilon_i,

where eta in R^k is an unknown, fixed nuisance vector and epsilon >=0 is known.
The target is b^T j. The parameter space is the ENTIRE conserved-current linear
space, eta is unrestricted, and the uncertainty set is the full Cartesian box.
These scope assumptions matter. Bounds on traffic, prior parameter bounds,
correlated errors, nonlinear responses and stochastic loss change the problem.

Theorem 1 (standard linear minimax geometry with an explicit nuisance contract).
The global deterministic minimax absolute error over all estimators is

 R = min_{w,phi} sum_i epsilon_i |w_i|
       subject to H^T w + B^T phi = b,  A^T w=0,
   = max_{h,z} b^T h
       subject to Bh=0, |Hh+Az| <= epsilon.

If the primal identity has no solution, R=+infinity. Otherwise both finite LP
values are attained, including zero error radii and redundant constraints.
An optimal linear estimator is w^T y. Nonlinearity cannot improve the stated
global worst-case radius.

Proof. The target identity cancels j's conservation constraints and all fixed
nuisance, so |w^T y-b^Tj|=|w^T e| <= sum epsilon_i |w_i|. For any dual-feasible
(h,z), the two mechanisms (j,eta)=(h,z) and (-h,-z), with errors -(Hh+Az) and
Hh+Az, generate the same record y=0 and targets +/- b^T h. Every estimator has
maximum error at least |b^T h|. LP duality gives equality. If the target identity
is infeasible, finite-dimensional annihilator duality supplies Bh=0,
Hh+Az=0 and b^T h !=0; scaling yields unbounded ambiguity. Existence of finite
optima follows from finite-dimensional LP attainment when its value is finite.
Equivalently append exact zero observations Bj=0 and use ordinary linear
optimal recovery on the augmented parameter (j,eta). QED.

An exact independent certificate consists of (w,phi,h,z) with the displayed
identities, feasible error bounds and matching primal/dual values. The checker
does not trust or rerun the optimizer. The synthesis implementation enumerates
small rational LP supports and vertices; it has no large-system scaling claim.

Example. On an oriented triangle conservation gives j1=j2=j3=J. Two differential
probes report y1=J+eta+e1 and y2=-J+eta+e2. Then (y1-y2)/2 has exact minimax error
(epsilon1+epsilon2)/2. At errors 1/10 and 1/5 this is 3/20. Two same-orientation
probes y1=J+eta+e1 and y2=J+eta+e2 leave J unidentifiable. Replacing the one shared
eta with two independently unknown offsets also restores ambiguity. Repetition
does not cancel a fixed arbitrary bias. The sign inversion must be a physically
justified probe response; changing the sign of an already recorded number also
changes the bias sign and creates no new information.

Physical realization of lower bounds. For a connected undirected support with
bidirectional channels, choose any positive stationary distribution pi. For any
finite circulation j choose a symmetric traffic t_e>|j_e| and channel rates
Q^e_uv=(t_e+j_e)/(2 pi_u), Q^e_vu=(t_e-j_e)/(2 pi_v). Conservation makes pi
stationary, all channels are positive, and the stationary channel current is
j_e. The least-favorable pair +/-h can use the SAME t>|h| and pi. Thus the
conserved-current ambiguity is not an artifact of allowing physically
impossible negative rates. This does not establish that a fixed bounded-rate
device can realize every circulation or every shared-nuisance response model.

2. Exact error for one hidden edge

The graph agent proves this result in graph_theory.txt and graph_access.py.
Let e0=u->v be a target edge of a finite undirected physical graph. Reference
orientations are bookkeeping; currents may have either sign. Remove e0. Some
remaining edge currents are observed with a full jointly allowed error box
|e_e|<=epsilon_e; this is not a probabilistic independence assumption.
Other edges are unobserved. All real circulations Bj=0 are allowed.

Then R equals the minimum u-v cut sum of epsilon_e, where an unobserved edge
has infinite capacity. A finite cut gives a signed sum estimator by conservation.
A matching maximum flow in the remaining graph, closed through e0, gives the
least-favorable circulation. An unobserved u-v path gives unbounded ambiguity.
If e0 is a bridge its stationary current is already zero and R=0.

This exact radius uses a DIFFERENT capacity from an installation-cost cut.
The same combinatorial structure has several interpretations, but cost, error
and physical energy must not be conflated.

3. A calibrated acquisition-energy law

Assume each available edge sensor has a DECLARED and justified joint error
contract

    epsilon_e(E_e) = delta_e + sqrt(a_e / E_e)
                    = delta_e + sqrt(q_e^3 / E_e),
    q_e = a_e^(1/3) >0, delta_e >=0, E_e>0.

E is acquisition energy in consistently declared units. E=0 means no reading,
hence infinite uncertainty. The idealized allocation is continuous. A real
experiment may require integer records, minimum pulse durations or power
limits, all of which alter this exact optimum. The law is an input, not a
universal detector law derived from thermodynamics. It can encode a conservative
finite-confidence envelope at a fixed declared confidence level. If that JOINT
envelope holds with probability at least 1-alpha, the resulting accuracy holds
with the same probability. No independence claim is needed for this implication.
Constructing that envelope may itself require confidence allocation and
calibration; a separate marginal confidence for each sensor is insufficient.

For a fixed cut C define A_C=sum_{e in C} q_e and D_C=sum_{e in C} delta_e. With
desired target-current error tau>0, the optimal acquisition energy on C is

    E_C^* = A_C^3 / (tau-D_C)^2,            tau>D_C,

with E_C^*=infinity for tau<=D_C and nonempty C. The optimal allocations are

    E_e = A_C^2 q_e / (tau-D_C)^2,
    epsilon_e = delta_e + (tau-D_C) q_e / A_C.

The empty cut has energy zero.

Proof. Let x_e=sqrt(q_e^3/E_e)>0. The constraint is sum_C x_e<=tau-D_C. Holder
gives

    sum_C q_e
    = sum_C (q_e^3/x_e^2)^(1/3) x_e^(2/3)
    <= (sum_C E_e)^(1/3) (sum_C x_e)^(2/3).

Rearranging yields the lower bound; the displayed allocation attains it. QED.

For fixed, nonadaptive resource allocations, the global optimum, allowing
arbitrary sensor subsets and nonlinear estimators,
is min_C E_C^*. Indeed every successful energy allocation has some finite cut
whose summed error is <=tau by the exact minimax cut theorem. Its total energy
is at least the fixed-cut optimum for that cut. Conversely a cut estimator with
the displayed allocation attains E_C^*. This is exact RELATIVE TO the full
adversarial uncertainty contract; it is not a universal physical energy limit.

Adaptive acquisition with repeated measurements and history-dependent budgets
requires a sequential error and resource contract and is outside this theorem.

4. Ideal calibration gives a polynomial design and a compact certificate

If delta_e=0 for every sensor, then

    E^* = [ minimum u-v cut sum_C q_e ]^3 / tau^2.

Thus one max-flow/min-cut computation jointly chooses which edge sensors to
use and their precision/energy allocations. Available sensors use capacity q_e;
unavailable sensors have infinite capacity. For rational q and tau the cut,
dual flow, allocations and energy are all rational and can be checked exactly.
The inputs q can equivalently represent the restricted rational sensor family
a=q^3. General algebraic q require algebraic or certified interval arithmetic;
the exact implementation does not silently approximate cube roots.

The independent certificate checker verifies graph conservation, capacity
bounds, cut/flow equality, the energy/error contract and equality in the energy
lower bound. It also binds the external q, tau and chosen graph to the claim.

5. Calibration floors create an optimization complexity boundary

Decision problem FLOOR-ENERGY-CUT. Given a finite graph and a target edge,
positive rational q_e, nonnegative rational delta_e on available sensor edges,
accuracy tau>0 and budget L>=0, does a sensor/energy allocation attain global
worst-case target error <=tau at total acquisition energy <=L under the declared
contract? All inputs are binary encoded. There are no setup costs.

Theorem 2. FLOOR-ENERGY-CUT is NP-complete. NP-hardness already holds when all
sensors are available, all calibration floors are positive, all cuts are
feasible at finite energy, and the graph is a direct target edge plus n parallel
two-edge paths with distinct internal vertices. This is a simple graph.

Proof of membership. A cut is a polynomial-size witness. Compute rational A_C,
D_C and check tau>D_C and A_C^3 <= L(tau-D_C)^2. Its optimal allocation is rational
and has polynomial encoding length. An empty cut is handled separately.

Proof of hardness. Reduce PARTITION on positive integers v_1,...,v_n with total
V. Set b=V. For each path s--i--t assign its first edge parameters

       (q,delta)=(b,b+v_i),

and its second edge (q,delta)=(b+v_i,b). A separating cut chooses exactly one
edge on each path. If S indexes its second-edge choices, then

       A = nb + sum_{i in S} v_i,
       D = nb + V - sum_{i in S} v_i,
       A+D=K=2nb+V.

Define A_*=nb+V/2, tau=K-A_*/3 and L=9A_*/4. All numbers have polynomial bit
length. Every cut has tau-D=A-A_*/3>0, because A>=nV>A_*/3 for n>=1. Its energy is

       f(A)=A^3/(A-A_*/3)^2.

For x>A_*/3, differentiating gives sign(f'(x))=sign(x-A_*). Hence f has its
unique global minimum f(A_*)=9A_*/4=L. Equivalently, avoiding calculus,

       4 x^3 - 9 A_* (x-A_*/3)^2
       = (x-A_*)^2 (4x-A_*),

which is nonnegative in the domain, with equality iff x=A_*.

There is an energy-feasible cut with budget L iff A=A_*, iff the original
integers have an equal partition. QED.

This proof establishes NP-hardness by a PARTITION reduction. It does NOT establish
strong NP-hardness, nor exclude useful approximation, pseudopolynomial methods,
special graph classes, or practical mixed-integer optimization. It also does
not imply that experimental access itself is uncomputable. The exact general
algorithm may enumerate cuts, and every claimed candidate design remains
checkable. The zero-floor and floor-aware results therefore distinguish an
algebraically exact certificate from an efficient search for its optimum.

Closely related background. Bicriteria budgeted terminal-cut optimization is
already NP-hard. Aissi and Mahjoub, 'On the minimum s-t cut problem with budget
constraints', primary manuscript https://www.lamsade.dauphine.fr/~mahjoub/Papers/
Paper-Aissi-Mahjoub-2023.pdf, discusses the known complexity and references the
earlier literature. The present reduction is included because the nonlinear
calibrated energy objective is the one actually used here. It must not be sold
as discovering the general hardness of multiobjective cuts. Global-cut and
specified-terminal-cut problems have different complexity variants; do not
substitute claims between them.

Floor threshold. Let rho be the minimum cut sum_C delta_e, with unavailable
sensors given infinite capacity. For a non-bridge
target and strictly positive a_e, finite-energy target accuracy tau is possible
iff tau>rho. At tau=rho finite energy cannot attain the floor, although the
infimum error can approach rho as energy diverges. A known bridge target is
the zero-energy exception. A q-minimum cut need not be the energy-minimum cut
when delta is nonzero.

6. Checkable global bounds when exact floor-aware design is hard

For any chosen rational t>0, define a supporting cut capacity

    c_e(t)=3 t^2 q_e + 2 t^3 delta_e.

Let W(t) be the minimum u-v cut sum of these capacities. Then

    E^* >= max{0, W(t)-2 tau t^3}.

Proof. For each feasible cut write s=tau-D>0 and x=A/s. Its energy obeys

    A^3/s^2 - [3 t^2 A - 2 t^3 s]
    = s (x-t)^2 (x+2t) >=0.

Thus the displayed expression is a valid lower bound for each feasible cut.
Taking the minimum over ALL cuts on its right-hand side remains a valid lower
bound, even when the minimum cut itself has D>=tau. QED.

The mincut carries an independent flow certificate. If its cut also satisfies
D<tau, the fixed-cut allocation supplies a feasible upper bound. Equality is
certified whenever the two values match; in particular it holds if t=A/(tau-D)
for that minimum cut. Otherwise the gap is reported. Repeating over any supplied
finite set of t values improves the maximum lower bound and minimum available
upper bound. This is a supporting-hyperplane/Lagrangian bound, an established
optimization method, not a claimed general exact solver for an NP-complete
problem. Its usefulness here is an inexpensive, independently checkable global
performance bracket tied to the physical error contract.

The PARTITION construction also supplies a strict-gap negative control. At
t=3/2 the q and delta weights coincide, and the lower bound is exactly L for
every instance. If the integer instance has no equal partition, every real cut
has energy strictly above L. Thus this lower-bound family need not eliminate
the combinatorial obstruction. Example [1,2,4] is tested explicitly.

7. Matched-accuracy example and negative controls

Graph: target s--t; alternative path starts with one edge s--a, then splits into
two parallel physical channels a--t. All currents are conserved, target cannot
be directly read. Let q(single)=4, q(pair1)=1, q(pair2)=2 and tau=1/10.

With zero floors:
  Fewest-sensor choice: single edge, energy 4^3/(1/10)^2 = 6400.
  Joint optimal design: pair, energy 3^3/(1/10)^2 = 2700.
  Optimal pair energies: 900,1800; errors 1/30,1/15.
  Equal pair precision 1/20 each: energy 400+3200 = 3600.

Now retain the same q but give floors delta(single)=0, delta(pair1)=3/50,
delta(pair2)=1/50. The pair requires energy 3^3/(1/50)^2=67500. The global optimum
switches to the single edge at 6400. This is a constructed, exactly solvable
comparison at the same robust target accuracy, not a measured detector speedup.
At support parameter t=40, a single supporting mincut certifies matching global
lower and upper energies of 6400 despite the nonzero floors. This is saved as
an independently checkable certificate.

It shows why minimizing measurement count, selecting sensors without allocating
precision, and ignoring calibration floors solve different design problems.
