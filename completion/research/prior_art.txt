ACCESS COMPLETION: PRIMARY-LITERATURE AUDIT
Abdinoor / 2D Labs / 12 October 2026

Purpose and status
------------------
This audit tests attribution and positioning, not theorem correctness. It extends
the earlier unified/research/bibliography_sources.txt and positioning.txt audits.
The search was performed during this research phase. A failure to locate an
identical statement is not evidence of historical priority. No outside peer
review, laboratory validation, or bibliometric impact assessment occurred.
The accompanying sources.json records precise reading limits. Search snippets
and metadata were not treated as substitutes for unavailable theorem statements.

Decision for the current contribution
-------------------------------------
The basic row-space criterion, equivalence algorithms, flow-conservation sensor
placement, cotree construction, robust linear recovery, optimal experimental
design, lumpability, and the need for compositional equivalence are established.
They are useful foundations and implementation components, not new physical laws.

The strongest candidate developed in this phase is more specific: finite-time,
finite-amplitude recovery of the sign of a stationary physical-channel current
from the endpoint response to selective channel suppression; explicit local
calibration bounds; and a quantum model whose response crosses the forbidden
classical sign boundary. The all-time sign statement must remain restricted to
the stated preparation, intervention, and singleton detector contract. A hidden
classical network can have arbitrarily many states within that contract. This
does not exclude arbitrary classical descriptions with a different intervention,
coarse detector, time-dependent rates, or nonstationary preparation.

Suggested positioning:
"We combine established flow observability, optimal recovery, and Markov response
theory into a constructive procedure for completing experimental access. A
finite-pulse endpoint-response theorem gives a network-independent current-sign
test under selective channel control. Its violation in a driven-qubit model
provides a conditional classical-network exclusion witness."

This is a substantive testable mathematical target. Do not call it the first
such theorem, a universal law of physics, a device-independent quantum witness,
or a solved measurement technology without further evidence.

1. FLOW OBSERVABILITY AND GRAPH SENSOR PLACEMENT

Ali--Narasimhan (1993, 1995), Bagajewicz--Sanchez (2000), He (2013), and
Xu--Lo--Chen--Castillo (2016) establish a substantial earlier literature on
conservation-law sensor design, complete/target observability, redundancy, cost,
and uncertain measurements. In the full Bagajewicz--Sanchez article, section 3
explicitly describes observability using instruments on the chords of a spanning
tree. This blocks a novelty claim for the minimum measured cotree construction.
Its sections 2 and 6 already discuss target-variable measurement design and
precision constraints. Our exact interval-radius objective still needs a precise
comparison; general cost-optimal robust sensor placement itself is established.

Prakash (2026), DOI 10.1016/j.compchemeng.2026.109863, is exceptionally close by
title and publisher-indexed description: an exact algebraic graph framework for
cost-optimal sensor placement in flow processes. Author publication metadata and
the publisher abstract were inspected; the full paper was inaccessible. Its
unread theorems are an unresolved priority risk, not a basis for asserting a
distinction. Retrieve and compare it before any priority-sensitive submission.

The circulation target condition (a separating measured cut in the graph with
the target removed), the max-flow dual of a deterministic error radius, and the
maximum-weight spanning-forest solution should be presented as explicit
specializations of classical graph linear algebra/optimization. The mixed
parallel-channel/aggregate-sensor classification is a useful scoped extension
to document with proof and an executable certificate. This audit did not locate
an identical full theorem, but its kernel/quotient decomposition is elementary
relative to the established foundations; no priority claim is justified.

2. PERTURBATIONS AS ACCESS COMPLETION

Gross--Bluthgen (2020), DOI 10.1093/bioinformatics/btaa404, is a direct neighboring
framework for perturbation identifiability and experimental design. Its steady
linear-response model is R approximately -J^{-1}SP; known network topology and
generic noncancellation assumptions yield graph path/rank and maximum-flow
criteria. The accompanying IdentiFlow software designs informative experiments.
Our exact stationary circulation model and calibrated finite pulse are different
inverse problems. The general claim that graph analysis can find informative
perturbations is already established.

Martinez--Bisker--Horowitz--Parrondo (2019), DOI
10.1038/s41467-019-11051-w, includes an informed observer controlling an observed
transition while leaving hidden rates fixed. Use it as a selective-access
precedent. Do not transfer all entropy-production claims without examining the
linked later Matters Arising discussion. Earlier unified references already
cover high-order fluorescence correlations and coarse thermodynamic inference.

3. EXACT RESPONSE AND FIRST PASSAGE: CLOSEST PHYSICAL PRIOR ART

Aslyamov--Esposito, PRL 132, 037101 (2024), derive exact stationary response
relations and bounds for Markov jump processes. Their PRL 133, 107103 (2024)
develops a broader static-response theory. Barrier response proportional to an
edge current is therefore not an original inference principle here. The
derivative of an occupancy following a selective perturbation is also a direct
generator identity and should not be advertised as a new law.

Bao--Liang, arXiv:2412.19602v6 (2026 revision), is especially important. Equations
(2b), (3)--(5) give finite-amplitude stationary barrier response in terms of
mean-first-passage differences and current-rescaling factors. Supplement VIII,
(S82)--(S84), explicitly covers parallel physical transition channels. The
conclusion identifies transient response as further work. Accordingly,
finite-amplitude stationary channel sensitivity, including parallel channels,
is already covered; our candidate distinction is an all-finite-time endpoint
sign guarantee plus finite-precision access calibration.

Zheng--Lu, Phys. Rev. E 114, 014126 (2026), DOI 10.1103/tvqj-7ddn,
arXiv:2604.06162v2, develops response relations out of stationarity.
Section V contains time-dependent response kernels and Laplace-domain mutual
linearity. The kernels involve differences of transition probabilities from the
two endpoints. The inspected statements do not supply the particular fixed-time,
finite-amplitude positive endpoint occupation kernel of this phase. Do not claim
that transient Markov response is absent from prior work.

Floyd--Dinner--Vaikuntanathan, arXiv:2507.07295v2 (2026 revision), proves fixed-sign
steady-state responses across broad perturbation amplitudes using graph-tree
structure. Section III and Appendix B include edge barriers. The independent
variable in this monotonicity result is the perturbation strength, evaluated
after equilibration to the new stationary state. Our candidate holds a
perturbation fixed and constrains the sign during the entire transient.

Liang--Bao, arXiv:2608.06368, and Bao--Liang, arXiv:2608.11202, develop bounds and exact
responses for first-passage times. Their observables are completion times, not
endpoint occupancy at a prescribed finite time. These are relevant methods and
priority neighbors, not evidence of the endpoint theorem's novelty.

The occupation-kernel proof uses the ordinary strong Markov property at the
first hit of the measured endpoint. Deviation-matrix and expected-reward theory
are established (Coolen-Schrijner--van Doorn 2002; Dendievel et al. 2017). We did
not complete a historical search of every finite-time potential-kernel identity.
An exact sign theorem can be a useful new application even if its short
first-passage lemma is standard. Attribute the lemma's method accordingly.

One newly indexed paper, Bao--Liang, "Exact Finite Response of Higher-Order
Statistics in Nonequilibrium Networks," DOI 10.1088/0256-307X/43/10/100002,
describes higher-cumulant response and variance sign reversals. Only its primary
abstract was available. It is not evidence of transient endpoint sign reversal;
the full text remains a live overlap check.

4. QUANTUM VIOLATION: WHAT IS AND IS NOT DISTINCT

Smirne et al., Quantum Sci. Technol. 4, 01LT01 (2019), DOI
10.1088/2058-9565/aaebd5, connects generation and subsequent detection of coherence
with nonclassical Markovian multi-time statistics. Coherence in a model alone
does not exclude an alternative classical description. The present proposed
test uses a different observation/intervention contract and a different
inequality: no change of endpoint-response sign following the specified pulse.

Prech--Potts--Landi, PRL 134, 020401 (2025), DOI
10.1103/PhysRevLett.134.020401, analyzes violations of classical kinetic
uncertainty bounds caused by quantum coherence. It includes a coherently driven
qubit and effective classical rate descriptions. Their comparison concerns
current fluctuations/activity; ours concerns a signed transient response to a
specified change of a dissipative channel. The shared physical theme of using
dynamical data to test a classical rate description requires attribution.

The all-time classical theorem excludes a structural model class rather than
"classicality" without qualifications. Stationary preparation, a single
bidirectional physical channel multiplied by one constant factor, fixed other
rates, and singleton endpoint readout are substantive assumptions. They cannot
be certified from the two endpoint averages alone. Oscillation by itself is
not the witness: classical networks and classical oscillators can oscillate.
What matters is response sign crossing under the common calibrated contract.

The exact qubit threshold reported by the composition agent is an internal
derivation, not a literature fact or external validation. No identical
threshold/witness was found in the inspected sources. Historical priority is
still unestablished. Compare directly with stochastic-model tests and response
witness literature before using "new" in an abstract.

5. ROBUST RECOVERY, ENERGY ALLOCATION, AND COMPUTATIONAL COMPLEXITY

Elfving (1952), DOI 10.1214/aoms/1177729442, already studies optimal allocation to
estimate a specified linear functional, including different measurement costs.
Harman--Jurik (2008), DOI 10.1016/j.csda.2008.06.023, gives a linear-programming
method for finite c-optimal design. Donoho (1994) is a foundational optimal-recovery
reference already in the unified release. These block a broad novelty claim for
target-specific allocation. Adversarial interval widths and stochastic variance
are different loss models and must not be silently interchanged.

For the explicitly assumed error-energy law epsilon_e = delta_e + sqrt(a_e/E_e),
the phase's fixed-cut formula and its calibration-floor complexity contrast are
specific mathematical claims worth proving. The error-energy law itself is a
calibration assumption, not a consequence of thermodynamics or a universal
energy-information bound. Joules require an experimentally calibrated meaning.

Aissi--Mahjoub (2024), DOI 10.1007/s10107-023-01987-9, studies terminal cuts with
budget constraints and discusses strong NP-hardness already for two criteria.
Kratsch et al., DOI 10.1007/s00453-019-00609-1, studies multi-budgeted directed
cuts. Thus multiobjective cut hardness is established. A direct PARTITION
reduction for this phase's nonlinear calibration-floor objective is a scoped
specialization, not discovery of the general phenomenon. Global budgeted cuts
and terminal s--t budgeted cuts have different complexity; do not conflate them.

6. COMPOSITION AND CONTEXT-STABLE INTERFACES

Buchholz (1994), Paige--Tarjan (1987), and Bian--Abate (2017) are established
sources for lumpability, partition refinement, and approximate probabilistic
bisimulation. The deterministic Markov interface compiler and adaptive trace
error bound should be presented as a supporting integration with these ideas.

Lalire, arXiv:quant-ph/0603274 (2006), explicitly distinguishes process
bisimilarity from congruence under parallel composition. Deng--Feng (2012),
DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-33475-7_9, develops quantum open bisimulation and its
congruence properties. Hence isolated equivalence failing under coupling and
the search for context-stable equivalence are not new foundational concepts.
The classical interface result must not be extended to arbitrary entangled
quantum contexts without a separate theorem.

7. REQUIRED LIMITS ON APPLICATION CLAIMS

No audited source makes the new framework a universal solution to AI energy,
fault-tolerant quantum computing, in vivo metabolic flux, or biological sensing.
Identifying a targeted channel current does not identify all microscopic
thermodynamic forces, eliminate dissipation, or remove tomography's worst-case
dimension. Selective suppression may be difficult experimentally, and a
biochemical concentration often groups many microscopic states. The singleton
classical sign theorem cannot be applied automatically to that coarse observable.

A mature release should distinguish: established mathematical foundations;
newly derived scoped propositions with reproducible checks; calibrated-model
application demonstrations; and untested experimental proposals. The promising
bridge here is a reusable intervention test with explicit failure contracts,
not a claim to have created an unavoidable universal language of science.
