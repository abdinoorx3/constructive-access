1. FIXED-CUT ALLOCATION

Inputs are q_e>0, delta_e>=0, tau>0 and the declared relation
epsilon_e(E_e)=delta_e+sqrt(q_e^3/E_e), with E_e>0 for acquired sensors. Let
Q=sum q_e, D=sum delta_e, and s=tau-D. For a nonempty cut, s<=0 excludes a
finite-energy solution. For s>0, the optimal allocation is

  E_e=Q^2 q_e/s^2,
  epsilon_e=delta_e+s q_e/Q,
  E_total=Q^3/s^2.

I independently derived this both by a Lagrange multiplier for positive slack
errors and by Holder's inequality. Strict positivity of q ensures no boundary
case with a zero-cost exact sensor has been silently included. The empty cut is
handled separately with zero energy, as required when the target is a bridge.
The code's check of (epsilon-delta)^2 E=q^3, positive slack, total error tau,
and equality of summed energy with the closed-form lower bound is sufficient.
It does not trust the producing optimizer.

2. GLOBAL IDEAL DESIGN

The exact minimax edge-current radius is the minimum cut sum of the declared
sensor errors. Therefore every feasible energy allocation admits at least one
cut with summed error <=tau. Restricting to that cut cannot increase energy.
Without floors its best energy is (sum_cut q)^3/tau^2, monotonically increasing
in the q-cut value. A minimum q-cut and its allocation are globally optimal
among sensor subsets and estimators within this contract. The matching flow
certificate supplies the lower bound. verify_ideal_energy correctly treats
verify_target_cut as a Boolean and rejects a counterfeit target flow.

3. CALIBRATION-FLOOR HARDNESS

For positive PARTITION integers v_i, put V=sum v_i, b=V, Q_*=nV+V/2. A cut
chooses one edge on each of n two-edge paths. Its sums are

  Q=nV+sum_selected v_i,
  D=nV+V-sum_selected v_i=2Q_*-Q.

At accuracy tau=5Q_*/3 and budget L=9Q_*/4 every cut has positive slack, since
Q>=nV>Q_*/3. Its optimal energy is Q^3/(Q-Q_*/3)^2. I independently checked
the exact identity

  Q^3/(Q-Q_*/3)^2 - 9Q_*/4
    = (Q-Q_*)^2(4Q-Q_*) / [4(Q-Q_*/3)^2].

The right side is nonnegative and vanishes only at Q=Q_*. Thus a cut meets the
energy budget if and only if the original integers admit an equal partition.
The graph is simple: a direct target s-t edge and distinct interior vertices
on the other length-two paths. No parallel physical edges are required here.

All q, delta, tau and L are rationals with polynomial bit length. Choosing
a=q^3 also preserves polynomial bit length. Membership in NP follows from a
cut witness and the rational check Q^3<=L(tau-D)^2, with tau>D. The optimal
allocation can also be written with polynomially many rational bits. Therefore
the stated decision problem is NP-complete for rational q. The reduction is
from PARTITION and does not establish strong NP-hardness. It makes no claim
against approximation, pseudopolynomial algorithms, tractable subclasses, or
practical optimization. Exact algebraic verification remains easy even when
finding an optimum is hard.

5. SCIENTIFIC SCOPE THAT MUST SURVIVE INTEGRATION

The correct interpretation is minimum certified acquisition energy under the
declared adversarial box and precision-energy model. If epsilon(E) is merely a
conservative confidence envelope, the result establishes the optimum for that
guarantee, not the minimum physical energy achievable by all conceivable
experiments. The inverse-square sensor model is not a universal physical law.
In particular, the separate finite-time channel-pulse experiment may incur
systematic transient bias and a different precision-energy relation; it must
not be inserted into this ideal contract without derivation and calibration.

A valid joint error envelope is required. Marginal confidence bounds alone do
not establish the same simultaneous confidence across several sensors. Full
box error uncertainty is a worst-case admissibility assumption, not a claim of
probabilistic independence. Known error correlations can improve the optimum.

Only acquisition energy is optimized. Setup, preparation, intervention, sensing,
reset, thermal support and calibration costs remain additional unless included
explicitly in a revised resource model. Continuous real-valued allocations also
exclude record-count integrality and power or duration restrictions.
The chosen sensors and allocations are fixed before observations. Sequential
adaptive acquisition needs an additional protocol and conditional-error contract;
it is not included in this fixed-design optimum.

The unrestricted conserved-current model is what makes the minimax bound exact.
A fixed finite device with rate, traffic or thermodynamic-affinity constraints
can have a smaller admissible class and a different sharp bound. Positive-rate
realizability establishes that a finite least-favorable pair is physically
possible in the declared Markov class, not that every device realizes that pair.

7. ADDITIONAL REVIEW: SCALABLE FLOOR-AWARE BOUND CERTIFICATES

The final producer version adds floor_energy_bound and verify_floor_energy_bound.
For any positive rational supporting parameter t, compute a minimum cut with
capacities 3 t^2 q_e + 2 t^3 delta_e. Subtract 2 tau t^3 from the cut value and
take the maximum with zero. This is a valid global energy lower bound. If the
returned cut has D<tau, its exact allocation supplies a feasible upper bound.

I independently proved the lower bound as follows. For any feasible cut, put
s=tau-D>0 and x=A/s. Its energy minus the supporting expression is

  A^3/s^2 - (3 t^2 A - 2 t^3 s)
    = s (x-t)^2 (x+2t) >= 0.

Minimizing the supporting expression over all cuts therefore lower-bounds the
minimum feasible energy. Including infeasible cuts in that supporting minimum
cannot invalidate the lower bound. Clipping a negative value at zero is valid.
For an empty cut, the optimum and the clipped lower bound both equal zero.
If a returned feasible cut also satisfies t=A/(tau-D), equality certifies its
global optimality. Other choices may leave a strict gap. Sampling several t
values yields valid bounds, without an assertion that this solves the general
NP-complete optimization or finds the best possible relaxation parameter.

The checker independently validates the graph flow/cut certificate, supporting
value, chosen cut, allocation and gap. No optimizer is called by the checker.
I tested 100 additional random multigraphs using random.Random(884701), with
unavailable sensors, rational q and floors, target bridges and disconnected
cases. Independent exhaustive vertex-cut enumeration found 62 finite optima
and 38 instances without a finite feasible design. Across five rational t
values per graph (1/3, 1, 3/2, 7, 21), all 500 producer certificates correctly
bounded the independently computed optimum. Every reported exact optimum was
correct. No infeasible instance was supplied a finite feasible upper bound.

This useful search-versus-verification distinction strengthens the engineering
workflow. It remains an application of standard convex supporting bounds and
minimum-cut duality; historical originality needs its own literature assessment.
