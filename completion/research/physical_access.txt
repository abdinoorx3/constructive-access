STRONGEST SURVIVING RESULT

Row-generator convention. Let Q and Q' be valid finite CTMC generators, pi a
stationary probability row for Q, and i != j. Suppose their only changes are the
two directed rates i->j and j->i and compensating diagonals. Set

 gamma = pi_i (Q'_ij-Q_ij) - pi_j (Q'_ji-Q_ji).

Then at every finite t>0 under the same constant Q' through readout,

 [pi exp(tQ')]_j-pi_j = gamma H_ij(t),
 H_ij(t) = integral_0^t [P'_jj(s)-P'_ij(s)] ds
         = integral_0^t P'_jj(s) Pr_i^{Q'}(tau_j>t-s) ds > 0.

This statement requires no irreducibility or positive stationary mass at every
state. The finite generator and distinct endpoints imply finite exit rates and
positive no-jump probabilities. Exact zero at any positive finite time is
equivalent to gamma=0; otherwise the baseline displacement has one fixed sign.
It need not be monotone, nor stay on one side of its eventual stationary value.

PROOF

Stationarity and the two-endpoint support give pi Q'=gamma(e_j-e_i).
Integrating d[pi exp(sQ')]/ds proves the first identity. Start the perturbed
process at i and stop at the first hitting time tau_j of j. There is no j
occupation before tau_j. The strong Markov property gives

 integral_0^t P'_ij(s) ds
 = E_i[1_{tau_j<=t} integral_0^{t-tau_j} P'_jj(s) ds].

Tonelli's theorem subtracts this nonnegative expression from the integrated
return probability and gives the survival convolution above. No assumption
P'_jj(s)>=P'_ij(s) is used; that pointwise statement is false on a directed
three-cycle. Positivity follows from

 P'_jj(s)>=exp(-lambda_j s),
 Pr_i(tau_j>u)>=exp(-lambda_i u),

where lambda_k=-Q'_kk. Endpoint upper bounds Lambda_i,Lambda_j thus give

 integral_0^t exp[-Lambda_j s-Lambda_i(t-s)] ds <= H_ij(t) <= t.

For a common cap Lambda, t exp(-Lambda t)<=H<=t. These are local endpoint
bounds; large exit rates elsewhere do not enter the proof.

SELECTIVE PHYSICAL CHANNEL

For a separately specified bidirectional physical channel e with rates a_e,b_e,
native J_e=pi_i a_e-pi_j b_e, multiply that channel's two rates by r in (0,1),
without changing any other rates. Then gamma=-(1-r)J_e and the paired endpoint
difference D=E[1{X0=j}-1{Xt=j}]=(1-r)J_e H. This is a sign-identifying
experiment on any finite network obeying the access contract.

Three channel decompositions (3,1,1,3), (2,2,2,2), (1,3,3,1) give the same
two-state aggregate generator with rates4,4, but first-channel currents1,0,-1.
Passive full state trajectories cannot distinguish them. Selective suppression
changes their aggregate generators differently and activates the current sign.
A common additive state-level control preserves the equal generators instead.

A calibrated Arrhenius barrier shift can realize common forward/reverse
scaling, preserving that channel's local rate ratio. Enzyme perturbations,
binding reporters, or shared fuel changes do not automatically satisfy this
assumption. The intervention can change native current and stationary occupancy;
the theorem infers the original current from a newly induced response.

SENSOR ACCESS AND COMPOSITION

Full-state paths already identify simple-edge currents. Selective perturbation
is needed for physical parallel-channel decomposition, or can supply a weaker
endpoint-only sensor when full paths are unavailable. These are distinct access
contracts. The graph completion layer must not silently assume full-state paths
while claiming simple-edge currents were unobservable.

For a coarse detector B containing j and excluding i, positivity survives if
every directed path from i into B first enters at j. Removing j and testing
reachability into B is an executable sufficient certificate. Before tau_j there
is no B-occupation, hence the same proof replaces P'_jj(s) by P'_j(B,s), which
is at least P'_jj(s). This is a genuine coarse-readout extension with an explicit
physical interface requirement.

More generally, if pi Q'=gamma(nu_B-nu_A), nu_A is outside B, nu_B inside B,
and the first entrance into B from nu_A resets to nu_B independently of entrance
time, the same first-hitting convolution is positive. The entrance-time/state
factorization is a substantive assumption, not a consequence of coarse graining.

ESSENTIAL FAILURE CONTROLS

1. Arbitrary coarse readout can cross classically. Four channels
   (0,1,2,1),(0,2,10,1),(1,3,10,1),(2,3,1,3) have native pi=(7,4,80,30)/121.
   Suppression of channel0 by1/2 gives perturbed pi=(13,6,140,50)/209.
   For B={1,2}, initial response derivative=-5/121 and eventual displacement
   =10/2299>0. Continuity proves a finite-time crossing. A destination singleton
   still has the required sign. Red-team independently certified responses at
   t=.1 and t=.5 by rational Taylor bounds.

2. Native stationarity is essential. In the two-state (3,1,1,3) model with true
   J=1 and suppression1/2, preparation(.9,.1) instead of(.5,.5) lets destination
   population rise. Its response sign is opposite the native-stationary law.

3. The controlled generator must stay fixed until readout. The red-team found a
   three-cycle cw4,ccw1/10, suppression1/2 on0<->1 for duration1/10, whose
   immediate destination displacement is about-.04823. Restoring the native
   generator and waiting2/5 produces about+.002293. Readout latency and release
   therefore require explicit treatment.

4. Feedback, state-dependent stopping, simultaneous changes on other channels,
   or invalid preparation can violate the stated contract. A sign crossing alone
   does not certify quantum coherence or reject all classical hidden-state models.

5. A fixed pulse with only a lower/upper kernel bound identifies sign more sharply
   than magnitude. Infinite records do not collapse multiplicative kernel
   uncertainty. Kernel calibration or a shrinking-pulse scheme is needed for
   arbitrary-precision magnitude inference.

CALIBRATION AND FINITE DATA

For independent classical native-stationary preparations and noninvasive
initial/final singleton readout, Z=1{X0=j}-1{Xt=j} lies in[-1,1]. Hoeffding gives
P(|Zbar-EZ|>=s)<=2exp(-Ns^2/2). The code chooses a sufficient N and checks its
exponential with rational Taylor bounds. This is not a minimum-record claim and
does not transfer automatically to an initially disturbed quantum state.

With factor interval[rlo,rhi] inside(0,1), endpoint capLambda, and response
interval[dlo,dhi], preparation TVeta contributes at most2eta expectation bias.
Add detector and fully propagated model-response error to obtain biasb. Then

 Dideal in ([dlo-b,dhi+b] intersect[-1,1]),
 (1-r)H in[(1-rhi)t exp(-Lambda*t),(1-rlo)t].

Taking four ratio endpoints gives a rigorous current interval. The code uses
an exact rational exponential enclosure. An interval containing0 yields the
status unresolved. This is conservative, not a sharp minimax claim over networks.
Joint calibration failure adds once to the sample allowance by union bound.

An improved magnitude plan exploits paired-record variance. Write a=1-r,
|J|<=Jmax, pi_j<=pmax. Exact stationarity implies

 EZ^2=2pi_j(1-P'_jj(t))-D
     <=[2pmax Lambda+a Jmax]t.

Also |Z-EZ|<=2. Bernstein gives failure<=2exp[-N s^2/(2(V+2s/3))],
V=min(1,[2pmax Lambda+aJmax]t). The bias of Zbar/(at) is at mostJmax Lambda t.
For desired current error eps, take t=eps/(2Jmax Lambda), s=at eps/2 and
N>=2log(2/alpha)(V+2s/3)/s^2. This is a sufficient O(eps^-3) record count.
The earlier range-only Hoeffding O(eps^-4) bound is valid but weaker. Neither is
a minimax lower bound. Fixed response calibration error is amplified1/t and
can impose an error floor; the exact-preparation theorem must not hide it.
If no tighter current bound is calibrated, Jmax=Lambda/r follows from the
perturbed endpoint exit caps: r*a_e,r*b_e<=Lambda and |J_e|<=max(a_e,b_e).
This is conservative but uses no global generator reconstruction.

ENERGY

If separately calibrated per-record energy is bounded by E0+Pmax*t, then the
preceding plan gives total boundN(E0+Pmax*t), in addition to any shared calibration
cost not included inE0. As eps->0 this sufficient preparation term scaleseps^-3
and active-pulse term scaleseps^-2. This is not a universal energy-information
law. E0 must include resets, preparation, readout, recording, and control costs
outside the acquisition interval. Arrhenius DeltaB=-kBTlogr is a barrier
parameter, not total work consumed per measurement.

STRONGER LOCAL RATE-COUNT INTERFACE

For users with local transition counters, suppressing a channel by knownr gives

 J_e=[pi_i(q0_ij-qr_ij)-pi_j(q0_ji-qr_ji)]/(1-r).

The exact interval helper evaluates all128 vertices of a rectangular box in
pi_i,pi_j,q0_ij,q0_ji,qr_ij,qr_ji,r. Each coordinate dependence is affine or
linear-fractional with positive denominator, so extrema occur at vertices.
The box may contain physically inconsistent points; the returned enclosure is
sharp algebraically for that box and can be conservative physically.

Finite transition-rate records can be justified using inverse local time:
tau_i(T)=inf{t:integral_0^t1{Xs=i}ds>=T}. In a fixed finite irreducible CTMC,
source-time marked departures are independent Poisson processes and
N_ij(tau_i(T))~Pois(q_ij*T). This is not a conditional-on-occupation assertion
at a fixed wall time. For q_ij<=Lambda, Bernstein gives

 P(|N_ij/T-q_ij|>=eps)
 <=2exp[-T eps^2/(2(Lambda+eps/3))].

The code chooses sufficient rationalT and certifies the exponential bound.
Starting at i, E tau_i(T)=T/pi_i by the renewal/Kac identity. The actual elapsed
time is unbounded; in a perturbed experiment use the perturbed pi_i. A fixed
deadline can report incomplete exposure and abstain. Coverage remains an
unconditional probability of a wrong reported interval; no conditional-on-
completion coverage is claimed.
