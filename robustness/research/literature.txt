This extends access_completion/research/prior_art.txt. It is a primary-source
comparison, not evidence of historical priority or physical validation. The
phase investigates occupation-response tests and a simpler two-preparation,
random-readout experiment. No apparatus was operated and no new empirical data
were obtained. Full third-party papers are not redistributed here.

The strongest positioning supported by this audit is an explicit operational
test of a declared time-homogeneous classical Markov model class, accompanied by
calibration bounds, failure examples, and reproducible comparison with existing
tests. Neither testing a classical Markov explanation from populations nor
avoiding full tomography is new in itself. The integrated endpoint inequality
is a direct corollary of established first-passage theory. The precise
combination of finite-window random sampling, calibrated acceptance regions,
and access assumptions may be useful; its priority remains unresolved.

1. ATTRIBUTION OF THE POSITIVE OCCUPATION KERNEL

Coolen-Schrijner and van Doorn, "The deviation matrix of a continuous-time Markov
chain," Probability in the Engineering and Informational Sciences 16, 351–366
(2002), DOI 10.1017/S0269964802163066, supply the relevant first-entrance identity
as equation (3.11) of their author preprint. Their paper primarily studies
infinite-horizon centered occupation matrices. The first-entrance decomposition
is described there as well known. Our finite-window sign follows immediately by
integrating that identity and using nonnegative occupation probabilities.

For clarity, this phase's elementary derivation is as follows. Let
U_xj(T)=E_x integral_0^T 1_{X_s=j} ds and tau_j be the first hit of j. The strong
Markov property gives U_ij(T)=E_i U_jj((T-tau_j)_+). Since U_jj is increasing,
U_jj(T)-U_ij(T)>=0. For a finite CTMC, finite rates and i!=j make the inequality
strict at every T>0. Therefore a single observation at an independent time
uniform on [0,T] compares the two preparations through this occupation gap
divided by T. These are deductions in the present work, with established
probabilistic ingredients. Do not call the first-hit identity or its positivity
corollary a newly discovered physical law.

The preceding phase already compared exact finite-amplitude stationary
response with Aslyamov–Esposito and Bao–Liang, and transient response with
Zheng–Lu. Zheng–Lu arXiv:2604.06162v2 was revisited: section III uses transition
probability differences in trajectory response kernels; section V addresses
Laplace-domain nonstationary mutual linearity. Those topics are established
neighbors, although the inspected text does not state this phase's random-time
test. A failure to find an identical formulation does not prove novelty.

Other occupation references searched included Colwell, Statistics & Probability
Letters 195, 109786 (2023), DOI 10.1016/j.spl.2023.109786. Only its primary abstract
was inspected; no theorem-level priority conclusion is drawn from it.

2. CLOSEST POPULATION-BASED CLASSICAL-MODEL TESTS

Lambert, Emary, Chen and Nori, Physical Review Letters 105, 176801 (2010), DOI
10.1103/PhysRevLett.105.176801, is the essential comparator. For a protected
singleton return probability it obtains |2 P_jj(t)-P_jj(2t)|<=1 from the
Chapman–Kolmogorov property. The proof does not require a known finite state
count. Thus broad claims of the first size-independent population test against
classical Markov networks are blocked. Their physical example concerns coherent
transport. This phase must compare a randomized-time occupation test with that
two-time return test at matched records, acquisition time, and calibration.

Li, Lambert, Chen, Chen and Nori, Scientific Reports 2, 885 (2012), DOI
10.1038/srep00885, develops population/propagator witnesses and explicitly
discusses the trade between preparation access, noninvasive observation,
state-space knowledge, and classical memory. Its witness 1 compares a later
population with a classical mixture of propagated preparations; witness 2 tests
consistency of propagators inferred at different times. These are not the same
finite-window inequality. They establish that limited-access coherence testing
without tomography is a mature subject, with preparation and memory loopholes.

Zhou, Huelga, Li and Guo, Physical Review Letters 115, 113002 (2015), DOI
10.1103/PhysRevLett.115.113002, demonstrates a related Leggett–Garg-type test in a
light–matter system and separately benchmarks time-translation invariance and
memory assumptions. Huelga, Marshall and Santos, Physical Review A 52, R2497
(1995), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevA.52.R2497, is earlier foundational context; only
primary metadata and its identification in the later primary references were
inspected here. No unread theorem is attributed to it.

Distinction to preserve: the standard Leggett–Garg inequality, a
time-homogeneous Markov-model inequality, and this work's occupation inequality
have different premises. A final measurement on each fresh preparation avoids
an intermediate-measurement disturbance in that protocol. It does not establish
noncontextuality, eliminate hidden classical memory, or certify that a detector
corresponds to one classical microstate. In particular, classical coarse
detectors and preparation-dependent hidden distributions require separate
analysis. Rejecting the declared contract does not identify which premise
failed.

3. RANDOM TIME, REPEATED MEASUREMENT, AND OCCUPATION

A random terminal observation is not repeated projective monitoring. The latter
changes quantum dynamics and raises quantum-Zeno issues. Mueller et al.,
Scientific Reports 6, 38650 (2016), DOI 10.1038/srep38650, is a neighboring study
of stochastic Zeno detection of noise correlations. Its primary text describes
repeated projections with random intervals, rather than our single terminal
readout. It should not be used as an implementation proof for the current test.

Grunbaum, Velazquez and Wilkening, arXiv:2012.07786, discusses occupation
statistics in monitored discrete quantum walks. Its introduction and relevant
definitions were inspected. It does not establish historical novelty or
non-novelty of this phase's continuous-time two-preparation test.

If nonincreasing readout-time densities are represented as mixtures of uniforms,
attribute that representation to the classical Khintchine mixture theorem.
Pavlides–Wellner's primary paper on multivariate scale mixtures of uniforms,
available at PMC3318987, explicitly recalls its one-dimensional form. The
mixture representation is not a new operator construction. A full
characterization of which time weights preserve the inequality for every
generator would be a separate theorem and must not be inferred from sufficiency.

4. PHYSICAL FEASIBILITY: WHICH EXPERIMENT FIRST

The simpler experiment is operationally preferable as a first demonstration:
prepare either of two specified basis states, run the same fixed dynamics,
choose the terminal readout time independently from a preregistered law, and
measure the same endpoint. A resonantly driven qubit already supplies a
candidate quantum response. This avoids the need to selectively scale one
particular dissipative mechanism. It does not remove the preparation, hidden
state, time-homogeneity, and detector contracts. A literature demonstration of
Rabi control is evidence that the required operations exist, not calibration of
this proposed experiment or evidence of an advantage over existing witnesses.

The exact two-channel dissipative model of the previous release is more
demanding. Guo, Du and Ma, Physical Review Research 8, 033175 (2026), DOI
10.1103/mr1g-zqtq, arXiv:2605.12429, demonstrates programmable incoherent pump and
loss using transmon–resonator sidebands. Sections II and IV and Appendices A–C
were inspected. It is relevant hardware precedent, but not a demonstration of
our particular paired-channel suppression. Reservoir bandwidth, residual
thermal occupation, and common-mode interference require explicit modeling.
The reported assignment fidelity is 98.4%; this figure cannot be substituted
for a uniform error bound on every setting of our protocol. Their data and code
record was identified at https://zenodo.org/records/20190733 but its contents
were not downloaded or independently analyzed in this audit.

Our additional model-level assessment: four engineered rates can represent two
distinct bidirectional channels only if the apparatus supplies an independently
calibrated physical decomposition. Identical jump operators with separately
named coefficients do not by themselves establish distinct controllable
reservoirs. An upward-biased channel requires an engineered drive or inversion;
two ordinary positive-temperature thermal reservoirs on the same energy gap
cannot produce the example's opposite biases and exact unital balance. No heat,
entropy-production, or gate-energy interpretation follows from coefficient names.

A digital quantum simulation of a Lindblad model can test the simulator's
controlled dynamics. Time slicing, resets, compiler changes, and ancilla memory
must enter its error contract. Agreement of such a simulation with its input
model is not independent evidence that an unspecified natural system obeys the
same physical decomposition. Garcia-Perez et al., npj Quantum Information 6, 1
(2020), DOI 10.1038/s41534-019-0235-y, is a relevant experimental open-system
simulation precedent; only the primary abstract and indexed article excerpts
were inspected in this audit.

5. CALIBRATION AND ADOPTION GATES

Recommended experiment contract, to be instantiated rather than asserted:

* Preparation error measured separately for each preparation; a shared average
  gate fidelity is not a worst-case preparation bound.
* The same dynamics and detector across arms, with setting-dependent drift
  explicitly bounded. Interleaving/randomizing arms assists drift diagnosis but
  does not make drift mathematically impossible.
* Independent sampling of the terminal time. A finite timing grid is not uniform
  continuous sampling; use a derived discretization bound or prove the grid
  preserves the required kernel sign. Dead time and omitted short delays matter.
* No selection of favorable windows on the same uncorrected significance test.
* Endpoint leakage and hidden detector microstates explicitly bounded, or a
  regenerative/lumpable interface certified. Ordinary qubit process tomography
  alone cannot prove every possible classical ontological singleton assumption.
* Reset and shot correlations handled in the statistical contract. A nominal
  binomial count is inadequate if records share unmodeled drift or memory.
* Positive controls and classical counterexamples published with the test.
* Physical acquisition energy measured separately from the drive-generator
  coefficients. Total energy includes preparation, resetting, measurement,
  control electronics and refrigeration when the chosen boundary includes them.

The most useful next comparison is a measured, scoped advantage: a wider
calibration margin, a smaller number of independent records under the same
resource accounting, or detection of a family missed by an established witness.
No universal superiority or empirical advantage is established by this audit.

6. PRIORITY STATUS

Established ingredients: first-hit renewal decomposition, finite occupation
monotonicity derived from it, population-based Markov-model tests, randomized
sampling, concentration inequalities, driven-qubit control, and reservoir
engineering. Newly developed in this research program: the exact combination of
its response contracts, finite-window tests, calibrated certificates and
adversarial examples, subject to the mathematical review elsewhere in this
phase. Novelty of each particular result remains unestablished until a more
targeted bibliographic comparison and external review. No new universal
physical law or experimentally established scientific field is claimed.

7. FOLLOW-UP: SHARP RESET-DEFECT AND CROSSING-INTENSITY ENVELOPE
Follow-up recorded after the 2026-10-12 19:06:08 UTC clock reading.

The detector-boundary branch proposes a more precise result than positivity
alone. For reference reset inside B and comparison preparation outside B, it
bounds the cumulative occupation difference by G-eta K, where
G=integral_0^T exp[-bs-a(T-s)] ds,
K=T-A_a-A_b+G, A_c=integral_0^T exp(-ct) dt.
The eta parameter bounds conditional first-entrance mismatch in total variation;
a,b cap the relevant conditional macro-entry and exit intensities. Its
four-state saturation example is to be checked in that branch's independent
mathematical review. This audit does not substitute for that review.

At eta=1, the claim follows directly by comparing the two initial survival
events: occupation from inside is at least exp(-bt), while occupation from
outside is at most 1-exp(-at). Integration gives A_a+A_b-T. This is an elementary
survival-envelope argument, also applicable to non-Markov/time-dependent
processes with the stated hazard bounds. Calling this endpoint a new general
classical speed limit would overstate the evidence. The interpolation with a
conditional reset defect, a saturating classical construction, and inversion
into experimentally interpretable necessary conditions is the more specific
candidate contribution. No identical full statement was located in this audit;
priority remains unestablished.

Physical terminology and inference limits:

The inverted rate bound constrains necessary peak macro-crossing intensity.
It does not measure expected total dynamical activity, total microscopic jump
count, heat, entropy production, or energy. If a,b are given and K>0, an upper
confidence bound H_hi yields eta >= max(0,(G-H_hi)/K) for the declared class.
An answer above 1 excludes that entire class. An answer inside [0,1] is a
necessary condition, not identification of the hidden law. Estimating a mean
crossing rate does not calibrate a uniform history-conditioned hazard cap, and
two binary terminal preparations do not identify an unobserved entrance law.
These physical inputs require additional access or justified structural bounds.

8. FOLLOW-UP: A FOUR-STATE CLASSICAL REALIZATION THRESHOLD
Recorded after the 2026-10-12 19:17:29 UTC clock reading.

New candidate interpretation, subject to the independent mathematical review:
two preparations respectively inside and outside a fixed detected region have
nonnegative uniform-time cumulative occupation contrast in every homogeneous
finite CTMC with at most three states. One side of the detector partition must
then be a singleton; the established first-hit occupation ordering, applied to
the detector or its complement, gives the result. Four hidden states suffice to
realize any negative contrast strictly between -1 and 0. This establishes a
minimal realization size for this scalar datum under a specified dynamical
contract. It does not establish the minimal realization of the complete quantum
time series, a quantum computational advantage, or exclusion of all classical
explanations.

Important detector strengthening supplied to the mathematical reviewer: fixed
stochastic readout f(x) in [0,1] is allowed if the two initial means are exactly
1 and 0. These conditions force their supports onto the extremal level sets.
For at most three states every threshold detector separates those supports and
has a singleton side; integrating the threshold inequalities proves positivity.
Equivalently, for prepared states j,i and a third-state response c,
f=(1-c)1_j+c(1-1_i). This eliminates an unnecessary determinism assumption on
the third hidden state, while preserving substantive preparation/calibration
requirements. Imperfect extremality requires a separate robust theorem.

Targeted searches for occupation-time dimension witnesses did not locate this
exact at-most-three-state inequality with its four-state sharpness statement.
That negative search result is not a novelty certificate. The theorem is a short
corollary of standard first-passage ordering; presentation should emphasize the
operational interpretation, explicit assumptions, robustness and reproducibility
rather than a new general theory of dimension or memory.

Counting caveat: the state bound concerns a single common homogeneous Markov
realization that includes any hidden persistent degrees of freedom influencing
evolution. An omitted environment, switching controller, elapsed-time memory or
preparation-dependent generator changes the model class. Independent uniform
terminal sampling is an allowed external clock and must not drive the system.
The two-state quantum model proves attainability within quantum theory; actual
hardware validation remains absent. The four-state construction matches the
scalar contrast, not every accessible quantum transcript.

9. STRONGER FORM: MEASURE INITIAL CONTRAST INSTEAD OF ASSUMING EXTREMALITY

The independent reviewer subsequently proved a stronger operational statement:
with any two fixed preparation distributions and any fixed response f in [0,1],
every at-most-three-state homogeneous CTMC obeys |C0-C_uniform(T)|<=1. Here C0
is the directly measured preparation contrast and C_uniform the contrast at an
independent uniform terminal time. This replaces unobserved support assumptions
by two additional immediate-reference probabilities. Section 8's sign rule is
its extremal-preparation corollary, not the strongest current certificate.

Reason supplied by the reviewer: R=(1/T)integral exp(tQ)dt is stochastic with
column diagonal maxima Rjj>=Rij, by first-hit occupation ordering. For each
three-state row pair the signed vector (ei-ej)(I-R) has total-variation norm
1-min(Rii-Rji,Rjj-Rij)<=1. Arbitrary preparation mixtures and f in [0,1] preserve
the resulting bound. This concise proof is grounded in the previously credited
first-passage identity, not a newly discovered general stochastic principle.

Follow-up keyword searches including integrated/averaged occupation, temporal
dimension witnesses, and Dobrushin/three-state contraction did not locate the
exact inequality. The verified dimension references in section 8 remain the
essential comparisons. A claim of historical novelty still requires wider
theorem-level review. No generic first temporal witness or first quantum memory
advantage claim is appropriate.

Useful implementation consequence sent for independent checking: a shared fixed
delay d can be absorbed into both preparations if immediate-reference trials
measure at d and randomized trials at d+U. Homogeneity gives the effective
preparations mu exp(dQ), nu exp(dQ), allowed by this unrestricted-preparation
theorem. An independent shared random delay can likewise be averaged into the
preparations. Arm-dependent delay, detector drift, a clock that drives the
evolution, and a setting-dependent generator remain different assumptions.
