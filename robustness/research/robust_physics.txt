STATUS

The statements below are proved finite-state continuous-time Markov model
results and conditional inference certificates. They are not laboratory
observations or universal laws of energy expenditure. Their contribution is to
replace brittle ideal preparation and immediate readout assumptions by explicit
dimension-independent error bounds. Coupling, Duhamel identities, first-hitting
arguments, and oscillation contraction are established mathematics. No claim
of historical priority for those ingredients, or for the assembled bounds, is
made here. Independent internal red-team review has accepted the main factors;
external peer review and physical calibration are absent.

CONVENTIONS AND IDEAL THEOREM

All generators act on probability rows from the right. Q is a finite native
generator; pi Q=0. A separately selectable bidirectional physical channel has
rates a from i to j and b from j to i, i!=j. Its generator L has offdiagonal
entries L_ij=a,L_ji=b and diagonals -a,-b. Put s=1-r in (0,1], Qr=Q-sL,
and require both Q and Qr to be generators. Let P(t)=exp(tQ), Pr(t)=exp(tQr).
Native channel current is J=pi_i a-pi_j b. f=1_j is a singleton detector.

The previous finite-pulse theorem proves

  D_pi(t)=pi[P(t)-Pr(t)]f=s J H(t),
  H(t)=int_0^t [Pr_jj(u)-Pr_ij(u)]du>0,
  t exp(-Lambda_p t)<=H(t)<=t,

where Lambda_p bounds the two controlled endpoint exit rates. It follows from
pi Qr=-sJ(e_j-e_i) and the first hitting time of j under Qr. The integrated
kernel is positive even when its integrand changes sign. No irreducibility or
global exit-rate bound is required. Rates, current, and time must use a
consistent unit system. Exact finite precision inputs represent calibrated
envelopes, not literal claims that every physical rate is rational.

1. SHARP GENERATOR COUPLING RATE

For finite generators A(u),B(u), define

 kappa(u)=max_x 1/2 sum_y |A_xy(u)-B_xy(u)|
         =max_x max(sum_{y!=x}(A_xy-B_xy)_+,
                     sum_{y!=x}(B_xy-A_xy)_+).

The diagonal is included in the first sum. Measurable bounded time-dependent
rates are allowed. With the same initial distribution,

 TV(law(X_t),law(Y_t)) <= 1-exp[-int_0^t kappa(u)du].

Proof. While the coupled processes agree at x, perform equal jumps x->y at
rate min(A_xy,B_xy). Let U and V be the total leftover rates for A and B.
Their leftover destinations are disjoint. Couple leftover jumps simultaneously
to their distinct destinations at total rate min(U,V), and the remaining rate
|U-V| unilaterally. Separation therefore has hazard max(U,V), bounded by
kappa(u). The probability of any separation by t is at most the displayed
quantity; terminal disagreement and total variation cannot exceed it. The
coupling may be completed arbitrarily after separation. The same proof applies
to piecewise deterministic intervention schedules, provided A and B describe
the full actual and reference histories. For general measurable bounded rates,
standard time-inhomogeneous jump construction gives the integrated-hazard
bound. The implementation accepts an already calibrated rational upper bound
on this integral.

This improves the sufficient rate max_x sum_{y!=x}|A_xy-B_xy|, sometimes by a
factor of two. Example rows (-3,2,1) and (-3,1,2) have exact coupling hazard1
although their offdiagonal absolute difference sum is2. A one-way jump from a
fixed initial state against a frozen process attains 1-exp(-kappa t), so the
bound cannot be improved using kappa alone.

2. MATCHED NATIVE CONTROLS CANCEL THE SMALL-TIME PREPARATION PENALTY

Suppose both arms begin with the SAME preparation rho, with TV(rho,pi)<=eta.
Define D_rho=rho[P(t)-Pr(t)]f. Then

 |D_rho-D_pi| <= min{eta s(a+b)t,
                     2eta[1-exp(-s max(a,b)t)], 2eta}.

The same bound holds after any common stochastic postprocessing channel R,
with f replaced by Rf. It therefore survives common readout delay.

Proof. For a signed zero-mass row v, |vg|<=TV(v) osc(g), where
osc(g)=max g-min g. Markov semigroups contract oscillation. Duhamel gives

 P(t)-Pr(t)=s int_0^t Pr(u)L P(t-u)du.

For any real g, Lg is a(g_j-g_i) at i, -b(g_j-g_i) at j, and zero elsewhere.
Thus osc(Lg)=(a+b)|g_j-g_i| <=(a+b)osc(g). The integral consequently has
oscillation at most s(a+b)t for osc(f)<=1. This proves the first bound.
The coupling theorem gives |[P-Pr]f(x)|<=1-exp(-s max(a,b)t) for every x;
its oscillation is at most twice that bound. Finally every stochastic
expectation is in [0,1], giving oscillation at most2. Replacing f by Rf does
not change osc(f)<=1.

The linear coefficient eta s(a+b) is sharp at t=0. Take a positive two-state
stationary pi and rho-pi=(eta,-eta); then the derivative error is exactly
eta s(a+b). Parallel physical channels permit nonzero native selected-channel
current despite the two-state stationary aggregate current being zero.

After dividing by s t to estimate current, the first preparation penalty is
eta(a+b). It stays finite as t shrinks, unlike the old response bound 2eta,
which would give 2eta/(s t). This does not remove all preparation uncertainty:
eta(a+b) is a genuine possible current bias. If the two arms have different
preparations rho_c and rho_n, add TV(rho_c,rho_n) to the response error after
using rho_n as the matched preparation. That unmatched term can again be
amplified by 1/t. Random assignment to the same prepared ensemble supports the
matching premise; it does not by itself establish stationarity or independence
of records in the presence of uncontrolled temporal drift.

3. DELAYED READOUT HAS A PROVABLE SAFE WINDOW

After the pulse, apply common native evolution R(d)=exp(dQ). For singleton j,
put omega(d)=osc((R(d)-I)f). If lambda_j bounds the native departure rate from j
and beta_j bounds max_{x!=j}Q_xj, then

 omega(d) <= [1-exp(-lambda_j d)]+[1-exp(-beta_j d)].

Proof. At j, (R-I)f=P_jj(d)-1<=0 and its magnitude is at most the probability
of at least one departure, 1-exp(-lambda_j d). At any x!=j, it equals
P_xj(d)>=0, bounded by the probability of entering j. Until first entrance,
the hazard of entrance is at most beta_j, so this probability is bounded by
1-exp(-beta_j d). The range is bounded by the sum.

The ideal delayed contrast is still linear in the native current:

 D_pi(t,d)=s J K(t,d),
 K(t,d)=int_0^t (e_j-e_i)Pr(u)R(d)f du.

Its difference from the original kernel satisfies |K-H|<=t omega(d), since
the difference of two stochastic expectations of (R-I)f is bounded by its
oscillation. Also |K|<=t directly. Therefore

 max{-t,t[exp(-Lambda_p t)-omega(d)]} <= K(t,d) <= t.

If omega(d)<exp(-Lambda_p t), the native-current sign is protected despite
the delay, for every hidden network satisfying the local rate caps. This is
sufficient and generally conservative, not a necessary condition. When the
condition fails the checker abstains; it does not assert that the sign flips.
The prior release includes an actual classical delay-induced reversal, so an
unrestricted-delay theorem would be false.

4. CLOCK JITTER AND DETECTOR CONTRACT

Let the duration T be independent of the hidden state and preparation, with
T in [tlo,thi], tlo>0. It can have an arbitrary distribution and the same law
must be used in matched arms. The ideal response is the average of kernels.
For any fixed bound omega>=omega(d), a valid lower bound is

 min_{u in {tlo,thi}} u[exp(-Lambda_p u)-omega],

further maximized with -thi; the upper bound is thi. The endpoint minimum is
valid because u(exp(-Lambda_p u)-omega) has at most one positive critical point
at which it changes from increasing to decreasing. For Lambda_p>0 its derivative
is exp(-Lambda_p u)(1-Lambda_p u)-omega. It is nonpositive beyond1/Lambda_p;
before that point its positive part decreases strictly. For Lambda_p=0 the
function is linear. Thus no interior minimum occurs. Matched preparation error
is bounded using thi. If zero delay and tlo>0, arbitrary such duration jitter
cannot cause a sign reversal. State-dependent stopping or clocks invalidate
this argument; neither is included in the result.

A common fixed binary detector has mean offset+gain*p_j. Its offset cancels
in contrasts. Positive gain in [glo,ghi] rescales the positive kernel without
changing sign. Negative known gain can be handled by swapping detector labels;
unknown sign and zero gain do not allow current-orientation inference. Changes
of detector response between settings require a separate contrast-error bound.
An arbitrary coarse state partition is not such an affine singleton detector;
the previous classical coarse-detector counterexample still applies.

5. COMPLETE CONDITIONAL CURRENT CERTIFICATE

Let eta bound common preparation TV from native stationarity, zeta bound the
preparation mismatch between arms, K_n,K_c bound integrated row-TV generator
discrepancy between each entire actual history and its declared ideal history,
and epsilon_det bound remaining detector contrast error. Define

 b_p = min{eta s(a_cap+b_cap)thi,
           2eta[1-exp(-s max(a_cap,b_cap)thi)],2eta},
 b = ghi[b_p+zeta+(1-exp(-K_n))+(1-exp(-K_c))]+epsilon_det.

Suppose the sampling procedure provides a response interval [Dlo,Dhi] for the
actual native-minus-controlled recorded mean. Widen it by b and intersect with
[-ghi,ghi]. If the delayed/jittered kernel lower bound L is positive, the ideal
mean equals c J for some

 c in [glo*s*L, ghi*s*thi].

Evaluating the four endpoint ratios gives a rigorous current interval; intersect
also with [-b_cap,a_cap]. A strictly positive/negative interval certifies current
orientation under the declared contract. Empty intervals indicate inconsistent
data and contracts. Nonpositive L returns an unresolved delay contract. Joint
calibration failure probability, if present, adds once to the sampling failure
bound; software does not invent a confidence level for unvalidated calibration.

All response uncertainties above concern expectations. Arbitrarily many records
do not remove systematic mismatch. Generator error may be time dependent and
may act on many channels; only its integrated discrepancy cap enters this
robustness certificate. Perfect channel selectivity is therefore not required,
but its violation must be bounded independently. Different clocks, preparations,
or detector histories cannot be silently called shared nuisances.

6. STATIONARITY-FREE DIFFERENTIAL CURRENT ASSAY

For some applications a controlled preparation has an interesting instantaneous
channel current even when no stationary pi is known. With an arbitrary common
rho, set J_rho=rho_i a-rho_j b. The same matched experiment gives

 D_rho(t)=s int_0^t J_{rho Pr(u)}
                  [P_jj(t-u)-P_ij(t-u)]du.

This identity follows directly from Duhamel and the two supported rows of L.
It is exact and does not invoke stationarity. Let k_i',k_j' each bound both
the controlled departure hazard from the named endpoint and its maximal
controlled entrance hazard. Let lambda_j,beta_j bound native departure and
entrance as above, and |J_rho|<=Jmax. Then

 |D_rho(t)/(s t)-J_rho|
 <= t/2 [a k_i'+b k_j'+Jmax(lambda_j+beta_j)].

Proof. The membership indicator for state x can change only by a departure
from x or entry to x. Starting from an arbitrary distribution, the probability
of any membership change by u is at most 1-exp(-k_x' u)<=k_x'u. Hence
|J_{rho Pr(u)}-J_rho|<=a k_i'u+b k_j'u. Put
d(v)=P_jj(v)-P_ij(v), for which |d(v)|<=1 and
|d(v)-1|<=1-exp(-lambda_j v)+1-exp(-beta_j v)
           <=(lambda_j+beta_j)v.
Subtract J_rho inside the integral, use these inequalities, and integrate u
and t-u from0 to t. The displayed bound follows. Without a tighter current
cap, Jmax=max(a,b) is valid. The inference target is the prepared ensemble's
instantaneous physical channel current, not an unspecified steady-state flux.

This result removes the stationarity requirement for short-pulse current
measurement. It does not prove an all-time response sign law for arbitrary rho,
and cannot replace the stationary premise of a classical-null exclusion test.
Preparation matching, selective calibrated control, and sufficient rate bounds
are still needed. This is a certified finite-difference method, not a new
definition of current or an assertion that all latent mechanisms are observable.

7. COMMON GENERATOR DRIFT ALSO CANCELS TO FIRST ORDER

Suppose the same additive matrix E perturbs both pulse arms: actual native and
controlled generators are Q+E and Qr+E. All four generators must be valid.
Let max_x 1/2 sum_y |E_xy|<=kappa. With the same arbitrary initial rho and
common terminal effect f in [0,1], write D^E and D for actual and reference
matched contrasts. Then

 |D^E-D| <= s[a+b+2max(a,b)] int_0^t [1-exp(-kappa u)]du
          <= s[a+b+2max(a,b)] kappa t^2/2.

Proof. Apply the response Duhamel formula to each contrast and subtract:

 D^E-D=s int_0^t rho[(Pr^E(u)-Pr(u))L P^E(t-u)
                          +Pr(u)L(P^E(t-u)-P(t-u))]f du.

For the first term, the row difference has TV at most1-exp(-kappa u), and
osc(L P^E f)<=(a+b). For the second term, rho Pr(u)L equals its instantaneous
channel current, of absolute value at most max(a,b), times(e_j-e_i). The
oscillation of(P^E-P)f is at most2[1-exp(-kappa(t-u))]. Integrating proves
the first bound; 1-exp(-kappa u)<=kappa u proves the second. The exact integral
is t-[1-exp(-kappa t)]/kappa for kappa>0 and zero at kappa=0.

After division by s t, the drift-induced current error is at most
[a+b+2max(a,b)]kappa t/2 and vanishes with t. This is stronger than treating
the two arms' generator errors independently. It is valid only for a shared
additive E, not merely two errors with the same norm or similar verbal origin.
Common stochastic terminal processing is allowed. If the actual and reference
postpulse delay semigroups also differ, their difference needs a separate
bound; it is not silently included in this pulse-only helper.

This distinction is experimentally useful. Shared drift preserves the leading
channel-current response, while arm-specific intervention spillover can produce
a first-order response error and a nonvanishing current-calibration floor.
The existing complete current helper conservatively uses independent-arm
error bounds; users can compose the shared-drift helper only when its stronger
premise has been separately established.

VALIDATION AND REPRODUCTION

Run:
 python -m unittest discover -s tests -p test_robust_physics.py -v
 python code/robust_physics.py

Fourteen tests cover exact rational exponential brackets, generator validation,
the sharper coupling norm, sharp preparation derivative, bounded clock jitter,
delay abstention, detector constraints, and exact current certificates. A seeded
synthetic experiment checks180 positive random networks of dimensions2 through8
against five analytically proved bounds: coupling, matched preparation, delayed
response, stationarity-free short-pulse bias, and shared generator drift. It uses NumPy and SciPy expm;
the observed ratios are numerical checks, not substitutes for the proofs.
The public certificate code uses Fraction arithmetic and explicit positive
Taylor remainder bounds; it never certifies a sign using floating arithmetic.

One test was corrected during development: clipping a kernel bound below -thi
is valid because |K|<=thi, but comparing that clipped bound against the unclipped
auxiliary expression was an invalid test. The test now respects both proved
bounds. The implementation and theorem did not need alteration.

LIMITATIONS AND NEXT PHYSICAL GATE

The retained endpoint is a singleton or a separately certified regenerative
coarse interface. The model is a finite classical Markov process; instruments
with memory require an enlarged hidden state space and a newly justified
physical channel/readout contract. Common preparation TV from a hidden stationary
distribution can be difficult to calibrate; it cannot be inferred from observing
a single steady mean. The stationarity-free assay offers a meaningful alternate
current target when that calibration is unavailable. None of these results
makes a response crossing a device-independent witness of quantum coherence.

The highest-value empirical gate is now a matched-control experiment that
calibrates preparation matching, native endpoint hazards, unwanted intervention
rates, detector gain/drift, and the full timing schedule. Given those inputs,
the exact software can determine whether a finite response interval certifies a
current sign or whether the experiment is limited by a named calibration floor.
