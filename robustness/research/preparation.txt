Preparation, randomized readout, and a Markov occupation maximum principle
Abdinoor / 2D Labs / 12 October 2026
Status: scoped mathematical derivations and reproducible simulations; historical
priority and independent physical validation are not established.

1. The experimental change

Replace a stationary preparation plus selective channel pulse by a trusted
preparation of a detected state j and a comparison preparation. Evolve under the
same fixed dynamics in both arms. Select an independent readout time uniformly
from [0,T], starting at preparation, and record whether the system occupies j.
The comparison preparation can be ANY fixed initial distribution over the same
classical states. Only the reference j preparation must be trusted as a singleton
or satisfy the explicitly stated reset condition below. No stationary state,
selective channel control, network size, or full dynamical reconstruction enters
the resulting classical inequality.

This changes the access contract; it does not universally dominate the pulse
experiment. Physical state preparation, common dynamics, and a protected readout
must themselves be independently justified. A qubit computational-basis label
does not by itself certify a singleton classical hidden state.

2. Classical theorem and proof

Let Q be a finite, time-homogeneous CTMC generator, P(t)=exp(tQ), and i!=j.
No irreducibility assumption is needed. Write
  K_ij(T)=integral_0^T [P_jj(t)-P_ij(t)] dt.
For the first hitting time tau_j from i, the strong Markov property gives
  P_ij(t)=integral_[0,t] P_jj(t-u) Pr_i(tau_j in du).
Tonelli's theorem then gives the exact positive identity
  K_ij(T)=integral_0^T P_jj(s) Pr_i(tau_j>T-s) ds > 0, T>0.
Strict positivity follows, for example, from the event of no departure from j
for s time units and no departure from i for T-s units. Finite exit rates make
both probabilities positive. If the two exit rates are bounded by Lambda,
  K_ij(T)/T >= exp(-Lambda T).
This lower bound is sufficient, not generally sharp. With separate caps a,b,
  K/T >= (1/T) integral_0^T exp(-b s-a(T-s)) ds.

For any initial probability row rho,
  K_rho,j(T)=sum_i rho_i K_ij(T)>=0,
where K_jj=0. It is strict whenever rho assigns positive mass outside j. Therefore
  Pr(Y=1 | prepare j, uniform time) >=
  Pr(Y=1 | prepare rho, uniform time).
The preparation rho need not be known to establish this null inequality.

The finite-state assumption can be relaxed to a conservative, nonexplosive,
strong Markov jump process with a genuine singleton j and finite local holding
rates at the relevant preparations. The finite version is the release claim;
no diffusion, arbitrary macroscopic observable, or generic quantum extension is
inferred automatically.

3. Reset targets and the limits of the theorem

A coarse target B is admissible under a sufficient regeneration contract: the
reference preparation nu_B lies inside B; the comparison starts outside B; upon
first hitting B the conditional state distribution is nu_B, independently of the
hitting time and previous history. Subsequent evolution must be the same fixed
Markov process. Then
  E_rho[1_B(X_t)] = integral_0^t E_nuB[1_B(X_(t-u))] F_tau(du)
and precisely the same positive integral argument holds. Mixtures with nu_B can
also be admitted. This is a real, testable interface assumption, not a consequence
of calling B a detector state. Equal entry laws alone without conditional reset
at every entry-time value are insufficient.

Coarse counterexample: states j=0,i=1,k=2,a=3; rates j->a and i->k both equal 2,
with a,k absorbing. Detect B={j,k}; reference starts exactly at j, comparison at i.
The instantaneous contrast is 2 exp(-2t)-1. Uniform averaging on [0,1] gives
  contrast = -exp(-2) <0.
Thus ordinary finite classical dynamics can reverse this coarse response. The
result is not a device-independent quantum witness.

The same time-homogeneous dynamics must hold throughout. A classical externally
scheduled process can violate the inequality: in the first unit interval allow
only j->a at rate4, then in the second allow only i->j at rate4. The uniform
contrast on [0,2] is negative. Such a process can also be represented by augmenting
the system with a clock, but the enlarged preparation/readout then fails the
singleton or reset contract. An independent clock for readout is distinct from a
clock that changes the tested dynamics.

4. Which readout clocks preserve the inequality?

Let w(t) be a nonnegative, normalized, nonincreasing density on [0,infinity),
independent of system trajectory and the preparation arm. For bounded support,
Stieltjes integration by parts gives
  integral w(t) dK(t) = w(T)K(T) - integral K(t) dw(t) >=0.
The infinite-support result follows by truncation or the uniform-mixture theorem
below. No differentiability of w is required. The contrast is strictly positive
for different singleton preparations under any genuine normalized decreasing
density. A common, positive-gain affine binary detector preserves the sign:
m(p)=d0+k p, k>0, and its offset cancels.

Every such w (up to changes on null sets) is a mixture of uniform clocks:
  w(t)=integral_[t,infinity) mu(dT)/T,
  mu(dT)=-T dw(T),
where mu is a probability measure. Finite downward jumps produce atoms of mu.
The identity integral mu=integral w=1 follows by integration by parts, with the
usual endpoint limits t w(t)->0 at zero and infinity for an integrable decreasing
function. Tonelli proves that each protected-clock contrast is the same mixture
of uniform-clock contrasts. Consequently no protected density can beat the best
uniform horizon for a specified alternative if there is no extra clock-resource
constraint. Under an average-time constraint, mixtures can matter; this is a
standard convex optimization problem, not a new variational principle.

Randomizing the cutoff T and then drawing time uniformly in [0,T] preserves the
classical bound exactly, even if the cutoff distribution is broad. A delayed
uniform interval [d,d+T], a readout selected by the observed trajectory, unequal
clock laws between arms, or an increasing density lacks this guarantee. An
increasing density can give a negative contrast in an ordinary three-state
cycle. Nonincreasing density is sufficient, not claimed necessary: a clock with
mass >=1/2 at zero and its remaining mass at any positive time is nonnegative
for every stochastic transition kernel. The full universal clock cone is open
in this work and unnecessary for the stated protocol.

5. A physical quantum alternative and its sharp damping threshold

Use a qubit with
  L(rho)=-i (omega/2)[X,rho]+gamma(Tr(rho)I/2-rho),
where omega>0 and gamma>=0. This is a valid Lindblad generator: the second term
is the depolarizing semigroup generator, expressible using Pauli jumps of rate
gamma/4 for each of X,Y,Z. Prepare j=|0>, i=|1>, and read Pi_j=|0><0|. The
instantaneous probability contrast is exp(-gamma t) cos(omega t). Hence
  C(T)=[gamma+exp(-gamma T)(-gamma cos(omega T)+omega sin(omega T))]
       /[(gamma^2+omega^2)T].
For gamma=0, this reduces continuously to sin(omega T)/(omega T).

Let I(T)=T C(T). Its derivative is exp(-gamma T)cos(omega T). Its first local
minimum after the initial maximum is T*=3pi/(2omega). Later minima are
  I(T_n)=[gamma-omega exp(-gamma(3pi/2+2pi n)/omega)]/(gamma^2+omega^2),
and increase toward gamma/(gamma^2+omega^2). The initial value is zero and the
first minimum is the smallest nontrivial one. Therefore negative contrast exists
if and only if
  omega exp(-3pi gamma/(2omega))>gamma.
For gamma>0 write r=omega/gamma. This is equivalent to
  r log r>3pi/2,
  r>exp(W(3pi/2))=3.64417367164563213617... .
The root is unique because r log r is strictly increasing for r>1. This is an
exact threshold for this qubit model and these orthogonal preparations; it is
not a universal threshold of quantum coherence or of experimental accessibility.

The optimal negative uniform average lies before T*, within (pi/omega,T*).
There is a unique minimum in the negative part of this interval: the derivative
of C is [T I'(T)-I(T)]/T^2, and the numerator has derivative T I''(T)>0 throughout
(pi/omega,T*). At the first zero of I it is negative; at T* it is positive. Later
periods cannot improve the negative contrast because
  I(T+2pi n/omega)=c+exp(-2pi n gamma/omega)(I(T)-c),
with c=gamma/(gamma^2+omega^2), while the denominator T increases. The unitary
limit obeys the same conclusion with c=0.

At gamma=1/10, omega=2, T=9/4 and common detector gain9/10,
  observed C=-0.1440713137250376... < -18/125.
An 80-degree rational Taylor enclosure certifies the final strict inequality.
Mean evolution time is9/8 per record, excluding preparation, readout, and reset.
This is substantially stronger signal than the prior selective-pulse example,
but the access assumptions differ and signal alone does not establish general
experimental superiority.

Exponential readout time with rate lambda is a protected clock, yet its contrast
  lambda(lambda+gamma)/[(lambda+gamma)^2+omega^2]
is strictly positive. Thus preserving the classical inequality does not guarantee
power against a specified quantum alternative. A truncated exponential density
has the same sign condition as a uniform clock with gamma replaced by gamma plus
its decay rate; it can erase the reversal by overweighting early readouts.

6. Coherent comparison preparation: a stronger assay with stronger controls

For any CPTP semigroup Phi_t and effect Pi_j, form the accessible averaged effect
  A_T=(1/T) integral_0^T Phi_t^dagger(Pi_j) dt.
Reference score is <j|A_T|j>. A freely chosen comparison state achieves the
largest score lambda_max(A_T); the best possible reversal gap is
  V_T=lambda_max(A_T)-<j|A_T|j>.
This is the Rayleigh variational principle, not a new spectral theorem. Any
nonzero matrix element of A_T coupling j to its orthogonal subspace guarantees
strict V_T>0. The associated comparison preparation is a top eigenstate.

For the qubit above, let
 C=integral_0^T exp(-gamma t)cos(omega t) dt/T,
 S=integral_0^T exp(-gamma t)sin(omega t) dt/T.
Then A_T=(I+C Z +/- S Y)/2, with sign depending on the Hamiltonian convention,
  V_T=(sqrt(C^2+S^2)-C)/2,
  S=[omega-exp(-gamma T)(omega cos(omega T)+gamma sin(omega T))]
    /[(gamma^2+omega^2)T].
For gamma>0, omega>0, and T>0, S>0. This follows by splitting sine into successive
half-periods: positive lobes precede negative lobes and carry larger exponential
weight; within a negative lobe the minimum accumulated integral is at its end,
where the paired contribution remains positive. Therefore V_T>0 for every such
T. The orthogonal-preparation damping threshold disappears when arbitrary
coherent comparison preparation is permitted. At gamma=0 the exceptional full
periods have A_T=I/2 and V_T=0.

This distinction is useful experimental design, not a new claim that arbitrarily
strong damping is easily overcome. For fixed T and gamma>>omega, V shrinks
approximately as omega^2/(4gamma^3 T), requiring many more records and precision.
Moreover coherent preparation already relies on quantum controls. The result is
an assay of a declared reset-and-dynamics contract, not device-independent proof
of quantum mechanics or a solution to hidden classical-state ambiguity.

7. Calibration and statistics interface

Fixed total-variation preparation errors eps_j,eps_i change the contrast by at
most eps_j+eps_i. Because the classical null permits any comparison distribution,
only eps_j is necessary for null validity; eps_i is useful for alternative power
planning. Common clock-law discrepancy delta in total variation changes the
contrast by at most2delta, since the difference of two probabilities lies in
[-1,1]. Separate detector probability errors d_j,d_i add d_j+d_i. Common fixed
positive-gain affine detector errors need not be treated as independent offsets;
its offset cancels exactly. Errors should not be double-counted if one calibrated
record-law envelope already includes several mechanisms.

One can use a fixed-sample one-sided difference test, or randomize preparations
and transform each trial into a Bernoulli outcome, enabling an anytime-valid
likelihood-ratio test. These statistical guarantees require the declared record
independence/conditional mean assumptions. They do not certify physical reset,
common dynamics, or clock independence from the same records being tested.
The statistical companion agent owns those implementations.

8. Closest established comparator and honest interpretation

Root's literature audit identified Lambert et al., Physical Review Letters105,
176801(2010), DOI10.1103/PhysRevLett.105.176801, as a close and important prior
operational inequality for arbitrary-size classical hidden-state dynamics with
a singleton preparation/readout. Its inequality
  |2P_jj(t)-P_jj(2t)|<=1
already excludes a class of dynamics using survival probabilities. Indeed the
classical semigroup gives the stronger elementary relation
  P_jj(2t)>=P_jj(t)^2,
from the j term of sum_k P_jk(t)P_kj(t); and 2p-p^2<=1 gives the upper inequality.
A coherent qubit violates these established tests. Therefore the new randomized
occupation assay cannot honestly be presented as the first way to test an
arbitrarily large classical network, or as newly discovering the significance
of coherent oscillations. A useful claimed difference must concern its exact
integrated preparation inequality, clock robustness, quantitative design, and
specific experimentally matched advantage, if one survives.

The integrated test uses a different comparison preparation and one random-time
readout per record, while the survival test uses one preparation and two fixed
readout durations. Which is preferable depends on preparation costs, detector
calibration, timing, and the anticipated alternative. There is no universal
ordering. Comparing only the linear survival inequality does not establish
superiority over the stronger nonlinear semigroup relation or general likelihood
methods.

10. Additional completed design and comparator checks

The final module has ten tests. It additionally certifies the fixed +Y comparison
preparation at gamma1/10, omega2, T7/4, detector gain9/10:
  gap >257/1000,
with mean evolution time7/8. This uses the Hamiltonian convention in section5,
for which the averaged effect is (I+C Z+S Y)/2. Its exact gap is (S-C)/2; no
transcendental optimal-state angle is required. Spectral optimal-state bounds use
rational integer-square-root enclosures.

For a weighted difference sum_k a_k p_k with alternative gap g and independent
arm-observation costs c_k, the optimized sufficient Hoeffding resource proxy is
  (sum_k |a_k| sqrt(c_k))^2/g^2.
The common confidence factor is omitted consistently. Physical preparations and
expected evolution time are evaluated separately; this does not certify physical
energy or exact minimum sample complexity. Numerical grid search plus bounded
minimization over time parameter [.001,5] gives, at gamma=.1,omega2 and ideal
known detection with no preparation overhead:
  Method                       Physical records    Expected evolution time
  Uniform orthogonal             156.0767                  174.0245
  Lambert linear survival        219.6776                  133.6403
  Survival product                75.8371                   58.0190
  Uniform fixed quadrature        48.8052                   40.1474
  Uniform optimized state         48.3858                   40.0173
Each column optimizes its own duration; these are not certified global minima.
For the survival-product baseline, an arm observation multiplies two independent
length-t binary survival outcomes (cost two physical records), and is compared
with one length-2t survival outcome. Its classical inequality is p(t)^2<=p(2t).
The comparison honestly finds that this established semigroup consequence beats
the simple orthogonal randomized assay. The improved quadrature/optimized assay
uses an extra coherent preparation resource. It is not a generally superior test
under identical operation sets. Manuscript/preparation.tex records this result.

11. Baseline-subtracted dimension-witness preparation design

The root and dimension agent subsequently proved a separate classical bound for
<=3 hidden states with arbitrary fixed initial distributions mu,nu and stochastic
f: |C0-CT|<=1. I did not derive that classical dimension theorem here. Conditional
on that theorem, quantum optimization uses D=Pi-A, not A alone. For antipodal
qubit preparations n,-n, W=n_z(1-C)-n_y S; the maximum over preparations is
sqrt((1-C)^2+S^2), attained along (0,-S,1-C). This is the ordinary spectral-diameter
variational principle. Orthogonal Z preparations yield W=1-C and require modeled
detector gain k>1/(1-C). The classical null does not assume that gain model: it
already permits arbitrary fixed stochastic detector f.

Exact rational design: gamma1/10, omega2, T2, gain9/10; n=(0,-12/37,35/37).
Because12^2+35^2=37^2 these are pure antipodal preparations. Eighty-degree exact
Taylor bounds certify
  W=1.0806050936582667... >27/25.
The proposed simpler n=(0,-7/25,24/25) instead gives W=1.07938595976 at T2.
Numerical state-and-time optimization gives Wideal≈1.20122939091557 at
T≈2.04524467, or1.08110645182401 after gain .9; no global optimizer is certified.
