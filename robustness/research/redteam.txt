CORE POSITIVITY: ACCEPTED

For any finite, time-homogeneous CTMC with transition semigroup P(t), distinct
individual states i,j and any T>0,

 K(T)=integral_0^T (P_jj(t)-P_ij(t)) dt > 0.

The first hitting time tau_j from i and the strong Markov property give

 K(T)=integral_0^T P_jj(s) Pr_i(tau_j>T-s) ds.

This follows by subtracting expected occupation accumulated after tau_j from
occupation under an initial j preparation. Holding rates are finite, so both
integrand factors have positive no-jump lower bounds at every finite time.
Irreducibility, reversibility, stationarity, graph size and detailed balance
are unnecessary. Reducible networks and infinite hitting times are included.
Endpoint exit caps Lambda_i,Lambda_j provide a computable lower bound
integral_0^T exp[-Lambda_j s-Lambda_i(T-s)]ds.

A clock U uniform on [0,T], sampled independently of preparation, trajectory,
and readout, implements K(T)/T with one endpoint readout per preparation arm.
Thus the new protocol can dispense with stationary preparation and selective
channel intervention if the two actual classical singleton states can instead
be prepared and read out. This is a change in the access contract, not removal
of every experimental assumption.

A nonnegative nonincreasing density w has a positive mixture-of-initial-window
representation; integration by parts against K proves nonnegative response.
Monotonicity is SUFFICIENT, not claimed necessary. For example a clock measure
3/4 delta_0 + 1/4 delta_T has contrast at least 1/2 for every pair of stochastic
kernels, despite a positive-time atom. A complete characterization of universal
clock measures has not been proved in this audit.

CLASSICAL FAILURE CONTROLS: VERIFIED

1. A fixed-time readout can be negative. The directed unit-rate three-cycle
   Q=[[-1,1,0],[0,-1,1],[1,0,-1]], j=0,i=2 has
   P_jj(2)-P_ij(2) approximately -0.0363653, while K(2)>0.
   Pointwise return-probability dominance is false.

2. A shifted uniform clock is not covered. For the same directed cycle,
   averaging over [3/2,5/2] gives a strictly negative result, certified using
   rational Taylor bounds. A readout dead time cannot silently be relabeled
   as a uniform initial-window clock. An increasing normalized density 2t/25
   on [0,5] also gives approximately -0.00010680343058, exactly certified.

3. Coarse observation can violate the sign even with a homogeneous finite
   generator. Take j=0,i=1,k=2,a=3, j->a and i->k at rate2, with k,a absorbing,
   and detect B={j,k}. The instantaneous contrast is 2exp(-2t)-1; averaging
   over [0,1] is -exp(-2)<0. The initial i path enters B at k rather than
   regenerating the j preparation. A protected interface/reset condition is
   essential for extending the singleton theorem to macrostates.

4. Time-dependent generators can violate the sign with exact singleton
   preparations and readout. During [0,1], only j->a occurs, at rate4.
   During [1,2], only i->j occurs, at rate4. Both preparation arms experience
   the same schedule. The average over [0,2] is

   [2(1-exp(-4))/4 + exp(-4)-1]/2 = -0.2454210902778...

   Therefore Markovianity by itself is insufficient; time homogeneity (or a
   separate proved nonstationary extension) is required.

5. A trajectory-dependent clock invalidates the simple mixture argument.
   Preparing the same observed macrostate with different hidden ages or
   environmental correlations also invalidates a singleton Markov model.
   Independent fresh trials are sufficient for the fixed-sample analysis;
   sequential analysis must state its conditional-mean assumption explicitly.

These failures mean that negative contrast excludes the declared classical
access model. It does not exclude all classical hidden-state descriptions or
certify quantum coherence without independent access calibration.

QUANTUM EXAMPLE: ACCEPTED AND INDEPENDENTLY VERIFIED

For a qubit with Hamiltonian H=(omega/2)X and depolarizing generator
L(rho)=-i[H,rho]+gamma(Tr(rho)I/2-rho), prepare Z eigenstates and read the first
Z projector. The pointwise contrast is exp(-gamma t)cos(omega t), and the
uniform contrast is

 [gamma+exp(-gamma T)(-gamma cos(omega T)+omega sin(omega T))]
 /[(gamma^2+omega^2)T].

For gamma>0, the integrated contrast has its first negative candidate minimum
at T=3pi/(2omega). All subsequent minima have smaller oscillatory amplitudes.
A negative contrast exists iff r log r > 3pi/2, r=omega/gamma. The unique
positive boundary with r>1 is approximately 3.644173671645632. This criterion
is a property of this chosen physical model, not a universal coherence threshold.

At gamma=1/10, omega=2, T=9/4, a 96-order EXACT RATIONAL matrix Taylor enclosure
certifies contrast<-4/25. Independently constructing the 4x4 complex Liouville
superoperator and its augmented 8x8 integration matrix gives the same result.
This avoids reliance on the implementation's closed-form formula. Results are
model predictions, not measured physical data.

ROBUST PHYSICS BOUNDS: ACCEPTED

Let pi Q=0, Qr=Q-sL_e, s=1-r, with selected forward/backward rates a,b, and
endpoint test f=1_j. Matched controlled and native arms starting at common rho
have contrast C_rho=rho(P_r-P)f. For TV(rho,pi)<=eta,

 |C_rho-C_pi| <= eta*s*(a+b)*t.

Proof: Duhamel writes P_r-P as an integral of Markov contractions around the
local generator difference. Its action on a [0,1] test has oscillation at most
s(a+b). Signed mass rho-pi contracts test oscillation with coefficient eta.
The separate maximal-coupling bound gives

 |C_rho-C_pi| <= 2eta[1-exp(-s max(a,b)t)].

The factor2 is necessary for the generic oscillation bound: the controlled-minus-
native test can have both signs. Fixed initial TV error is not automatically
amplified as 1/t in the matched contrast, unlike a comparison to an unmatched
stationary baseline.

For two finite generators Q,R, set kappa=max_x ||(Q-R)_{x,.}||_1/2. Equivalently
this is max over rows of the larger of positive and negative off-diagonal
excess totals. A valid common-state coupling matches shared jumps and pairs
positive and negative excess departures to different destinations. Its total
divergence hazard is the larger excess total, not their sum. Therefore

 sup_x TV(P_Q(t)(x,.),P_R(t)(x,.)) <= 1-exp(-kappa t).

This also covers a known pointwise bound on a time-dependent mismatch rate via
an integrated hazard, but it does not make the underlying sign theorem valid
for arbitrary scheduled native generators.

For a common post-pulse semigroup R_d and stationary ideal preparation, write
omega_d=osc((R_d-I)f). The delay error is at most s|J|t omega_d. For native
postprocessing,

 omega_d <= (1-exp(-lambda_j d))+(1-exp(-beta_j d)),
 beta_j=max_{x!=j}q_xj.

The first term bounds loss from j; the second bounds entry to j from outside.
A sufficient sign-protection condition is omega_d<exp(-Lambda_p t). This is a
sufficient bound, not a necessary criterion or a statement that arbitrary delay
preserves the sign. The old delayed-readout counterexample remains relevant.

STATISTICAL REVIEW CHECKPOINT

The proposed matched-pair e-process is valid if X_n in[-1,1] and conditional on
all available past evidence E[X_n|past]<=b_n, with predictable b_n in[0,1).
Then W_n=(X_n-b_n)/(1+b_n) lies in[-1,1] and has conditional mean<=0. For any
predictable lambda_n in[0,1], products of 1+lambda_n W_n are nonnegative
supermartingales. A fixed nonnegative mixture is also one, so Ville bounds
anytime rejection at 1/alpha by alpha. For the prepare-integrate protocol,
X should be detector outcome under i minus outcome under j. A globally valid
joint calibration event adds its failure probability once.

Adaptive protocol choice is safe only if EACH chosen experiment has the
conditional null bound. Marginally unbiased trials with latent shared drift do
not imply this bound. Two sign-oriented e-processes can be combined by an
intersection-union rule only if at least one orientation is a null for the
whole path; varying native current sign across chosen setups breaks that premise.

FINAL ADDENDUM: SHARP COARSE-INTERFACE BOUND — ACCEPTED

Let B be a detected set, nuB supported inside B, nuA outside B. Every hidden
outside state has entrance hazard into B at most a; every inside state has
exit hazard from B at most b. Let conditional first-entrance laws, at almost
every entrance time, differ from nuB in total variation by at most eta.
For U_nu(T)=integral_0^T P_nu(B,t)dt, define

 A_a=(1-exp(-aT))/a, A_b=(1-exp(-bT))/b,
 G=integral_0^T exp[-bs-a(T-s)]ds,
 K=T-A_a-A_b+G.

Zero rates use the continuous extension A_0=T. Then

 U_nuB-U_nuA >= G-eta K.

The proof is valid: the reset term in the hitting decomposition is at least G.
For any x in B, integrated B occupancy lies between A_b(v) and v, so the
oscillation of that occupation reward within B is at most v-A_b(v). The reset
error is at most eta E[g_b((T-tau)+)] with g_b(v)=v-A_b(v). Bounded entrance
hazard makes tau stochastically no smaller than Exp(a); this expectation is at
most K. The four-state chain i->j at a(1-eta), i->k at a eta, j->ell at b,
B={j,k}, with k and ell absorbing, attains equality. Seven rational extremizers
were independently evaluated by integrated matrix Taylor series; 360 random
coarse-network cases check the unrestricted eta=1 inequality.

At eta=1 the bound reduces to A_a+A_b-T and does not need a regenerative
interface or time homogeneity: bounded conditional macrostate crossing hazards
are sufficient. This is the strongest robust scope extension in the phase.
The caps must apply to every relevant hidden state or history. They cannot be
identified with observed initial slopes or dissipative Lindblad jump rates
without a separate physical argument. A violation can supply a lower bound on
the peak classical crossing intensity required to explain the data. It does not
bound total dynamical activity, energetic dissipation, or all classical models.

The mixed-preparation extension is also correct. If a comparison preparation
has inside mass p, inside-reset TV defect eta0 and outside-entry defect eta1,
then its contrast satisfies

 H >= (1-p)(G-eta1 K)-p eta0(T-A_b).

The same extremizer attains equality with inside preparation
(1-eta0)delta_j+eta0 delta_k. Taking both defects to one gives
H>=A_b+(1-p)A_a-T. This is necessary when using a comparison such as a quantum
Y state with initial detector probability one half; the outside-only bound
cannot silently be applied to that experiment.

FINAL DIMENSION-WITNESS REVIEW: ACCEPTED

For a finite, time-homogeneous classical CTMC with at most three hidden states,
let R=T^{-1}integral_0^T exp(tQ)dt. The integrated return maximum proves
R_jj>=R_ij for every i,j, strictly for distinct states at finite positive T.
This gives two useful and distinct conclusions.

A. If a reference preparation is supported inside a proper detected set B and
   the comparison outside B, their uniform integrated contrast is positive.
   For at most three states, either B or its complement is a singleton. The
   first case is exactly the return maximum. In the second, complementing the
   detector swaps the two preparations and reduces to the same maximum.
   Therefore a negative contrast requires at least four hidden classical states
   under these assumptions. Four suffice for this one averaged contrast: the
   four-state extremizer with equal entrance/exit rate lambda gives

     C(T)=2(1-exp(-lambda T))/(lambda T)-1.

   This decreases continuously from1 to-1, since exp(z)>1+z for z>0. Every
   negative value strictly between-1 and0 has a finite-rate realization.

B. A stronger operational form avoids the trusted-support assumption. For any
   two arbitrary fixed preparation distributions mu,nu and any fixed stochastic
   detector f taking values in[0,1], define

     C0=(mu-nu)f,
     CT=(mu-nu)R f.

   Every at-most-three-state homogeneous CTMC satisfies

     |C0-CT|<=1.

   Proof: for any two distinct states i,j, put
   a=R_ii-R_ji>=0 and b=R_jj-R_ij>=0. For three states, the row difference
   (e_i-e_j)(I-R) has entries1-a, -1+b, and a-b. Its total-variation norm is
   exactly1-min(a,b)<=1. For two states a=b and the same bound follows.
   Any zero-mass difference of preparation distributions is a convex mixture
   of such row differences, and any f in[0,1] contracts their TV norm.
   This proves the claimed absolute bound. It is strict for finite positive
   T and distinct preparations, but1 is a sharp universal supremum.

   Sharpness example: state0 is absorbing, state1 jumps at rate lambda to
   absorbing state2. Take detector responses(c,0,1), reference state0 and
   comparison state1. Then C0=c, CT=c-1+(1-exp(-lambda T))/(lambda T), so
   C0-CT approaches1 from below. No exact extremal-preparation assumption is
   needed in this version; C0 is measured as an additional reference arm.

The qubit example with gamma1/10, omega2, T9/4, and common detector gain9/10
has C0=.9 and CT<-.144, hence C0-CT>261/250>1. The strict rational inequality
is independently certified from the matrix Taylor enclosure. It excludes
at-most-three-state homogeneous classical explanations with the specified
common evolution, detector, preparations, and independent randomized timing.
It does not exclude four-state explanations, arbitrary classical memory,
within-record scheduled dynamics, or setting-dependent preparation/detection.

A shared dead time d is harmless for this stronger form if the baseline reads
at d and the delayed arm at d+U: use effective preparations mu exp(dQ) and
nu exp(dQ). The same applies to a shared random latency independent of the
preparation arm and U. Static arm-independent mixtures of valid classical
models also preserve the linear inequality by convexity. Decreasing-density
clocks preserve the diagonal-column maximum of R, so the proof applies to
those clocks as well.

The four-state sufficiency statement concerns the integrated endpoint record,
not the full time-tagged trajectory law. With symmetric rates, the four-state
model also matches both averaged arm probabilities of the unital orthogonal
qubit example; it need not match probabilities conditioned on the actual
readout time. This distinction is essential when claiming minimal state count.
The conclusion is a hidden-state cardinality requirement, not a lower bound on
Shannon entropy, physical energy, or a universal computational memory advantage.
