PRIMARY RESULT
Let R be a finite CTMC, B a detector region, nuA supported outside B, and nuB
supported inside B. Define U_nu(T)=integral_0^T Pr_nu(X_s in B) ds and
H=U_nuB-U_nuA. Every outside-to-B instantaneous hazard is <=a and every
inside-to-outside hazard is <=b. Conditional on first entrance time tau=u,
the entrance distribution zeta_u obeys TV(zeta_u,nuB)<=eta for almost every
entrance time u<=T. This time-conditional reset bound must not be replaced by
an unconditional mixture bound.

Set A_c=(1-exp(-cT))/c (A_0=T), G=integral_0^T exp(-bs-a(T-s)) ds,
and K=T-A_a-A_b+G. Then H>=G-eta K, sharply.

PROOF
First entrance gives the exact decomposition H=G_actual-Remainder, where
G_actual=integral_0^T P_nuB(B,s) Pr(tau>T-s) ds >= G.
For each x in B, A_b(v)<=U_x(v)<=v, hence osc_B U(v)<=v-A_b(v).
The reset defect gives Remainder<=eta E[g_b((T-tau)+)], with g_b(v)=v-A_b(v).
Since the entrance hazard is <=a, tau stochastically dominates Exp(a).
Integrating the increasing function g_b gives E g_b<=K. Neither irreducibility
nor stationarity is required.

Attainment uses states i,j,k,l, detector B={j,k}, initial states i and j, and
transitions i->j at a(1-eta), i->k at a eta, j->l at b. States k and l are
absorbing. Every inequality in the proof is then an equality.

PHYSICAL SELECTIVE-CHANNEL COMPATIBILITY
For 0<eta<1, add j->i, k->j, and l->i at rate epsilon, reducing j->l to b-epsilon.
The caps and reset law remain unchanged, and the graph becomes irreducible.
Doubling the i<->j rates defines a native generator Q; suppression by r=1/2
returns R. Its positive stationary pi gives pi R=gamma(ej-ei), D_B=gamma H,
and gamma=-J_native/2. The kernel H approaches the sharp bound as epsilon
vanishes. The native current also vanishes, so sharpness is not a resource-free
measurement advantage. Four epsilon values verify the stationary identity.

BROADER ETA=1 RESULT
Without a reset assumption, H>=A_a+A_b-T. A direct first-crossing survival proof
applies to any classical binary process whose conditional crossing intensities
are uniformly bounded under all histories, without a finite hidden dimension,
time homogeneity, or Markov property. Singular deterministic jumps and unbounded
crossing intensities are outside this class.

With a common cap lambda, H/T>=F(lambda T), where
F(z)=2(1-exp(-z))/z-1 decreases strictly. Its zero is 1.593624260..., defined
exactly by z=2(1-exp(-z)); rational certificates bracket it by 1.593 and 1.594.
Given a calibrated upper contrast bound c_U, every finite-hazard classical
explanation has lambda>=F^{-1}(c_U)/T. This is a minimum kinetic activity
required of an explanation, not an energy cost.

With declared caps a,b and K>0, a contrast bound implies
eta>=max(0,(G-T c_U)/K). A value exceeding one excludes the declared hazard
contract; it does not exclude every possible classical model.

MIXED PREPARATIONS
The manuscript and mixed_preparation_bound helper also handle
rho=p nu_in+(1-p)nu_out, with nu_in supported inside B and within TV eta0 of
nuB, while the outside component has entrance reset defect eta1. The sharp bound
is H >= (1-p)(G-eta1 K)-p eta0(T-A_b). The same four-state extremizer attains it
with nu_in=(1-eta0)delta_j+eta0 delta_k. Without reset assumptions the bound is
H >= A_b+(1-p)A_a-T and has the broader conditional-hazard scope. This prevents
applying the p=0 theorem to a preparation with partial detector occupancy. The
p=1 negative-bound control is included in the tests.

CERTIFICATE CODE
code/detector_boundary.py implements bounded_hazard_response,
reset_defect_lower_bound, classical_hazard_lower_bound, and
mixed_preparation_bound using exact rational interval arithmetic. Inputs are
integers, Fractions, or rational strings; floats and bools are rejected.
Exponential enclosures use Taylor sums and geometric tails. Large-root
bisection uses exp(z)>=(1+z/64)^64. The inverse helper returns a root bracket
and an explicit lower-endpoint contrast certificate. If either hazard cap is
zero, K is set exactly to (0,0), and no inverse defect division is attempted.
Graph gate preorders and contamination envelopes are separate helpers.
Numerical illustrations are isolated in make_results().

GRAPH CERTIFICATE
U_x>=U_y whenever x is inside B and all paths x->B-complement hit y, or y is
outside B and all paths y->B hit x. These primitive first-hitting comparisons
compose transitively. The resulting preorder strictly extends the original
entrance-gateway condition and includes closed detector regions.

A complete universal-support characterization was explored but not proved.
Failure of the implemented certificate does not imply that a graph is
unprotected. Preliminary four-state combinatorial searches found uncertified
graphs with no obvious disjoint first-crossing paths; that observation was not
promoted to a theorem.

FINITE CALIBRATION LIMIT
Adding transitions with total intensity at most delta gives full-record
TV<=delta L for total pathwise horizon L, including adapted controls preserving
the intensity bound. Arbitrarily weak hidden bypasses cannot be uniformly
excluded with finite evidence.

An explicit family has i->k at rate one and j->l at delta, with B={j,k}.
At delta=0, H=1-exp(-T)>0 for all positive T. At any delta>0,
H=A_delta+A_1-T eventually becomes negative. Records nevertheless approach
those of the protected model on every fixed bounded horizon as delta tends to
zero. A structural assumption, tolerance, or separation gap is therefore needed.

LIMITATIONS
Hazard caps must hold at every relevant hidden state or history, not merely for
observed mean rates. Dissipative Lindblad rates cannot be substituted for an
arbitrary classical hazard cap. The direct preparation comparison is free of
stationarity assumptions; using a stationary pulse response requires separate
forcing calibration and valid interval division by gamma. Reset defect does not
necessarily indicate temporal memory or quantum coherence. No thermodynamic
energy floor follows from a hazard lower bound alone. Physical validation,
historical priority assessment, and external review remain unresolved.

SHARP CLASSICAL STATE-COUNT COROLLARY
A new independent check confirms the root's structural corollary: a negative
uniform integrated contrast between preparations supported inside and outside
a deterministic binary detector requires at least four states in any common
finite homogeneous CTMC. With at most three states, B or its complement is a
singleton, so the first-hitting theorem (directly or after complementing) gives
strict positivity. The four-state eta=1 extremizer attains every scalar contrast
in (-1,0) at a chosen horizon. This is not a claim about reproducing the entire
quantum record with four states. A qubit with Hamiltonian gX and orthogonal
basis preparations has contrast sin(2gT)/(2gT), negative for suitable T.

A conservative robustness extension permits a fixed stochastic detector
f(x) in [0,1] and arbitrary preparations. Every <=3-state homogeneous model
satisfies C_f>=m_B-m_A-1, with m_B and m_A the initial detector means. Proof:
apply the singleton result after conditioning on threshold detector regions,
bound purification error by their initial impurity, and integrate the layer-cake
representation of f. Simultaneous initial-mean bounds therefore give an
experimentally usable state-count exclusion without exact support assumptions.

Time homogeneity is necessary: on three states, j->k at rate10 for duration0.1,
then i->j at rate10 for duration0.1, then a frozen generator for duration1 gives
H(1.2)=1.9/e-0.9<0. The code checks this counterexample. The classical claim
concerns autonomous common generators, not arbitrary hidden clocks or histories.

API: three_state_exclusion(contrast_upper, initial_inside_lower=1,
initial_outside_upper=0). Its confidence and calibration coverage must come
from external valid intervals. It reports a >=4-state requirement only when the
provided bounds exclude the <=3-state class. Two added tests check 200 random
two-/three-state models, stochastic detector/preparation bounds, and the
time-dependent failure case. Full proofs appear in reset_boundary.tex.

DIRECT OBSERVABLE FORM AND FROZEN INTERFACE
Swapping preparation labels gives the symmetric form |C0-C_uniform|<=1 for any
fixed stochastic readout and arbitrary preparations in a <=3-state homogeneous
model. This form is the primary directly observable dimension witness: exact
hidden preparation supports need not be certified. The bound is sharp as a
supremum. A three-state model with a reference absorbing state of response c,
a comparison state of response0, and a rate-lambda jump to an absorbing state
of response1 has score1-A_lambda(T)/T, approaching1. The more specific impurity
form is not claimed tight at every separately specified pair of initial means.

A common deterministic or independently randomized delay before every baseline
and uniform observation only changes the effective preparations and is allowed.
Readout stability is essential: reversing binary labels between baseline and
readout lets two frozen states produce score2. The time-dependent-generator
counterexample remains a separate essential control. For a unital qubit with
orthogonal preparations, the four-state equal-hazard example matches both
averaged arm probabilities after discarding timestamps. It does not match the
full time-resolved process. Source, tests, results, and manuscript are frozen
for parent integration; the owned suite remains16 tests.

CLOCK EXTENSION
The arbitrary-preparation dimension bound also holds for every independent
nonincreasing clock density starting at zero. Such clocks are mixtures of
uniform clocks, and |C0-C_w|<=integral |C0-C_uniform(T)| mu(dT)<=1. A common
random latency is independent of the clock draw as well as of the physical
trajectory. Trajectory-dependent stopping is not covered. No further source
mutations are planned after this integration note.
