Spectral dynamics: evidence and reproducibility record

Question
Can a direct physical dissipation inequality substantially improve the sixth
edition's record-divergence/Pinsker comparison without assuming a hidden graph?

The initial infinitesimal curvature candidate was not promoted. Although
C''(0) = ||Sf||² - ||Af||² is immediate, its conversion to entropy requires
additional kinetic information and derivative estimation. A finite-lag
transition-operator identity provided a more useful route.

Conditional theorem
For a stationary homogeneous Markov semigroup with ordinary physical reversal
of the complete hidden state, a real detector f of known range R, and real s,
Delta = -s² C(0) + 2s C(t) - C(2t)
      = ||Bf||² - ||(H-sI)f||²,
where H = (P_t + P_t†)/2 and B = (P_t - P_t†)/2.
With u = 4[Delta]+/R², conditional total variation, the sharp half-Jeffreys
inequality and Jensen give endpoint KL >= Phi(u), where
Phi(u) = 2 sqrt(u) atanh(sqrt(u)). Path KL = t sigma then implies
sigma >= Phi(u)/t >= 8[Delta]+/(R² t).
No hidden dimension or rate bound is needed. This does not establish the exact
minimum over all compatible continuous-time physical models.

Finite-confidence integration
The existing joint count confidence event gives 2g <= [Delta]+ with g >= 0.
Thus u = 8g/R² and sigma >= Phi(u)/t >= 16g/(R² t). For R = 2, the linear
bound is 4g/t. Analog squared-readout calibration is conservative because
B_obs >= E(f²) and its coefficient is -s². No additional alpha is used.
The numerical bridge uses rational positive series and a rigorous tail bound.
For u >= 1 it flags finite-entropy incompatibility on the coverage event,
retains the valid linear inequality and does not clip or interpret a possible
confidence failure as empirical model refutation.

Sharpness and its limit
A discrete-time four-cycle with forward probability (1+z)/2, backward
probability (1-z)/2, f = (1,1,-1,-1), and s = 0 gives Delta = z² and endpoint
KL = 2z atanh(z), saturating the nonlinear endpoint theorem exactly.
Its determinant is -z² < 0, whereas det(exp(tQ)) > 0 for a finite CTMC.
It proves endpoint sharpness, not CTMC rate sharpness. The realization gap
remains explicit. The physical_minimum agent's ring theorem addresses a
separate, architecture-restricted optimization.

Extensions
A bounded map F into a real Hilbert space works with its joint image diameter R
and C(t) = E <F(X_0), F(X_t)>. The difference of kernel expectations is TV times
a difference of barycenters, of norm at most R. The unit-circle detector
(cos(theta), sin(theta)) doubles the cosine-only defect with the same diameter
two; it changes experimental access and requires counting both detector
channels. No new physical vector-detector performance is claimed.
The diffusion extension requires a justified ordinary forward/reverse path
identity KL = t sigma. This is not the earlier additive-reversible comparison
with path KL = t sigma/4.

Counterexamples retained
1. A nonstationary preparation of a reversible two-state generator gives a
positive defect despite zero stationary entropy production.
2. An equilibrium inertial oscillator can give positive bounded-position
defects while velocity is odd under physical time reversal.
3. Circle drift-diffusion is generalized reversible under theta -> -theta,
although the even observable cos(theta) can have a positive defect. An even
retained observable alone does not establish ordinary full-state reversal.
4. The sixth edition's invalid-calibration control still produces false
certificates. A stronger mathematical implication cannot repair a wrong
measurement contract.
