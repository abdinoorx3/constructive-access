RESULTS AND SCOPE

1. The pairwise absolute-error lower bound is derived by integrating the common
   part of two record laws. A declared resource frontier yields a minimax lower
   bound without interchanging infimum and supremum. A common policy and common
   estimator/randomization are mandatory. Infinite risk follows from arbitrarily
   separated finite invisible pairs, not an undefined infinity-times-zero term.

2. The linear target criterion explicitly requires both law factorization and
   access sufficiency. Its known-F, full positive-orthant, full Cartesian-box
   minimax equality is proved using a common-observation two-point construction
   and ordinary LP strong duality. Zero radii are allowed. An independently
   checkable primal/dual equality certificate is distinguished from the solver.
   Bounded domains, unknown selectivity, local compatible-set radius, and
   correlated errors are stated as different recovery problems.

3. Known-law iid affinity provides an equal-prior average-error guarantee.
   A robust bounded-score gap provides uniform per-hypothesis concentration.
   Its conditional-mean extension uses Hoeffding's conditional mgf lemma and
   does not silently assume independent repeated samples. Fixed nuisance values
   remain fixed. One-record convex-hull overlap is not a general impossibility
   for repeated fixed-parameter experiments.

4. Classical calibration covers full next-state/output kernels and uses a
   maximal coupling bound; quantum calibration uses physical outcome-retaining
   channels and half-diamond norms with an additive hybrid argument. Sensor
   marginals or arbitrary signed-matrix norms do not establish these contracts.
   Whole-dataset and per-record error radii are explicitly distinguished.
   Cross-chapter review by unified_foundations requested explicit radii in [0,1]
   for the multiplicative expression; this premise was added.

5. The finite-horizon Bellman recurrence uses target-class stopping loss,
   fixed nuisance indexing, pathwise charges and grammar. The independent
   reviewer requested strictly positive priors when feasibility must hold
   for every candidate; this condition was added. Finite exact search is not
   claimed efficient in dimension or universally energy-optimal.
   Cross-chapter review also requested explicit nonnegative classical kernels
   and action normalization for the row-mass recurrence; both were added.

6. Anytime adequacy uses signed endpoint bets with valid conditional forecasts.
   One fixed correctly targeted candidate gives an alpha bound on the union of
   ever-empty survivors and ever-wrong commitment, without multiplying alpha
   by the candidate count. Protecting every valid candidate is a separate
   simultaneous statement. No guarantee of power or eventual commitment is
   implied; truth outside the family can produce wrong commitments.

7. Calibration is composed by stopping wealth before the first predictable
   invalid forecast, not by conditioning on an eventual good event. The
   counting-process energy bound controls the joint event N_tau>=n,A_tau<=mu;
   this allows a terminal resource premise on the same good event without
   claiming it is predictable. Gross work, native channel work, total apparatus
   work, expected expenditure, and high-probability caps are distinguished.

8. The final handoff corollary composes calibrated score separation, finite
   record count and a physical counting-channel cap into an alpha+beta+delta
   guarantee. Its proof replaces scores by deterministic endpoints after the
   first invalid conditional mean, yielding a valid comparison sequence that
   equals the executed sequence on the joint good event. No independence
   between calibration, inference and energy is used.
