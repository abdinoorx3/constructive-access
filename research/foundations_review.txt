Proof and scope checks
1. Target factorization is explicitly set-theoretic: no measurable inverse,
   stability, or efficient computation inferred.
2. Access refinement uses a candidate-independent simulation kernel. Resource
   frontiers are ordered only when simulation preserves the declared budget.
3. Compatible-target half-diameter is a data-local deterministic minimax
   statement for real estimates. Coverage is a separate premise; empty and
   unbounded target sets are handled explicitly.
4. Finite target suites assume finite candidates, finite alphabets, allowed
   fresh preparations, and conditional independence under a fixed candidate.
   Finite-error implementation is delegated to workflow:suite.
5. Finite effect-span proof handles beta=0 separately and makes the d-1
   bound conditional on beta nonzero. Quantum signed representations are
   not treated as classically stochastic.
6. Grammar lift masks inadmissible strings and is not a normalized physical
   instrument. Actual experiments stop only in accepting grammar states.
7. Priority closure has explicit rank termination and lexicographic cost
   filtration, including zero-cost actions. Certificate checking does not
   require a span solver or linear independence.
8. Strict-cutpoint reduction is for an unbounded one-terminal-readout grammar
   without repeated reset access. Its source is established undecidability,
   not a new physics theorem. No CTMC embeddability claim is made.
9. Finite-dimensional algorithmic bounds are stated in representation size,
   not qubit count or record count. Rational parameter-family quantifier
   elimination is an in-principle route, not an implemented general solver.
