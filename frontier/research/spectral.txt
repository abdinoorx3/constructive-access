FINITE RANDOM-CLOCK CERTIFICATES FOR CLASSICAL REALIZATION DIMENSION
Abdinoor / 2D Labs — research record, 2026-10-12 UTC

Status: complete producer proof and implementation; independent internal review
completed for both analytic bounds. No laboratory data, historical priority claim, or new physical law.

Question and pivot
The exact CTMC spectral wedge and its application to observable quantum dynamics
are established. Wolf and Pérez-García (2009) already obtain arbitrarily large
classical/quantum realization gaps. An oscillatory fit to noisy finite data does
not establish the existence of an exact pole, especially with unrestricted hidden
rates and modes. This branch replaces a fitted pole by finitely many directly
sampled bounded probabilities at independent Erlang clocks. The resulting bound
has a small but strictly positive error tolerance, without an exit-rate cap.

Classical contract
A finite, time-homogeneous, conservative row generator Q has at most n states.
Every trial has the same unknown initial probability row rho and fixed unknown
binary response vector f in [0,1]^n. The random sampling time is independent of
the physical process, and neither its choice nor its phase changes Q, rho, or f.
The hypothesis includes reducibility, nonnormality, defective matrices and noisy
fixed readout. It excludes uncontrolled time-dependent driving, correlated clock
noise, drifting preparations/readout, and uncounted dynamical hidden states.
The independent random clock is an experimental control and is not coupled to Q.

Finite-moment theorem: elementary bound
Fix s>0 and let T_k be Erlang(shape k, rate s), T_0=0. Define
  m_k = E[rho exp(Q T_k) f] = rho R^k f,
  R = s(sI-Q)^(-1).
R is stochastic. Its characteristic polynomial P has real coefficients, degree
n, P(1)=0, coefficient l1 norm <=2^n, and P(R)=0. Smaller models can be padded
by unreachable absorbing states to use the same n.

For a damped qubit, initialized +Z with fixed +Z readout and Hamiltonian omega X/2,
with isotropic depolarization gamma,
  m*_k = (1+Re(mu^k))/2,
  mu=s/(s+gamma-i omega)=u+i v, v>0.
Only settings k=0,...,n+1 are used. All target probabilities are rational when
gamma, omega and s are rational.

Established CTMC sector bound:
  |Im lambda| <= -Re lambda cot(pi/n).
Put t=tan(pi/n). Every resolvent eigenvalue z satisfies
  |z|^2-Re z+t |Im z| <= 0.
For any certified 0<t0<=t, all these z therefore lie in the disk with center
1/2-i t0/2 and radius sqrt(1+t0^2)/2. If gamma/omega<t0, mu is outside this disk.
Let d be any positive lower bound on its distance to that disk, reduced to at
most v. Let k=floor((n-1)/2), and K=sqrt(1+(1+u)^2/v^2). Then
  max_{0<=j<=n+1} |m_j-m*_j| >=
  |1-mu| d^k v^(n-1-k) / [2^(n+1) K] =: Delta_n > 0.

Proof
For error epsilon=max_j|m_j-m*_j| and C=||coeff(P)||_1, Cayley-Hamilton at shifts
0 and 1 gives |Re P(mu)|<=2 epsilon C and |Re(mu P(mu))|<=2 epsilon C.
Writing P(mu)=A+iB implies |B|<=2 epsilon C(1+u)/v, hence |P(mu)|<=2 epsilon C K.
The characteristic polynomial has a root at 1. Each other real eigenvalue is in
(0,1], so its factor has modulus >=v. Nonreal eigenvalues occur in conjugate pairs:
the upper root has distance >=d and its lower conjugate has distance >=v. Since
d<=v, the smallest guaranteed product uses the maximum possible number k of
pairs. Thus |P(mu)|>=|1-mu| d^k v^(n-1-k). Finally C<=product(1+|z_j|)<=2^n.
Combining gives the theorem. Neither diagonalization nor exact rank estimation is
used. The positive finite gap follows from a known spectral obstruction but does
not assume observed finite data determine an exact spectrum.

Normalized strengthening (independently reviewed)
Every resolvent eigenvalue has positive real part. The real-linear characteristic
factors and conjugate-quadratic factors therefore have alternating coefficients;
products preserve their signs. Hence C=||coeff(P)||_1=|P(-1)| exactly.
Set nu=(1-mu)/(1+mu)=a-i b, b>0. For each resolvent eigenvalue z,
  w=(1-z)/(1+z)=1-2s/(2s-lambda)
lies in the same lens, which is invariant under z -> 1-z. Let d_C<=b be a
positive certified distance of nu from this lens (use the reflected bounding disk).
The normalized factor satisfies
  |mu-z|/|1+z| = |1+mu| |nu-w|/2.
A real root gives factor at least r_0=v/|1+mu|. A conjugate pair gives at least
  h = |1+mu|^2 d_C(2b-d_C)/4 <= r_0^2.
To prove the pair inequality, write |Im w|=y. If y>=b-d_C, the near-root factor
is >=d_C and the far-root factor >=b+y>=2b-d_C. If y<=b-d_C, their product is
at least (b-y)(b+y)>=d_C(2b-d_C). The special root z=1 contributes |1-mu|/2.
Using as many conjugate pairs q=floor((n-1)/2) as possible and dividing the two
moment residuals by C yields the stronger explicit gap
  Delta_n = |1-mu| r_0^(n-1-2q) h^q / (4K).
The code computes both bounds and keeps the larger certified value. The new
proof assumes no additional access or rate calibration. Directed rounding of
r_0 and h is separate; the inequality h<=r_0^2 refers to their exact values.

Exact arithmetic
Machin's identity pi=16 atan(1/5)-4 atan(1/239), alternating-series enclosures,
and tan x>=x+x^3/3 supply a rational lower t0. For n=4 use t0=1 exactly. The tan
inequality follows from tan'>=1, then tan'>=1+x^2 for x>=0. Square-root bounds use
integer square roots on a dyadic grid. If F=|mu-c|^2-radius^2>0, the circle distance
is F/(|mu-c|+radius); upper bounds in the denominator give a certified lower bound.
Internal downward rounding is explicit; no binary floating-point datum enters a
certificate. Floating-point outputs are labelled decimal displays only.

Bounded clocks and records
An Erlang clock has infinite support. To obtain a genuinely bounded laboratory
schedule, if T_k>L the experiment returns the artificial outcome zero and stops
without waiting beyond L. The difference from the ideal moment is <=
  Pr(T_k>L)=exp(-sL) sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(sL)^j/j!.
The implementation bounds exp(-x) above by the reciprocal of a positive partial
sum of exp(x). This changes no assumptions about hidden exit rates.
For each setting use N independent Bernoulli trials. A simultaneous Hoeffding
radius r has failure probability <=2(n+2)exp(-2Nr^2). For a predeclared nominal
target, reject every <=n-state model only when
  max_j |mhat_j-m*_j| + r + calibration_radius + tail_radius < Delta_n.
Calibration and truncation errors belong in this inequality. Parameters fitted
after seeing the same data require a separate uniform/held-out correction.

For the supplied bounded_design, r=Delta_n/4 and tails <=Delta_n/8. With zero
unmodelled calibration error, both false rejection of the classical null and
missed detection of the nominal target are <=alpha. This follows because under
the nominal target the observed deviation is <=r+tail on the concentration event,
so the test's total distance <=2r+2tail<Delta_n. A common calibration bound c must
satisfy 2c+2r+2tail<Delta_n for the same power guarantee.

Controls and limitations
A 4-state directed cycle at rate 2, with fixed stochastic detector
f=(1,1/2,0,1/2), exactly realizes (1+exp(-2t)cos(2t))/2. The certificate correctly
fails at gamma=omega. More generally finite classical cyclic realizations exist
for every strictly damped sinusoid with sufficiently many states, so the result
never establishes absolute classical impossibility. The γ=0 limit is different.
Clock distribution, common generator, preparation consistency and readout
consistency are essential. Probability-only observations exclude those clocks'
full time-tagged records from this theorem; retaining more data can improve a
test but is not assumed. Record cost is not a thermodynamic energy lower bound.
