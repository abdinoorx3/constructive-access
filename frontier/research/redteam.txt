1. Robust positive-factorization hierarchy

Let M_ij = alpha_i b_j, with alpha_i nonnegative normalized row vectors on n
states and b_j in [0,1]^n. Assume M_ii <= epsilon and M_ij >= beta for i != j.
For 0<tau<1 set S_i={k:b_i(k)<tau}, the strict sublevel set of the ith
detector column. Markov's inequality yields
alpha_i(S_i complement)<=epsilon/tau. If S_i is contained in S_j, then
M_ij <= tau+(1-tau)epsilon/tau. Therefore beta exceeding this expression
forces all S_i to be an antichain. Sperner's theorem gives
N <= binomial(n,floor(n/2)). The optimal real threshold tau=sqrt(epsilon)
gives 2sqrt(epsilon)-epsilon. Strict inequality matters at the boundary.
For epsilon=0, exact zero supports give the corresponding exact-support proof.

This support argument is established at zero noise. The analytic nonzero
margin, one-common-semigroup temporal implementation, and finite-count
inference are the narrowly scoped contributions to assess. Exact same
sin-squared quantum probability matrices occur in prior work; there is no
first ideal quantum/classical separation claim.

Sharpness in the general factorization class: take all floor(n/2)-element
subsets S_i, alpha_i uniform on S_i, b_j the indicator of its complement.
Then M_ii=0 and every offdiagonal entry is at least 1/floor(n/2), attaining the
central-binomial count. This does not prove attainability under a single
common homogeneous generator.

Temporal use requires M_ij=alpha exp(Q t_i) exp(Q s_j)f with t_i,s_j>=0.
Unknown fixed preparation and stochastic fixed readout are allowed; arbitrary
time-dependent dynamics, detector drift, or hidden external control memory
are not covered. Calibration bias must be paid on both null and alternative.
The intersection-union test has type-I error bounded by the maximum component
type-I error because every classical null violates at least one component.
Alternative missed detections use a union bound across settings. This is not
an omitted multiple-comparison correction.

2. Finite Erlang-clock spectral certificate

For R=s(sI-Q)^(-1), R is stochastic. A fixed preparation/readout produces
m_k=alpha R^k f. The characteristic polynomial P of an n-dimensional R
annihilates two consecutive length-(n+1) windows and has root 1 and coefficient
L1 norm at most 2^n. The target moments are (1+Re(mu^k))/2. If every moment
differs by at most epsilon, then |Re P(mu)| and |Re(mu P(mu))| are both at
most 2 epsilon ||P||_1. Writing mu=u+iv recovers
|P(mu)| <= 2 epsilon ||P||_1 sqrt(1+(1+|u|)^2/v^2).

The established n-state generator sector maps into the intersection of two
disks described by |z|^2-Re z+tan(pi/n)|Im z|<=0. A rational lower bound on
tan(pi/n) gives a valid larger disk. If mu lies outside it, the radial disk
distance is a rigorous lower bound d on the distance to every allowable
resolvent eigenvalue; replace d by min(d,v). Of the n-1 roots other than a
selected root 1, real roots are at distance at least v. Each conjugate pair
contributes at least d*v because its lower-half-plane member is at distance
at least v. At most floor((n-1)/2) pairs therefore give the producer's bound
|P(mu)| >= |1-mu| d^floor((n-1)/2) v^(n-1-floor((n-1)/2)).
The denominator and strict finite-error exclusion criterion are correct.
Models of dimension smaller than n can be padded by inaccessible absorbing
states, so the theorem really excludes every dimension at most n.

The final producer implements a stronger normalization. Every resolvent root
has positive real part. Its real-linear and conjugate-quadratic characteristic
factors therefore have alternating coefficients, as does their product, so
||P||_1=|P(-1)|. Under w=(1-z)/(1+z), eigenvalues become 1 minus eigenvalues
of R(2s); the spectral lens is invariant under z -> 1-z. For target
nu=(1-mu)/(1+mu)=a-ib, the exact normalized factor is
|mu-z|/|1+z|=|1+mu| |nu-w|/2. Real roots contribute at least
r=v/|1+mu|. If d<=b bounds the target-to-lens distance, a conjugate pair
contributes at least h=|1+mu|^2 d(2b-d)/4. Indeed, for Im(w)=y>=0, either
y>=b-d and the two factors are at least d and 2b-d, or y<b-d and their
product is at least b^2-y^2>d(2b-d). Since h<=r^2, the worst case uses
q=floor((n-1)/2) pairs. The selected root 1 contributes |1-mu|/2.
After normalizing the two residual equations this yields
epsilon >= |1-mu| r^(n-1-2q) h^q/(4 sqrt(1+(1+u)^2/v^2)).
This improvement has been independently algebraically reviewed and tested;
the preceding unnormalized argument remains a valid weaker baseline.

Clocks must be independent of current hidden evolution. Erlang trials are
fresh prepare/wait/read experiments. Encoding the outcome as zero when the
independent clock exceeds L changes its Bernoulli mean by at most the clock
tail; this is safe without a hidden rate cap. The Hoeffding plan is highly
conservative and can demand billions of records even for exclusion of four
states. A finite certificate is not necessarily an efficient experiment.

Exact classical counterexample to broad quantumness claims: an even-N cycle
with clockwise/counterclockwise rates a,b satisfying
(a+b)(1-cos(2pi/N))=gamma and (a-b)sin(2pi/N)=omega has nonnegative rates iff
gamma/omega>=tan(pi/N). Detector f_k=(1+cos(2pi k/N))/2 and preparations 0,N/2
realize the complementary damped-qubit population traces exactly. This
construction was independently checked. Finite damping does not exclude all
finite classical models; exact spectral separation bounds dimension.

3. Finite-window reversible approximation and the unrestricted null

The Poisson clock construction is a physical state-space realization of the
established Szasz-Mirakjan approximation operator. For a trace p with Lipschitz
constant L on [0,T], a Poisson clock of rate r with clipped/frozen phase gives
|E p(clip(N_rt/r,0,T))-p(t)| <= L sqrt(t/r). Its finite state count must include
preparation sectors and detector-bit states. The smooth O(1/r) variant needs a
global second-derivative bound on t>=0 because it uses samples beyond T.

For any at-most-N adaptive fresh trials with waits in [0,T], a uniform
per-trial Bernoulli error epsilon gives transcript TV at most
1-(1-epsilon)^N, including retained timestamps. This follows by a sequential
coupling along coincident histories; adaptation to previous records causes no
problem. Continuous observation, invasive intermediate instruments, or one
fixed simulator valid at arbitrarily long times are different claims.

A small reverse phase rate d makes the phase process reversible. The lifted
deterministic binary readout has exact weights pi_(a,k,y) proportional to
(r/d)^k times the sampled Bernoulli probability of y. Every forward/backward
edge satisfies detailed balance exactly, including deleted zero-weight states.
Before the first backward event, the forward and reversible clocks agree, so
the added readout error is at most 1-exp(-dT)<=dT. Zero stationary entropy
production does not mean a free experiment: the initial phase preparation has
large relative entropy from equilibrium and can dissipate during relaxation.

Additional independent strengthening: disconnected sectors are unnecessary.
Given any stationary pi with all retained state weights positive, add
lambda(1*pi-I) to Q. This preserves detailed balance and makes the complete
chain irreducible. The transition law is exactly
exp(-lambda t) exp(Qt)+(1-exp(-lambda t)) 1*pi,
so each binary readout changes by at most 1-exp(-lambda T). Irreducibility
alone does not rescue finite-record transient identifiability.

Consequently, for this restricted finite fresh-trial observation class, a test
uniformly level alpha over all finite reversible transient classical models
cannot have rejection probability above alpha for a continuous target trace
in their operational closure. One must state a resource, stationarity,
preparation, instrument, or other substantive restriction before claiming an
unrestricted classical exclusion. This is an operational no-go, not a
universal classical simulation of quantum mechanics.

4. Stationary correlation and entropy-production certificates

For an ordinary even-state finite CTMC, stationary pi, and stationary reversal
Q*, the row escape rates of Q,Q*,S=(Q+Q*)/2 agree. Pairing stationary edge
flows a=pi_i q_ij,b=pi_j q_ji gives
D(path Q || path S)/t = sum_edges[a log(2a/(a+b))+b log(2b/(a+b))]
                         <= sigma/4,
where sigma=sum_edges(a-b)log(a/b). The constant is sharp near equilibrium.
This comparison and its underlying divergence inequality have prior art.

For the same fixed noninvasive binary detector, write f_i=E[Y|i] in [-1,1].
Independent endpoint detector noise gives C_S(t)=<f,exp(tS)f>_pi. Reversibility
implies a nonnegative spectral mixture of decaying real exponentials. At
positive integer lags k Delta, the same-outcome probabilities are
q_k=r+(1-r) integral_[0,1] x^k dmu(x), r=(1+(pi f)^2)/2. Missing spectral mass
for a noisy detector may be placed at x=0. The closure includes x=0 and is a
safe comparison class; no infinite exit rate needs to be physically attained.

For a randomized lag law w and positive scores h_k(y), the variational KL
bound is E_P log h - log(E_R h). Supremizing E_R h over the entire reversible
moment cone reduces to the maximum of one polynomial in x and, if r is
uncertain, its two endpoint values. Rational Bernstein upper bounds therefore
give a checkable lower bound J on record KL. Data processing implies
sigma E[lag]>=4J. Simultaneous probability intervals allow scores and weights
to be chosen after constructing those intervals, because the deterministic
certificate minimizes over the whole confidence box. Pointwise confidence
intervals selected after looking at data are not interchangeable with this.

The moment cone is stronger than nonnegative/monotone autocorrelation: it
also enforces complete monotonicity and Hausdorff moment constraints. A
positive decreasing but locally concave correlation can therefore yield a
positive certificate missed by a negative-correlation or monotonicity check.

Essential physical exclusions: equilibrium underdamped systems with odd
time-reversal variables can oscillate at zero dissipation; ordinary even-state
reversal cannot be silently imposed on them. Quantum coherent dynamics also
need not obey the classical reversible spectral cone. The certificate is a
cost of an admissible classical explanation, not unconditional measured heat.
Conversion to heat or power requires appropriate local detailed balance and
thermal accounting. Stationarity and noninvasive fixed observation are
substantive physical assumptions, not supplied by a fitted correlation curve.

5. Nonstationary resource alternative

For initial nu and full-support stationary pi, compare Q started at nu with S
started at pi. The path KL chain rule and equal row escapes give
D(path Q_nu || path S_pi)=D(nu||pi)+integral nu_s(i) sum_j q_ij log(q_ij/S_ij) ds.
Log concavity yields rowwise
sum_j q_ij log(q_ij/S_ij)<=1/2 sum_j q_ij log(q_ij/q*_ij).
The latter is a nonnegative equal-total-rate relative entropy in each row.
It is the ordinary-reversal housekeeping entropy rate sigma_hk(nu_s).
Therefore the same reversible record separation J implies
J<=D(nu||pi)+(1/2) E_lag integral_0^lag sigma_hk(nu_s) ds.
Under stationary initialization the sharper coefficient 1/4 applies. Under
detailed balance, housekeeping cost vanishes and J<=D(nu||pi).

Critical calibration issue identified in review: a transient observed mean
does not determine pi f. The nonstationary frontier must allow the complete
r interval [1/2,1], unless the reference stationary mean is independently
calibrated. The root implementation explicitly guards this distinction.

This creates a coherent preparation-versus-maintenance resource statement,
not a universal conservation law. The reversible clock no-go and positive
stationary dissipation certificate are consistent because they pay for the
observed behavior through different initial-state and sustained resources.

Remaining limitations and release recommendations

- Retain exact null classes in every abstract, figure caption, and example.
- Do not equate a dimension witness with all-classical exclusion or heat.
- Do not call inherited spectral/nonnegative-rank facts novel.
- The endpoint finite-count dual and positive-noise hierarchy are candidate
  methodological extensions; priority and external review remain unresolved.
- Synthetic confidence boxes and quantum models are not laboratory evidence.
- Report large sufficient state/sample costs and matched baseline failures.
- Preserve the transient reference-mean guard and negative controls for odd
  variables, time-varying dynamics, and missing stationarity.
