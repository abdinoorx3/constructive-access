1. QUESTION AND ACCESS CONTRACT

Can increasingly accurate prepare-wait-read data exclude every finite classical
Markov explanation, without an independent state-size, rate, or preparation
restriction? The answer is no. This remains true when precise timestamps are
retained and waiting schedules adapt to previous records. It also remains true
when the classical explanation must obey detailed balance at stationarity.

The experimental contract consists of finitely many preparations a=1,...,m;
one common time-homogeneous evolution between preparation and readout; one fixed
binary detector; and a fresh preparation/reset before each record. In a record,
the arm and waiting time are chosen before that record's hidden evolution and
independently of its subsequent noise. The schedule can depend on all earlier
records. Intermediate interventions, continuous monitoring, known microscopic
preparation relations beyond the listed distributions, and independently
measured energy/resource observables are not included.

2. CONSTRUCTIVE LIPSCHITZ APPROXIMATION

Let p_a:[0,T]->[0,1] have a common Lipschitz constant L. Fix r>0 and K=ceil(rT).
Define f_a(k)=p_a(min(k/r,T)). A pure-birth phase process k=0,...,K starts at0,
jumps k->k+1 at rate r, and freezes at K. Its random phase is min(Poisson(rt),K).

A stochastic detector of mean f_a(k) would suffice. To obtain a deterministic
detector, lift the state to (a,k,y), y in{0,1}, set detector value y, and at each
phase jump draw y' according to Bernoulli(f_a(k+1)). The initial distribution
draws y according to Bernoulli(f_a(0)). Every preparation uses its own counted
branch, while the block-diagonal generator and detector are common. The state
bound is 2m(K+1), including all clock and preparation-label memory. Zero-weight
bit states may be deleted.

The measured mean is q_a(t)=E[p_a(min(N_rt/r,T))]. Projection onto [0,T] is
nonexpansive and t lies in that interval. Therefore
  |q_a(t)-p_a(t)| <= L E|N_rt/r-t| <= L sqrt(t/r) <= L sqrt(T/r).
For a target error epsilon, r=L^2 T/epsilon^2 and K=ceil(rT) suffice. Sampling
the p_a values with uniform error eta adds at most eta to the output error.
The construction accommodates arbitrary endpoints and ideal separated-support
preparations when p_a(0) equals0 or1.

3. SMOOTH IMPROVEMENT AND EXACT TRUNCATION CERTIFICATES

If p_a:[0,infinity)->[0,1] is twice differentiable with global |p_a''|<=M, then
the untruncated Poisson operator has error <=M t/(2r). Taylor's linear term
cancels because E[N_rt/r]=t and its variance is t/r. Freezing at K changes the
mean by at most Pr(Poisson(rT)>=K). Hence
  sup_{a,t<=T}|q_a(t)-p_a(t)| <= M T/(2r)+Poisson_tail(rT,K).
The global bound and the extra sample times beyond T matter here. A merely
local second-derivative bound cannot justify the untruncated Taylor argument.
For any finite-dimensional Lindblad semigroup with a fixed binary effect E,
p_a(t)=Tr[E exp(t L)(rho_a)] satisfies the all-time bounds
Lipschitz<=||L^*(E)||_infinity and curvature<=||(L^*)^2(E)||_infinity,
because the evolved density matrix remains positive of trace one. Thus the
construction covers every finite set of preparations of such a semigroup.
No classical generator parameters are identified with the Lindblad damping
rates; the constructed classical clock generally uses additional resources.

For N~Poisson(mu), any integer s<=K gives
  Pr(N>=K) <= mu^s/(K)_s.
The implementation multiplies factors mu/(K-j+1) only while they are <=1,
which gives the best bound in this factorial-moment family using exact
rational arithmetic. For enormous clock dimensions, a cheaper Cantelli bound
  Pr(N>=K) <= mu/[mu+(K-mu)^2], K>mu,
has an exact rational certificate and sufficient K found by integer square
root. Neither tail bound is asserted to be optimal.

4. DETAILED-BALANCE REALIZATION AND PREPARATION COST

Replace the one-way clock by a birth-death chain with forward rate r and
backward rate d>0. Its stationary phase distribution is pi_k=R^k/Z, R=r/d,
Z=sum_{k=0}^K R^k. At either direction of a phase jump, resample the detector
bit from its destination-phase law. Its unnormalized stationary weights are
  pi(a,k,y)=(1/m) (R^k/Z) Bernoulli_{f_a(k)}(y).
For neighboring phases k,l,
  pi(a,k,y) q_kl f_a(l,y') = pi(a,l,y') q_lk f_a(k,y),
so the deterministic-detector lift satisfies detailed balance exactly.
Stationary entropy production is zero. Actual transient entropy production
can be positive: the initial state is far from stationary equilibrium.

Couple its forward jumps with the one-way process until the first backward
jump. The latter event has probability <=1-exp(-dT)<=dT. Thus the reversible
model adds at most dT to the uniform output error. Initial preparation in
branch a and phase0 has exactly
  D(nu_a || pi)=log(mZ).
For an isothermal detailed-balance physical realization, k_B Theta times this
quantity is the nonequilibrium free-energy difference from the specified
equilibrium reference; under suitable reversible preparation controls it is
the associated lower work cost from equilibrium. It is not an unavoidable
cost assigned to every already-given initial state. If the preparation label
is conditioned on rather than included in an equilibrium reference, the
within-branch divergence is log Z.

The construction above has m disconnected reversible components. If a single
irreducible classical generator is required, add c(1 pi-I), c>0, on the retained
positive-weight states. This connects all states, preserves the same detailed
balance law and adds at most cT to the output error by a reset-event coupling.
Thus the closure conclusion is not an artifact of absorbing states or
reducibility. All dimension counts include clock states. Small backward rates
can require steep equilibrium energy differences; zero stationary EPR does
not imply a free measurement device or free repeated reset.

5. FINITE TRANSCRIPTS AND A UNIFORM-TEST NO-GO

For a protocol using at most n fresh records, maximal coupling of each next
Bernoulli outcome conditional on an as-yet identical history gives
  TV(P_quantum, P_classical) <= 1-(1-epsilon)^n <= n epsilon.
The arm/time-selection randomness can be shared; timestamps remain in the
joint transcript. Adaptive schedules and bounded stopping do not change this
argument. Detector-bit samples on a persistent trajectory, however, are not
fresh-reset records and need not have the reproduced joint law.

Let C be the union of all finite detailed-balance CTMC models with arbitrary
allowed finite preparation distributions, deterministic binary readout, and
no dimension/rate/preparation-energy cap. Every smooth quantum prepare-wait-
read experiment in this access contract lies in the total-variation closure
of C at every finite horizon and finite record count. Consequently any test
phi taking values in[0,1] and satisfying sup_{Q in C}E_Q phi<=alpha also obeys
E_P phi<=alpha for every such quantum law P. Proof: approximate P arbitrarily
closely in TV; expectation differences of phi are bounded by TV; take limits.

The same conclusion holds for eventual finite-time rejection by a sequential
test, including protocols without a fixed record/time horizon, provided every
individual wait is finite and the target functions are regular on each compact
window. Apply the finite theorem to the event of rejection in at most n records
with every wait <=T, then let n,T increase. The events exhaust finite rejection;
the type-I guarantee holds for each constructed classical model uniformly.
This does not assert that a single fixed countermodel mimics infinitely many
records. Models are allowed to grow along the approximating sequence, precisely
because the null imposed no resource restriction.

7. A MATCHED EXACT CYCLIC REALIZATION

For p_+(t)=[1+exp(-gamma t)cos(omega t)]/2, gamma,omega>0, an n-state biased
cycle with theta=2pi/n and rates
  r+d=gamma/(1-cos theta), r-d=omega/sin theta
has Fourier eigenvalue -gamma+i omega. The detector
f(k)=[1+cos(k theta)]/2 is a fixed stochastic binary readout, and preparation
delta_0 realizes p_+ exactly for all t>=0. Both rates are nonnegative exactly
when n atan(gamma/omega)>=pi. For an even cycle, delta_{n/2} realizes1-p_+.
The detector can be lifted to a deterministic bit by the same destination
resampling construction, now on cyclic phases. This uses2n-1 states for an
odd cycle and2n-2 for an even cycle, after deleting zero-bit weights.

Combining the established generator-sector eigenvalue bound with this cyclic
upper construction gives exact minimum63 classical states for the single
trace gamma=.1,omega=2, when fixed stochastic readout is allowed. A62-state
model cannot contain the required complex poles, and63 states suffice.
This is an exact continuum statement, not a63-state lower bound inferred from
ordinary finite experimental samples. The related spectral chapter separately
quantifies the finite-error gap, which can be impractically small. For the
pair of complementary traces,64 cycle states suffice; no proof that a
different63-state model cannot realize the pair is claimed here.

Rational Machin-formula pi and alternating arctan bounds certify that
62 atan(.05)-pi<0 while63 atan(.05)-pi>0. At n63 the numerical rates are
r=20.10533483389629, d=.018529018774543715, and the101-point trace check has
maximum numerical error1.11e-15. At n64 they are r=20.585923793175958,
d=.18132931841930322, with both complementary trace errors below8e-15.
These are checks of a proved exact-form construction, not interval certificates
for the numerical matrices. Preparation KL relative to the uniform phase law
is log n. Stationary EPR in units of k_B per unit time is(r-d)log(r/d): about
140.395 for63 and96.556 for64. No minimum-dissipation claim follows.

Every finite detailed-balance generator has real eigenvalues. Consequently
it cannot exactly realize the nonzero complex poles of this damped oscillation.
Exact agreement on an open time interval would imply agreement for all time
by analyticity. Yet reversible models approximate it arbitrarily closely on
every finite interval. Thus exact continuum exclusion of detailed balance is
not a robust finite-precision inference without additional restrictions.

8. PRIOR ART AND POSITIONING

The Poisson approximation is the established Szasz--Mirakjan operator, not a
new approximation principle. Original source: Otto Szasz, Generalization of
S. Bernstein's Polynomials to the Infinite Interval, Journal of Research of
the National Bureau of Standards45(1950),239--245,
DOI10.6028/jres.045.024, https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/045/3/v45.n03.a09.pdf.
Taylor/variance and coupling estimates, birth-death detailed balance, and
closure obstructions in statistical testing are established tools. The present
work assembles them into a constructive access boundary with explicit counted
hidden clock states, retained timestamps, reversible alternatives, preparation
free energy and finite-record certificates. Historical novelty of this exact
combination remains unestablished; the valid scientific function is to delimit
what the framework's dimension/energy witnesses can and cannot establish.
