\chapter*{Purpose and use}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Purpose and use}
A scientific apparatus preserves some distinctions and erases others. This reference develops a unified way to determine which target properties remain identifiable, construct interventions that reveal them, and connect their probability contrasts to calibrated inference and physical expenditure.

The central object is the law of the complete accessible record under a declared experiment. General results describe targets on observational equivalence classes. Finite classical and quantum instruments admit exact algebraic certificates or finite witnesses. Further layers treat continuous time, calibration uncertainty, finite-error decisions, model adequacy and resource limits. Worked examples address hidden currents, chemical channel flux, quantum error mechanisms, Ramsey sensing and inaccessible power consumption.


The seventh edition adds a direct finite-lag dissipation inequality, joint
analog acquisition, a fully covered contrast-selection library, and explicit
physical attainability and temporal-aliasing results. Chapter~\ref{pa:entry}
provides the entry route to these developments. The older bounds remain useful
for their broader contrast and access contracts; the new result strengthens
the specified three-lag case. All claims remain conditional on the declared
physical reversal, stationarity and detector assumptions.


The eighth edition uses observed local diffusion geometry to close a physical
minimum over compatible ordinary hidden diffusions of arbitrary finite
dimension for a specified family of scalar process laws. The scalar records
can themselves be reversible. A finite-resolution variational certificate,
explicit bias control and frozen same-record comparisons connect the population
theorem to statistical inference. Chapter~\ref{og:entry} introduces this layer;
its physical and measurement assumptions are essential.


The ninth edition adds an explicit detector layer: population recovery through
known exposure and noise, a finite-data obstruction for the sharp physical
minimum, an exact fixed-grid ambiguity, and matching sampling and declared
measurement-energy orders for a controlled circle comparison.
Chapter~\ref{ch:dg-entry} connects these results and distinguishes ideal
identification from physical resource requirements.


The tenth edition makes detector calibration part of the inference contract.
It combines jointly covered nuisance parameters, finite-error diffusion
certificates, an auditable virtual experiment, and exact limits on learning
global calibration constants from finite passive records.
Chapter~\ref{ch:dc-entry} separates structural assumptions from measured evidence.
A sharp hidden-drive theorem also shows how zero mean circulation and a
reversible scalar record can coexist with an attained positive dissipation
minimum over the stated compatible diffusion class.

The organizing question is what every compatible explanation must contain.
The answer may be a distinguishable mechanism, a minimum classical state count,
a preparation resource, or sustained dissipation. These requirements are
conditional on experimental access. The reference develops both constructive
certificates and countermodels that show when a stronger conclusion is impossible.

The physical development begins with an all-time finite-pulse response
constraint derived from first-passage theory. It continues through robust
preparation and detector contracts, sharp reset and crossing-intensity bounds,
and finite-record classical dimension witnesses. Graph access completion,
shared calibration nuisance, acquisition-energy design and composition make
these results usable in larger experimental workflows.

The present extension supplies a finite-data hierarchy of hidden-state
requirements from one preparation, one repeated channel and one detector.
A separate Erlang-clock certificate converts an established spectral
obstruction into a finite-observation test without hidden-rate bounds or exact
pole fitting. Explicit classical realizations identify the boundaries of both
interpretations. In particular, arbitrary finite fresh-reset endpoint records
can be approximated by finite irreducible detailed-balance models when hidden
states, rates and preparation resources are unrestricted.

Stationary pair observations provide a different route. Reversible Markov
correlations are nonnegative mixtures of decaying exponentials. Certified
departures from that class impose sustained entropy-production requirements,
independent of hidden-state count. A nonstationary extension quantifies the
tradeoff between preparation divergence and integrated housekeeping entropy.
Exact polynomial and relative-entropy certificates connect this structure to
finite uncertain data. The software includes a curvature example that yields
information missed by both pairwise time-reversal and monotonic-revival tests.

The virtual-laboratory extension subjects these certificates to simulated
physical dynamics and apparatus imperfections. Independent trajectory and
open-system solvers generate records; inference receives only its declared
observations. Exact results identify which clock laws and exposure blocks
preserve the comparison class. Conditional-mean confidence bounds correct
correlated sampling, while fixed polynomial contrasts avoid an unnecessary
simultaneous-box penalty for predeclared targets. Matched comparisons retain
failed regimes, calibration costs and stronger baseline methods.

The calibrated-inference extension asks which additional measurements sharpen
these conclusions. It supplies calibrated comparison classes, nonlinear
information certificates, an activity-dependent entropy bound, limitations on
total dissipation inference, sequential acquisition, and sparse dependent
sampling. A scoped overdamped-diffusion corollary transfers the record-information
bounds beyond finite jump processes. Direct bounded analog scores preserve information otherwise lost
through randomized binary encoding. A public optical-tweezer dataset supplies
an exploratory test of the observation assumptions and a concrete calibration
gap, rather than an unsupported thermodynamic conclusion.

Worked counts are synthetic or prospective except in the explicitly identified
reanalysis of previously published optical-tweezer measurements. Conservative sample bounds
are reported even when large; unmatched examples are not presented as
performance advantages. The earliest simple survival assay loses to an
established nonlinear baseline. Core spectral, realization, combinatorial and
thermodynamic ingredients are attributed to prior work. Candidate originality
concerns the stated robust constructions and operational integration, and
historical priority remains unresolved.

All mathematical guarantees refer to stated model and access classes. The worked studies include analytical proofs, simulated benchmarks, and
explicitly labeled exploratory reanalysis of published measurements. None
supplies a newly measured apparatus or an empirical entropy-production certificate. The reference has undergone internal adversarial review and reproducibility checks; it has not undergone external peer review or empirical validation on a newly measured apparatus. The release ledger distinguishes those forms of evidence.

\begin{longtable}{p{0.19\textwidth}p{0.74\textwidth}}
\toprule
Symbol & Meaning\\
\midrule
$\theta$, $h$ & A fixed candidate physical mechanism; $h$ indexes a finite family.\\
$g(\theta)$ & The scientific target: a label, parameter, current or other specified property.\\
$\pi$, $\Pi_C$ & A permitted experimental policy; policies within a declared resource budget.\\
$P_\theta^\pi$ & Law of the complete accessible record, including observed action outcomes.\\
$\varpi$ & A stationary distribution in physical network calculations. Local chapter definitions take precedence when an inherited conventional symbol is retained.\\
$K^{a,y}$ & Classical kernel for action $a$, recorded outcome $y$, and the next hidden state.\\
$\mathcal E^{a,y}$ & Completely positive quantum instrument component, including its outcome probability and state change.\\
$\alpha M_w\beta$ & A finite linear representation of a probability difference for a chronological word $w$.\\
$V$, $d$ & Accessible effect span and paired representation dimension.\\
$C_t$, $g(C_t)$ & Retained model set and the corresponding set of possible target values.\\
$\operatorname{TV}(P,Q)$ & Total variation, with convention $\frac12\int|p-q|$.\\
$\eta$, $\varepsilon$ & Declared calibration radius or measurement error, defined for each use.\\
$\alpha$, $\beta$, $\delta$ & Error budgets in statistical/resource theorems; distinguished locally from linear representation vectors.\\
$Q$, $G$, $R$ & Continuous-time Markov generators; their roles are specified in each model.\\
$J_{ij}$ & Stationary probability current, with a stated edge orientation.\\
$W$, $\Sigma$ & A declared work/expenditure variable and entropy production; never interchangeable without physical assumptions.\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}
Classical probability distributions are row vectors and Markov generators have zero row sums. Quantum states are density operators; the finite linear representation uses an explicitly chosen real Hermitian basis. A word is chronological: its leftmost action occurs first. Exact software inputs are integers or rational strings. Floating-point approximations are labeled as numerical checks, not exact certificates.
