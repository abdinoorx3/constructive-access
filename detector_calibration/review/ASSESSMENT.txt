1. WHAT THE EXISTING FINITE CERTIFICATE ACTUALLY REQUIRES

The earlier score needs a pointwise bound Gamma_f(X0)<=a(U), where the
selection/weight rule is a function of the true central state and is frozen
before testing, or is covered by a separately proved simultaneous argument.
Replacing Gamma by E[Gamma|Y], a fitted bin average or a sample maximum does
not supply that contract. A noisy center is not automatically the true-state
coordinate U. Selecting or weighting with the same noisy center also breaks
the simple conservative shared-center-noise argument in the earlier chapter.

Global bounds on Lf, Ldagger f, L²f and (Ldagger)²f are separate assumptions.
Smoothness of each model, a stationary density and lower ellipticity do not
provide common numeric values. Calibration cannot assume a circle generator's
sup-norm formulas while claiming validity for all hidden diffusions.

2. A REPLICATED POLYNOMIAL ROUTE WITHOUT NOISY-CENTER SELECTION

For |f|<=1, take no retention, a constant pointwise envelope G>0, and
q(f)=−lambda f. The tuning value lambda is fixed before testing, or handled
uniformly through a jointly covered fixed moment vector. Put b=1−lambda h.
With two independently unbiased central readouts and independently unbiased
endpoint readouts, the raw score
 T=−(Z_plus−b Z0_1)(Z_minus−b Z0_2)/h²
has the correct ideal expectation conditional on the complete true triple.
Conditional independence of these detector errors is essential. Repeated
readout of the same exposure is not independent replication of the physical
trajectory and does not itself remove blur.

For point readouts, subtracting 2|lambda| beta_g+beta_K and dividing by G
gives the earlier finite-h inequality without local selection or estimated
bin occupancies. It uses four readouts per triple. Bounded detector output
permits bounded-score concentration. Gaussian output requires an actual
unbounded-score argument, for example calibrated variance concentration or
median-of-means, rather than simply using the bounded-readout theorem.
For the ideal circle with lambda=gamma and G=gamma, its small-h mean reaches
sigma/2. This is a potentially useful conservative baseline, not the sharp
local reciprocal-Gamma weight.

The polynomial hierarchy proposed independently by root is valid under the
STRUCTURAL relation Gamma_f=gamma(1−f²):
 w_m(f)=sum_(j=0)^m f^(2j),
 w_m(f) Gamma_f=gamma(1−f^(2m+2))<=gamma.
The same inequality for the entropy score uses the numerator
(Af)^2−(Sf+lambda f)^2. Its expectation is at most gamma sigma after
multiplication by w_m. All moments in w_m times the raw quadratic score can
be estimated without read-noise bias using 2m+2 independent center replicas
and two endpoint readings. Shared replicas across different monomials affect
their covariance but not unbiasedness; concentration must handle that shared
record structure.

At an ideal circle with lambda=gamma the population limit is
 sigma[1−E cos(phi)^(2m+2)],
which approaches sigma as m increases. The extra replicas, moment variances
and blur penalties are real resource costs. This is not a free replacement
for a local geometry measurement.

3. JOINT CALIBRATION AND ITS CIRCULARITY TRAPS

Stationarity implies C'(0+)=E fLf=−E Gamma_f, where C(t)=E f0 ft.
With the exact structural equality above,
 C0−Ch=h gamma(1−C0)+R, |R|<=h² ||L²f||infinity/2.
Thus jointly covered moments can constrain gamma. The equality is crucial:
if only Gamma_f<=gamma(1−f²) is assumed, the same ratio describes an
averaged quantity and does not upper-calibrate the pointwise coefficient.
If 1−C0 is not separated from zero on the confidence region, inversion
must retain that degeneracy and return an unbounded/inconclusive result.

Same-record calibration is not automatically invalid. A fixed vector of
unbiased moment statistics may receive simultaneous coverage, after which a
deterministic nuisance-region optimization preserves coverage. Supremizing
over lambda, or over a predeclared finite set of polynomial degrees, is valid
when the same covered moment event proves all those inequalities. Substituting
a fitted score into a concentration theorem for a predeclared score is not.

Likewise, a concentration radius monotone in a nuisance value can be evaluated
at a simultaneously covered upper nuisance bound using an unconditional union
bound. Conditioning on that random calibration event and assuming the original
sampling law remains unchanged would be a different, generally invalid step.

Independent Gaussian replica differences identify their read-noise variance
without the physical signal: (Z1−Z2)/sqrt(2) is centered Gaussian under the
declared noise contract. They do not by themselves identify shutter width or
generator suprema. Exposure needs declared reference calibration or a justified
model confidence region. A numerical parameter search box cannot silently
discard unbounded alternatives; any finite prior bounds must be explicit.

Model-specific inversion of a circle parameter confidence region is another
valid route, but then omega²/gamma is an entropy statement within the assumed
circle model. It must not be presented as a general hidden-diffusion inference
unless the broader geometry and generator contracts are separately proved.

4. A SHARP SIMPLE BLUR CORRECTION FOR THE CONSTANT WEIGHT

For a common causal boxcar of width tau<=h, stationarity gives
 C_A(t)=E C(t+U−V), with U,V independent Uniform[0,tau].
If |f|<=1 and ||L²f||infinity<=M2, then |C''(t)|<=M2 for t>=0.
Since E(U−V)=0 and E(U−V)²=tau²/6,
 |C_A(kh)−C(kh)|<=M2 tau²/12, k=1,2.
At zero, symmetry of C and its right derivative give
 C_A0=C0−(tau/3)E Gamma_f+R0, |R0|<=M2 tau²/12.
Here E|U−V|=tau/3.

The raw constant-weight score expectation is
 −[C(2h)−2b C(h)+b² C0]/h².
Consequently blur can INCREASE the score, creating an apparent positive
defect even for reversible dynamics. A safe upward-bias subtraction is
 b² G tau/(3h²)+(1+|b|)² M2 tau²/(12h²)
when E Gamma_f<=G. This is O(tau/h²), sharper than the generic pathwise
L2 perturbation bound O(sqrt(tau)/h²). The calculation is restricted to the
quadratic constant-weight score; it does not by itself prove a higher-degree
weighted correction.

5. ROOT'S GENERAL SEMIGROUP-WORD BLUR LEMMA

The proposed lemma survives independent derivation. Assume a stationary smooth
diffusion with invariant pi, |f|<=1 and ||Lf||infinity<=M. All products and
semigroup words below must lie in the generator domain; the smooth compact
diffusion setting supplies this closure.

For a real word g with ||g||infinity<=1 and B=||Lg||_(L1(pi)), invariance gives
 E Gamma_f=−E fLf<=M,
 E Gamma_g=−E gLg<=B.
The diffusion product rule and Cauchy--Schwarz give
 ||L(fg)||1<=B+M+2sqrt(MB)=(sqrt(B)+sqrt(M))².
Between products, L P_t g=P_t Lg and invariant Markov L1 contraction preserve
this bound. Starting from the constant one therefore proves ||Lg||1<=k²M
for every semigroup word containing k factors f. No reversibility is needed.

For a d-point ordered moment, differentiating gap j applies L to the future
suffix with d−j factors. The past prefix, transferred using the stationary
adjoint, is a SIGNED conditional moment with absolute value at most one.
It is not necessarily a nonnegative likelihood. Pairing this prefix with the
suffix derivative proves an absolute gap derivative bound (d−j)² M.
Coincident times use the right derivative and domain continuity.

Compare sorted blurred times with sorted ideal times. If each time is shifted
by a causal exposure offset in [-tau,0], sorting preserves
 t_i−tau<=u_i<=t_i.
Each adjacent gap thus changes by at most tau, rather than 2tau. Integrating
the derivative bounds yields the safe crude error
 M tau sum_(k=1)^(d−1) k².
Sharper constants follow by integrating the actual absolute gap displacements.
This avoids an unknown Gamma bound in the blur correction itself. The global
bound M remains a substantive structural/calibration premise.

I reviewed countermodels/RARE_BUMP_PROOF.txt and its implementation. The model
on T4 has L=d(z)partial_xx+Delta_z, d=1+(M−1)b(|z|/r), f=cos x. Its divergence

entry is the xx entry. Uniform pi is invariant, the generator is self-adjoint,
D>=I and the common L2 spectral gap is at least one. All models have zero
ordinary entropy. The plateau implies sup Gamma_f=||Lf||infinity=M and
||L^k f||infinity>=M^k.

The Newtonian-potential argument for the hitting bound is valid. A smooth
radial charge in ball r/2 gives p_r=r/|z| outside that support, with p_r>=1
on ball r. The C2 cutoff supported inside radius two fits the torus chart.
In its annulus, Delta[chi(s)/s]=chi''(s)/s and |chi''|<6, giving Delta u<=6r.
The stationary initial expectation is at most r(2+r/2)²/(4pi²)<9r/64.
Stopped Dynkin therefore bounds the hitting probability by r(9/64+6T).
The coupling of full hidden paths is identical before this hit, giving the
stated total-variation bound independently of the bump height M.

The resulting upper-confidence impossibility theorem is sound. Shrinking r
at each fixed M makes the bumped finite record arbitrarily close to baseline;
uniform coverage forces the baseline upper confidence bound to be infinite
with probability at least 1−alpha. The extension to an almost-surely finite
common stopping rule is valid when the rule is nonanticipating. Literal
pointwise and merely almost-everywhere envelopes are correctly distinguished
in the proof; evaluating an arbitrary a.e. envelope at y=0 would otherwise be
a null-set loophole.

This result concerns global assumptions used for finite-error inference, not

It explicitly does not prevent model-based or structurally justified
