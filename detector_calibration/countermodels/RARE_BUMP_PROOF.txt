COMPACT RARE-REGION COUNTERMODEL TO UNSUPPORTED GLOBAL CALIBRATION
Owner: /root/geometry_countermodels. Date: 2026-10-13 UTC.
Status: analytic proof with deterministic implementation checks. No trajectory
simulation, physical experiment, or historical-priority claim. All nine
released editions are unchanged.

MAIN CONCLUSION
For passive stationary observation over any finite total horizon, there is no
uniformly useful finite upper confidence bound on global local-diffusivity or
generator suprema over smooth compact uniformly elliptic diffusions without
additional structural bounds. This remains true with a bounded smooth detector,
a known uniform stationary density, fixed dimension four, a common ellipticity
lower bound, and a common L2 spectral-gap lower bound. It remains true even if
the full continuous hidden trajectory is observed noiselessly. Finer temporal
resolution or a noisier coarse detector cannot overcome this obstruction.

This is a statement about GLOBAL or stationary ESSENTIAL SUPREMA, not about
local volatility values already determined along an ideal observed path. It
does not state a nonzero dissipation lower bound, change a sharp ideal-law
minimum, or refute model-based calibration under additional verified structure.
Every model in the construction is reversible and has entropy zero.

1. EXACT MODEL CLASS
Let (x,z) lie on the four-dimensional torus (R/2pi Z)^4, where z has three
coordinates. Use the fundamental z cube [-pi,pi]^3 and the Euclidean norm near
its origin. Fix 0<r<=1/2 and M>=1. Choose a nonnegative C-infinity radial profile
b(s), equal to one for 0<=s<=1/2, equal to zero for s>=1, with 0<=b<=1.
For example, with h(t)=exp(-1/t) for t>0 and h(t)=0 otherwise, take
 b(s)=h(1-s)/[h(1-s)+h(s-1/2)] in 1/2<s<1,
with the declared constant pieces outside. This is smooth at both joins and
is constant near zero, so b(|z|/r) is a smooth function of z. Its support lies
inside the fundamental cube and it extends smoothly and periodically.

Define
 d_M,r(z)=1+(M-1)b(|z|/r),
 L_M,r=d_M,r(z) partial_xx + Delta_z,
 D_M,r=diag(d_M,r(z),1,1,1),
 f(x,z)=cos x.
In Ito form,
 dx_t=sqrt(2d_M,r(z_t)) dW_t^x mod 2pi,
 dz_t=sqrt(2) dW_t^z mod 2pi,
with independent Brownian components. The detector lies in [-1,1].

The normalized uniform density pi=(2pi)^(-4) is invariant. Indeed,
L=div(D grad) because d depends only on z, while its only nonconstant tensor
entry is the xx entry. Thus L is self-adjoint in L2(pi). Each model is smooth,
connected, compact, uniformly elliptic, and reversible under ordinary hidden-
state reversal. Its current velocity and entropy production are identically
zero. The common ellipticity lower bound is D>=I, independent of M and r.

The mixing gap is controlled as well: for every mean-zero smooth g,
 -<g,Lg>_pi=E_pi[d|partial_x g|^2+|grad_z g|^2]
             >=E_pi[|grad g|^2]>=E_pi[g^2].
The last inequality is the unit spectral-gap Poincare inequality on the
2pi-periodic torus. Hence all these reversible models have L2 spectral gap
at least one and mean-zero L2 semigroup contraction at most exp(-t).
The construction does not rely on a deteriorating lower ellipticity constant,
stationary density, or metastable mixing time.

2. CALIBRATION TARGETS THAT DIVERGE
For the bounded detector f=cos x,
 Gamma_f=d_M,r(z) sin^2 x,
 Lf=-d_M,r(z) cos x,
 L^2f=[d_M,r(z)^2-Delta_z d_M,r(z)] cos x.
Because d=M on the open inner ball |z|<r/2,
 sup Gamma_f=M,
 ||Lf||_infinity=M,
 ||L^2f||_infinity>=M^2.
More generally ||L^k f||_infinity>=M^k for every positive integer k, by
calculating at a point inside the plateau, where all derivatives of d vanish.
These are also stationary essential suprema: pi is positive everywhere and
the relevant functions are continuous. The maximum of sin^2 x or cos x at an
isolated x is no obstacle to an essential supremum, since every neighborhood
has positive stationary measure.

In contrast, the stationary mean local diffusivity remains close to baseline:
 1/2 <= E_pi[Gamma_f]
     <= 1/2+(M-1)r^3/(12pi^2).
The upper bound uses the volume 4pi r^3/3 of the bump's enclosing z ball and
E[sin^2 x]=1/2. Thus the choice r=M^(-2), for M>=sqrt(2), makes global suprema
diverge while this averaged quantity tends to 1/2. An average is not a global
pointwise envelope.


2b. BOUNDED DIFFUSIVITY DOES NOT BOUND THE FIRST GENERATOR
For a separate family, let N be a positive integer multiple of four and set
 d_N,r(x,z)=1+b(|z|/r)[1+sin(Nx)]/2,
 L_N,r=partial_x(d_N,r partial_x)+Delta_z.
This is again a smooth compact reversible diffusion with uniform stationary
law and spectral gap at least one, but now I<=D<=2I uniformly. The drift in
the x direction is partial_x d; retaining this divergence-form drift is
essential to uniform stationarity. The z dynamics are unchanged. Outside the
same rare z ball, both x drift and diffusion coincide with reference, so all
the coupling bounds below still apply.
For f=cos x,
 Lf=-d cos x-(N/2)b(|z|/r)cos(Nx)sin x.
At z in the plateau and x=pi/2, cos(Nx)=1, so Lf=-N/2. Thus
 Gamma_f<=2 but ||Lf||_infinity>=N/2.
Take N to infinity and r=N^-2. Known two-sided ellipticity, a global bound on
local diffusivity, uniform stationary density and a common mixing gap do not
supply a finite uniformly covered generator-first-derivative bound without
further regularity structure. The total-variation distance to reference still
tends to zero. Every model has entropy zero.

2c. BOUNDED DIFFUSIVITY AND Lf DO NOT BOUND L^2f
Return to the original radial family and fix M=2. Then I<=D<=2I and
sup Gamma_f=||Lf||_infinity=2 for every radius. For the explicit smooth profile,
at s=3/4 direct differentiation gives
 b(s)=1/2, b'(s)=-8, b''(s)=0,
 Delta_3 b=b''(s)+2b'(s)/s=-64/3.
At x=0 and |z|=3r/4 this implies
 L^2f=9/4+64/(3r^2).
Therefore ||L^2f||_infinity diverges as r tends to zero, even with the known
upper bounds D<=2I and ||Lf||<=2. The first and second generator regularity
constants cannot be interchanged. Again all models are reversible, the same
coupling bound applies, and the observed finite-record laws converge to the
reference law.

3. EXPLICIT HITTING-PROBABILITY BOUND IN THREE DIMENSIONS
Let z_t be stationary Brownian motion with generator Delta on the three-torus,
and let tau_r=inf{t>=0: |z_t|<=r}, with distance interpreted in the local ball.
For every T>=0 and 0<r<=1/2,
 P(tau_r<=T) <= r(9/64+6T).
One can replace the right side by its minimum with one. The constants are
convenient explicit bounds, not optimized capacity constants.

Proof. Choose a smooth nonnegative radial probability density rho_r supported
on the Euclidean ball |y|<r/2. Its Newtonian potential, scaled by r, is
 p_r(z)=r integral rho_r(y)/|z-y| dy.
It is smooth, nonnegative, and Delta p_r=-4pi r rho_r<=0. Outside |z|>=r/2,
radial symmetry and the elementary radial harmonic equation imply
p_r(z)=r/|z|. Inside the ball it is radially nonincreasing as radius increases,
so it is at least its boundary value 2. Consequently p_r>=1 on |z|<=r.

Let chi(s)=1 for s<=1, chi(s)=0 for s>=2, and on 1<s<2 set
 chi(s)=q(s-1), q(t)=1-10t^3+15t^4-6t^5.
This cutoff is C2, nonnegative, and has first and second derivatives zero at
its joins. It satisfies max|q''|=10sqrt(3)/3<6. Define u_r(z)=chi(|z|)p_r(z)
inside the fundamental cube and extend it periodically. Because its support
is contained in the radius-two ball, u_r is a nonnegative C2 torus function.
It is at least one on the target ball. Within |z|<=1 its Laplacian is
nonpositive. In 1<|z|<2, p_r=r/|z|, and the three-dimensional radial identity
 Delta[chi(s)/s]=chi''(s)/s
shows Delta u_r<=6r. Outside radius two the Laplacian is zero. Thus
 Delta u_r<=6r everywhere.

Its stationary expectation has an explicit upper bound. Since 0<=chi<=1,
Fubini's theorem and |y|<=r/2 give
 E_pi_z[u_r]
 <= r/(2pi)^3 integral rho_r(y) integral_{|z|<=2} |z-y|^-1 dz dy
 <= r/(2pi)^3 integral_{|w|<=2+r/2} |w|^-1 dw
 =r(2+r/2)^2/(4pi^2)
 <=81r/(64pi^2)<9r/64.
Dynkin's formula at the bounded stopping time tau_r wedge T now yields
 P(tau_r<=T)
 <=E[u_r(z_(tau_r wedge T))]
 <=E[u_r(z_0)]+6rT
 <=r(9/64+6T).
This proves the bound. Smoothness of the cutoff beyond C2 is unnecessary for
Dynkin's formula; all diffusion coefficients themselves remain C-infinity.

4. COUPLING THE COMPLETE CONTINUOUS RECORDS
Let the reference model be d=1, denoted P_0. Initialize it and a bumped model
with the same uniform (x_0,z_0), and use the same Brownian drivers. Their z
paths agree at every time, since the z dynamics do not depend on d. Their x
coefficients agree outside the bump support, so pathwise uniqueness implies
identical complete hidden trajectories until tau_r. The coupling inequality
therefore gives, for a full record on [0,T],
 TV(P_M,r^full[0,T],P_0^full[0,T])
 <=P(tau_r<=T)<=min{1,r(9/64+6T)}.
The bound is independent of the height M. It applies to the entire continuous
hidden path, not merely finitely many endpoints. Every common detector map,
exposure operation and randomized observation channel contracts total
variation, so the same bound holds for the bounded cosine detector and for
any common noisy readout obtained from it.

For N independent stationary records with durations summing to T_total, a
union coupling gives the bound
 min{1,r(9N/64+6T_total)}.
Every latent interval used by an exposure must be included in the duration
accounting. This theorem assumes passive stationary observations; it does not
cover an intervention that prepares the hidden system inside a known bump.

5. A PRECISE UPPER-CONFIDENCE IMPOSSIBILITY THEOREM
Fix any finite passive observation protocol covered above and 0<alpha<1.
Let U be a measurable statistic of its retained record, with values in
[0,+infinity], possibly including common external randomization. Suppose U is
an honest upper confidence bound over the entire constructed class, meaning
 P_M,r[U>=sup Gamma_f]=P_M,r[U>=M]>=1-alpha
for every M>=1 and every 0<r<=1/2. Then under the reference d=1 law,
 P_0[U=+infinity]>=1-alpha.

Proof. For each fixed M, total variation implies
 P_0[U>=M]>=1-alpha-min{1,r(9N/64+6T_total)}.
Let r down to zero. This gives P_0[U>=M]>=1-alpha for every M. Let the integer
M tend to infinity and use continuity from above of probability. The result
follows. Thus a bound that is finite almost surely under the benign baseline
cannot have the claimed uniform coverage. This is a nonparametric coverage
obstruction, not a statement that all model-based confidence bounds fail.

The same conclusion applies to an honest upper confidence bound on ||Lf||
or ||L^2f||. For the latter use ||L^2f||>=M^2 and let M tend to infinity.
The strengthened variants in Sections 2b and 2c give the same conclusion for
||Lf|| even under I<=D<=2I, and for ||L^2f|| even under I<=D<=2I and ||Lf||<=2.
In those variants take the displayed diverging target along r down to zero;
the TV bound again tends to zero. Additional smoothness constants, rather than
just an upper diffusion bound, are necessary to exclude these alternatives.
A reported pointwise envelope a_hat(y) claimed to satisfy
 Gamma_f(x,z)<=a_hat(f(x,z)) for EVERY hidden state
must in particular satisfy a_hat(0)>=M. Therefore, with uniform coverage for
that pointwise contract, a_hat(0) must be infinite with probability at least
1-alpha under baseline. This statement uses a literal pointwise contract.
If validity is instead only stationary-almost-everywhere, it still applies
when a_hat is continuous, by continuity and positivity of the stationary
density. Arbitrary measurable almost-everywhere envelopes require a separate
integrated formulation; one cannot infer their value at the zero-probability
level y=0 merely from an almost-everywhere assertion.

The impossibility also extends to a single passive path observed until a
common nonanticipating stopping rule that is finite almost surely under reference. Let S
be that reference stopping time. For any K<infinity, coupled stopped records
agree unless tau_r<=K or S>K, so their total-variation distance is bounded by
 r(9/64+6K)+P_0(S>K).
First let r down to zero and then K tend to infinity. This again gives
reference convergence and the same confidence argument. This extension does
not authorize infinite-time observation for finite cost.

6. WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY
- Ideal continuous scalar paths do determine Gamma_f(X_t) along those paths
  through quadratic variation. The obstruction concerns extrapolation to
  unvisited hidden regions and a global supremum. If the bump is encountered,
  exact path access may reveal its elevated diffusivity.
- Every constructed model has entropy zero. There is no claimed change in a
  sharp entropy minimum, hidden-current identifiability, or a lower bound on
  physical dissipation. The target here is an assumption used by finite-error
  inference, not entropy itself.
- Extra structure can change the conclusion: known derivative bounds, a
  bounded parametric family, spatial access and controlled preparation, or a
  separately justified envelope can exclude these alternatives. The theorem
  does not prohibit those routes.
- The lower ellipticity bound and mixing gap alone are insufficient; the class
  has no common upper diffusivity or higher-derivative bounds. Smoothness of
  each individual model does not supply such common numerical constants.
- Fixed observation dimension, positive stationary density and a bounded
  detector do not by themselves justify replacing a global envelope by an
  empirical average or a fitted conditional variance.

7. IMPLEMENTATION AND REVIEW STATUS
rare_bump.py implements the actual smooth plateau profile and evaluates the
proved deterministic TV and mean-volatility bounds. results.json gives three
examples (M=10,100,1000 with r=M^-2). test_rare_bump.py contains eleven checks:
exact symbolic cutoff curvature and radial Laplacian identities, generator
composition, plateau behavior, independent radial integration of the mean,
confidence-obstruction scaling, potential expectation constants and input
validation, plus exact transition-profile derivatives and the bounded-diffusion
generator variants. The reported floating values are non-outward-rounded
diagnostics, not numerical interval certificates; an extremely small positive
mean-volatility margin can round to zero. The analytic formulas are authoritative.
No discrete simulator is used as a substitute for a hitting-time proof.
Independent mathematical review is requested separately.
