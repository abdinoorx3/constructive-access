MAIN FALSIFIABLE THEOREM

Zt=(1/tau) integral[t-tau,t]Ys ds, tau>0. At a countable dense timestamp set,

NOISE STEP
If compactly supported latent vector laws P,Q satisfy P*N=Q*N for any known
noise law N, their characteristic functions agree near zero because Nhat(0)=1
and Nhat continuous. Compact support makes Phat,Qhat analytic, so equality
extends everywhere. Thus each latent finite-dimensional Z law is identified,
even if noise CF has zeros or noise lacks finite variance. Bounded latent
support is essential to that particular argument; without it, nowhere-zero
noise CF is a familiar alternative sufficient assumption. No stable analytic
continuation is claimed. Known correlated noise also works if its complete
joint laws, not merely marginals, are calibrated and independent of signal.
Continuity makes all dense-time finite-dimensional Z laws determine C(R) law.

ELEMENTARY EXPOSURE INVERSE (root and reviewer independently proposed)
tau Z'_t=Y_t-Y_{t-tau} exactly. Put mu=EZ0=EY0 and
R_N Z(t)=mu+tau sum[k=0..N-1](1-k/N)Z'_{t-k tau}.
Telescoping gives R_N Z(t)=Y_t-(N^-1 sum[j=1..N]Y_{t-j tau}-mu).
Mean-square error is covariance double sum/N² and tends0 under covariance
decay. Joint L² convergence at any finite set of times identifies Y path law.
If |Cov(Y0,Yt)|<=C exp(-kappa|t|), error²<=C coth(kappa tau/2)/N.
This is finite-window POPULATION truncation error with exact Z derivatives,
not a noisy finite-data estimation guarantee. Derivative RMS error delta
amplifies at most tau(N+1)delta/2 by the triangle inequality; mean error adds.

SPECTRAL VERSION / STANDARD LANDWEBER ITERATION
A is known shift-average operator, T=A*A, norm A<=1.
L_N Z=sum[j=0..N-1](I-T)^j A*Z=[I-(I-T)^N]Y when Z=AY.
By the spectral theorem, L² recovery of Y holds iff Y has no projection in ker A.
Boxcar multiplier H(lambda)=exp(-ilambda tau/2)sinc(lambda tau/2), zeros
at nonzero 2pi k/tau. The minimal signal premise is no spectral mass at those
zeros; covariance decay is sufficient. Operator norm L_N<=sqrtN, and exact
squared bias=integral(1-|H|²)^(2N)dmu_Y. This is standard linear inverse
theory, not a newly named mathematical operator. Finite-error rate needs
quantitative spectral-mass control near zeros and high frequencies, not just
injectivity.

DIFFUSION CONSEQUENCE
Smooth detector on compact connected uniformly elliptic stationary diffusion
is bounded with exponentially decaying centered correlations. Every compatible
hidden model under the calibrated detector therefore shares the identified
ideal law. For exposed/noisy cosine-circle law, the broad-class attained
ordinary-hidden-diffusion minimum remains omega²/gamma. This uses complete

UNKNOWN BOXCAR WIDTH: OPTIONAL PROPOSITION
Countermodel agent noted, and I independently checked, that ideal centered
detector PSD is strictly positive at every real lambda in this class. With
f centered and nonzero, u=(i lambda-L)^-1 f,
S_Y(lambda)=2Re<f,u>=2 integral grad(conj u)^T D grad u dpi>0.
The Poincare gap and ellipticity give the mean-zero resolvent; nonconstant f
gives strict positivity. Then S_Z=|Htau|² S_Y has zeros exactly at nonzero
2pi k/tau. Its smallest positive zero identifies width tau if the boxcar
shape and physical clock are known. Constant detectors are excluded. This
is population identification, not robust spectral-zero estimation or
identification of arbitrary unknown response kernels. Known noise can first
be removed at the law level without assuming finite variance.

FAILURE AND SCOPE
A random-phase periodic harmonic at 2pi/tau is erased exactly, so stationarity
or continuous-flow ergodicity alone is insufficient. Mixing excludes it.
Fixed-grid sampling is not dense access, so our theorem makes no claim about
recovering the path between grid points. An explicit construction gives a mixing
OU counterexample for a fixed grid. Near spectral zeros and at high frequency,
the inverse can be arbitrarily ill-conditioned despite injectivity.
Root covers a strong finite-noise obstruction: for a fixed-gamma, high-omega
cosine circle, exposure variance is O(omega^-2); the finite Gaussian-mixture
law approaches pure noise while the sharp ideal-law minimum omega²/gamma
diverges. Uniform finite upper confidence bounds fail under that scope.
Calibration and independence premises cannot be inferred silently from test
records. The sharp minimum is the target, rather than arbitrary invisible
extra dissipation, which already lacks upper bounds under ideal access.

INDEPENDENT REVIEWER'S DENSE-IID REFINEMENT, CHECKED AND ADDED
At a target t choose deterministic distinct dense timestamps t_j->t. Conditional
on the continuous exposure path, bounded complex variables exp(iu B_tj) have
independent centered noises, so Kolmogorov's strong law gives their average
->exp(iu Z_t)*noiseCF(u), regardless of noise moments. For countable u_k->0,
noiseCF nonzero eventually; ratio gives exp(iu_k Z_t), then Im()/u_k->Z_t.
Countable target times plus continuity reconstruct Z path almost surely as
a measurable function of infinite dense noisy readings. No bounded latent
amplitude needed for this iid result. Thus the main injectivity theorem extends
to continuous square-integrable stationary Y with covariance decay.
This is an infinite-readout information statement, not finite-time denoising
or a claim that real overlapping detector errors become independent.
