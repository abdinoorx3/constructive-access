2. POPULATION RECOVERY: ASSUMPTIONS AND PROOF CHECKS

I reviewed theory/detector_recovery.tex directly. For a continuous stationary
bounded Y and known boxcar width tau, Z_t=tau^-1 integral_[t-tau,t]Y_s ds is
C1, even though Y may be a diffusion observation. Thus Z has zero ordinary
quadratic variation, and the earlier local-geometry formula cannot be applied
directly to Z as if it were Y.

Known independent additive convolution is injective on compactly supported
finite-dimensional latent laws: the noise characteristic function is nonzero
near zero, and the latent characteristic functions are analytic. This proves
uniqueness even when the known noise characteristic function has zeros or the
noise has no moments. It does not provide stable analytic continuation. Known
correlated noise is allowed only when its full joint law is specified and is
independent of the signal; a one-time marginal is insufficient.

The stronger iid dense-readout argument is sound. Conditional on the continuous
Z path, averages of exp(iu B_tj) for distinct t_j -> t obey the independent
bounded-variable strong law. Dividing their limit by the known noise
characteristic function near zero recovers exp(iu Z_t). A countable sequence
u -> 0 recovers the real Z_t without phase ambiguity; countable dense targets
and continuity recover the path. This uses infinitely many fresh independent
readouts within arbitrarily small neighborhoods. It does not apply to a
continuous white-noise channel, reused photons, or general correlated noise.
The argument does permit a square-integrable unbounded latent Y in the iid
version of the later covariance-based theorem.

The triangular inverse identity was checked algebraically:
 R_N Z(t) = E Z_0 + tau sum_(k=0)^(N-1)(1-k/N)Z'_(t-k tau)
          = Y_t - [(1/N)sum_(j=1)^N Y_(t-j tau)-E Y_0].
Centered covariance decay makes the error tend to zero in L2. The finite-set
L2 convergence identifies all ideal finite-dimensional distributions from the
common exposure law, and continuity identifies the path law. For a covariance
envelope C exp(-kappa |t|), the bound C coth(kappa tau/2)/N is correct.
Derivative estimation error delta is amplified by at most tau(N+1)delta/2;
the truncation bound alone therefore does not solve finite noisy inversion.

The Landweber alternative is standard and correctly attributed. Its bias is
the spectral integral of (1-|H_tau|^2)^(2N); its noise-amplification bound is
sqrt(N). Recovery requires no spectral mass at the nonzero zeros 2pi k/tau.
Covariance mixing is sufficient; ergodicity of the full continuous flow is
not. A uniform-phase resonant harmonic is an explicit stationary, nonmixing
counterexample erased by exposure.

For compact connected uniformly elliptic diffusions and a nonconstant smooth
detector, the generator-resolvent PSD identity and its strict positivity also
check out. On the centered space the Poincare gap supplies the imaginary-axis
resolvent. Its real quadratic form is the positive Dirichlet form, and zero
would force the detector to be constant. Consequently a known boxcar shape's
width is identified by the first positive spectral zero at population level.
This needs calibrated physical time and the specified diffusion class; it is
not a stable finite-sample spectral-zero estimator or unknown-kernel theorem.

3. THE CORRECT ENTROPY TARGET

The finite-access obstruction uses the compact circle family with fixed
gamma>0 and frequency omega. Its ideal cosine observation law P_omega has
the previously proved sharp compatible minimum M(P_omega)=omega^2/gamma.
Thus the obstruction concerns an ideal-law invariant that changes across the
family, or actual entropy when the circle architecture is known. It is not
merely the familiar possibility of appending an invisible dissipative system.

The detector recovery result preserves that attained minimum at the complete
noisy dense-law level within the declared compatible compact diffusion class.
It does not assign entropy to detector electronics or equate inference cost
with the system's entropy-production rate.

4. FINITE GAUSSIAN-NOISE ACCESS AND CONFIDENCE OBSTRUCTION

For stationary exposed A in [-1,1], E A=0 and
 V_omega=Re[tau/(gamma-i omega)
       -(1-exp(-(gamma-i omega)tau))/(gamma-i omega)^2]/tau^2
 <= [tau gamma+1+exp(-gamma tau)]/[tau^2(gamma^2+omega^2)].
Independent quadrature verifies the expression. With n predetermined readings
and independent Gaussian noise variance nu^2>0, convexity of KL gives
 KL(P_omega^record || N(0,nu^2 I)) <= n V_omega/(2nu^2),
even when latent exposure windows overlap or are correlated. Hence TV tends
to zero at fixed n as |omega| tends to infinity, while M(P_omega) diverges.
Adaptive timing/stopping does not follow automatically from this proof.

If an upper confidence rule U has coverage at least 1-alpha for every omega,
then under the pure-noise reference Q it must satisfy Q(U=+infinity)>=1-alpha.
Indeed, for any fixed K choose omega with M(P_omega)>=K, transfer the coverage
event to Q by TV convergence, then let K grow. Every finite-dimensional
Gaussian location mixture has strictly positive density, so these laws are
mutually absolutely continuous with Q. In particular, a rule uniformly valid
over all omega cannot be almost surely finite under every family member.
The Gaussian noise being strictly positive and a missing frequency envelope
are essential; small latent variance alone does not imply TV convergence to
a noiseless point mass.

5. INDEPENDENT-TRIAL COUNTS: PASSIVE AND PREPARED

The following are binary testing results for omega versus 2omega along
omega=2pi m/tau as m -> infinity, with gamma,tau,nu>0 and target error below
1/2 fixed. Each record is an independent exposure trial. Merely nonoverlapping
windows of one stationary trajectory are not automatically independent.
These results do not establish a minimax estimator over a continuous unknown
frequency range.

For stationary trials, symmetry makes all odd exposure moments vanish. The
exact Gaussian-mixture identity is
 chi^2(Law(A+N)||Law(N))
   =sum_(k>=2)(E A^k)^2/(k!nu^(2k))
   <= V_omega^2 [cosh(1/nu^2)-1].
The looser exp(1/nu^2)-1-1/nu^2 constant is also valid. Tensorizing KL and
using Pinsker implies n=Omega(omega^4) to distinguish omega from 2omega.
The exact resonant variance satisfies
 V_omega-V_(2omega) ~ 3[gamma tau+1-exp(-gamma tau)]/(4tau^2 omega^2).
The sample mean of X^2 attains n=O(omega^4), because
 E X^4<=1+6nu^2+3nu^4 and Chebyshev gives per-hypothesis error at most
 4[1+6nu^2+3nu^4]/[n(V_omega-V_(2omega))^2].
The manuscript's sharper numerator also checks: Var(X^2) equals
E A^4-V^2+4nu^2V+2nu^4 and is at most
S_nu(V)=(1+4nu^2)V-V^2+2nu^4, since A^4<=A^2. The maximum of this
quantity over the two hypotheses replaces the looser fourth-moment bound.

For known oriented preparation phi_0=pi/2, the resonant mean is
 mu_omega=-(1-exp(-gamma tau))omega/[tau(gamma^2+omega^2)].
Writing c=(1-exp(-gamma tau))/tau, its absolute gap is
 c|omega||2omega^2-gamma^2|/
 [(gamma^2+omega^2)(gamma^2+4omega^2)] ~ c/(2|omega|).
The gap is zero at omega=gamma/sqrt(2); the rate assertion is asymptotic and
must not claim separation by this statistic at every frequency. Bounded
exposure plus Gaussian read noise is sub-Gaussian with proxy 1+nu^2, yielding
midpoint error <=exp[-n gap^2/(8(1+nu^2))] and n=O(omega^2).

The matching lower bound follows from Ito's formula for sin(phi):
 sqrt(E A^2) <= [2+gamma tau+sqrt(2gamma tau)]/(|omega|tau),
uniformly in initial phase. Gaussian-mixture KL and triangle-Pinsker then
require n=Omega(omega^2). The stationary ideal-law minimum remains the target;
the prepared experiment itself is not stationary. Preparation and reset costs
are separate. Measuring Y_0=0 alone does not select oriented phase: an equal
mixture of pi/2 and 3pi/2 cancels the activated mean. Exact point preparation
can be weakened to a known initial circular moment with E cos(phi_0)=0 and
E sin(phi_0)=r!=0; the mean gap is then multiplied by r.

6. DECLARED SENSOR-ENERGY MODEL

If, as a separate calibrated detector assumption, Gaussian variance is
nu_i^2=kappa/E_i, deterministic per-reading energies satisfy
 KL(P_omega||Q)<=H2 sum_i E_i/(2kappa omega^2),
where H2=[2+gamma tau+sqrt(2gamma tau)]^2/tau^2. Comparing omega and 2omega
therefore requires total readout energy at least
 16kappa(1-2epsilon)^2 omega^2/(9H2).
This is valid for a common predetermined energy schedule. Data-dependent
energy allocation would need a separate sequential proof.

The fourth-moment bound used for achievability is also valid. If
M_t=integral_0^t cos(phi_s)dW_s, then Ito's formula gives
 E M_tau^4=6 E integral_0^tau M_s^2 cos^2(phi_s)ds <=3tau^2.
Applying |a+b|^4<=8(|a|^4+|b|^4) to the integrated sine identity yields
 E A^4<=H4/omega^4,
 H4=8[(2+gamma tau)^4+12gamma^2 tau^2]/tau^4.
At E=c omega^2 per reading,
 E X^4<=[H4+6kappa H2/c+3kappa^2/c^2]/omega^4.
The stationary squared-reading test then needs only a bounded number of
trials, attaining total readout energy O(omega^2). The fixed-noise count
exponent difference therefore disappears for optimized readout energy under
this declared sensor model. This is not a universal energy-information law,
an experimental calibration of a sensor, or a bound on total physical cost.

7. EXACT FIXED-GRID OU AMBIGUITY

The countermodels proof was reviewed against independent double-integral
calculations. It uses noncompact smooth elliptic OU dynamics and an unbounded
linear detector, not the compact bounded-detector class of the earlier sharp
minimum. With omega_m=2pi m, exposed variance V_m and off-diagonal coefficient
F_m have the stated exact formulas. The ratio derivative is strictly negative,
which makes the two oscillator weights strictly positive and matches both
coefficients. Gaussianity then establishes equality of every sampled joint
law on the entire integer grid, rather than a finite covariance checklist.
Ordinary entropy differs as 8pi^2 versus 8pi^2 n^2.

Both mixture components contribute to the detector. Still, the result as
proved concerns compatible actual entropies in this noncompact class. The
optional local Gaussian lower-bound algebra in the review tests is not a
promotion of the earlier compact theorem to all noncompact diffusions; such
an extension would require its own integrability proof. The compact-circle
construction in section 4 is the primary ideal-minimum obstruction.

A separate exact rational check supplies one certified finite separation.
Set gamma=tau=1, nu=1/2, epsilon=1/10, omega=64pi (resonance m=32).
Using Machin's identity and alternating arctan series gives
157/50<pi<22/7. The exponential Taylor series gives 8/3<e<11/4 and
exp(7/3)>10, hence log(10)<7/3. Put
 wL=64(157/50), wU=64(22/7),
 dL=(5/8)wL(2wL^2-1)/[(1+wU^2)(1+4wU^2)].
Since 1-e^-1>5/8, the prepared mean gap is larger than dL. Its sufficient
count threshold is therefore below 10(7/3)/dL^2<10,000,000, an exact
rational inequality. Also cosh(4)-1<28, 2-e^-1<18/11 and
V(w)<(18/11)/w^2. The passive necessary count exceeds
 2(4/5)^2 wL^4/[28((5/4)(18/11))^2]>17,000,000.
All final comparisons use Python Fraction arithmetic. Thus 10,000,000
prepared trials suffice under the stated test bound, whereas 17,000,000
passive trials cannot attain the same equal-prior error. These are conservative
mathematical trial counts, not fitted operating characteristics or claims of
laboratory practicality; preparation and other excluded resources remain
different. This finite certificate supplements, rather than proves anew, the
asymptotic results.
