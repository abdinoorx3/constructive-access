1. VERDICT AND DISTINCTIONS
Known boxcar exposure does preserve the full ideal continuous path law of a
stationary square-integrable process under a suitable no-spectral-atoms
condition at its discrete transfer zeros. Mixing supplies that condition. It
would be incorrect to infer that fixed-rate samples or a finite noisy record
preserve that law, or that inversion is stable uniformly across model classes.

An independent-noise specification must distinguish:
(a) exact full record distributions and their deconvolution;
(b) one arbitrarily densely sampled path with fresh independent point noise;
(c) a continuous correlated-noise or white-noise observation channel;
(d) finitely many readings with their physical resource cost.
These are different information resources. A law-identifiability proof is not
a finite-sample certificate or a pathwise noise-removal theorem for every model.

2. WHERE BOXCAR INFORMATION IS LOST
For Z_t=(1/delta) integral_{t-delta}^t Y_s ds, the transfer function is
 H_delta(omega)=exp(-i omega delta/2)*sinc(omega delta/2),
 H_delta(0)=1, zeros at omega=2pi k/delta for nonzero integers k.
Thus DC is preserved, not erased. The exact nullspace on continuous paths is
delta-periodic zero-mean signals: Z=0 implies Y_t=Y_{t-delta} by differentiation,
and the average over one period is zero. Conversely, those signals integrate
to zero in every exposure window.

An exact stationary counterexample without mixing is
 Y_t=A cos(2pi t/delta+Theta), Theta uniform on [0,2pi).
Its full exposed path is identically zero for every A, so amplitude is lost.
It is stationary and continuous but not mixing. The phase dynamics are a
deterministic circle rotation; this is not a uniformly elliptic diffusion.
The continuous-time stationary process can be ergodic while the time-delta
sampled shift is not ergodic. Consequently, continuous-time ergodicity alone
does not justify replacing mixing in a proposed inverse theorem.

A direct reconstruction under sampled-shift ergodicity complements the spectral
proof owned by population_theory. For a two-sided integrable stationary signal
with continuous paths and known mean m, differentiation of the exposed path gives
 delta Z'_t=Y_t-Y_{t-delta}.
For every positive integer N,
 delta sum_{k=0}^{N-1}(1-k/N) Z'_{t-k delta}
   =Y_t-(1/N)sum_{j=1}^N Y_{t-j delta}.
If the time-delta shift is ergodic, Birkhoff's theorem makes the last average
converge almost surely to m. Hence the weighted derivative sums plus m recover
Y_t. The complete stationary exposed law determines m=E[Z_0]. Simultaneous
reconstruction at countably many times, followed by continuity, determines the
whole path law. This is an infinite-horizon, exact-derivative statement and is
not numerically stable under uncalibrated finite measurement error.

3. EXACT FIXED-SAMPLING AMBIGUITY WITH KNOWN NONZERO EXPOSURE
This example is within smooth, mixing, stationary, uniformly elliptic Gaussian
OU diffusions on Euclidean space. It is NONCOMPACT, with an UNBOUNDED linear
observable. It does not refute the continuous-law inverse theorem or claim an
example inside the earlier compact bounded-detector class.

For m=0,1,... and a>0 consider the two-dimensional oscillator
 dX_t=(-I+omega_m J)X_t dt+sqrt(2a)dW_t,
 omega_m=2pi m, J=[[0,-1],[1,0]].
Its stationary covariance is aI, and Y_t is the first coordinate. Then
 Cov(Y_0,Y_t)=a exp(-|t|)cos(omega_m t).
This is a smooth, nondegenerate, exponentially mixing diffusion. For ordinary
reversal (both hidden coordinates even), the stationary current velocity is
omega_m JX, D=aI, so entropy is
 sigma_m=E[(omega_m JX)^T(aI)^-1(omega_m JX)]=2omega_m^2.
Generalized parity conventions would change that physical interpretation and
are not being used here.

Retain unit-width boxcar averages ONLY at integer times:
 Z_k=integral_{k-1}^k Y_t dt, k in Z.
For a=1, write x_m=(2pi m)^2, e=exp(-1), C=2(cosh(1)-1). Direct integration gives
 V_m=Var(Z_0)=2[e+(2-e)x_m]/(1+x_m)^2,
 F_m=C(1-x_m)/(1+x_m)^2,
 Cov(Z_0,Z_k)=F_m exp(-|k|), k!=0.
For reference, using lambda_m=-1+i omega_m, these follow from
 V_m=2 Re[(exp(lambda_m)-1-lambda_m)/lambda_m^2],
 Cov(Z_0,Z_k)=Re[exp(lambda_m k)
                *(exp(lambda_m)+exp(-lambda_m)-2)/lambda_m^2], k>=1.
Since exp(lambda_m)=exp(-1), every integer-frequency alias has the same
sampled exponential decay. Its variance and off-diagonal coefficient differ.

Define R_m=F_m/V_m. As a function of x>=0,
 R(x)=(C/2)*(1-x)/[e+(2-e)x],
 R'(x)=-C/[e+(2-e)x]^2<0.
Therefore, for every integer n>=2, R_0>R_1>R_n. Define strictly positive weights
 A_0 = V_1(R_1-R_n)/[(R_0-R_n)V_0],
 A_n = V_1(R_0-R_1)/[(R_0-R_n)V_n].
These exact real formulas satisfy
 A_0 V_0+A_n V_n=V_1,
 A_0 F_0+A_n F_n=F_1.

Model A: one oscillator m=1 with stationary coordinate variance a=1.
Model B_n: independent oscillators m=0 and m=n with stationary variances A_0
and A_n, observing the sum of their first coordinates. The hidden state is
four-dimensional, its diffusion matrix diag(A_0I_2,A_nI_2) is positive definite,
and the model is exponentially mixing. Both models have centered Gaussian
records. The displayed identities match variance and all nonzero-lag
covariances of the sampled averaged records. Consequently EVERY finite-
dimensional distribution of the entire doubly infinite sampled record agrees
exactly. This is not equality of only a finite covariance checklist.

Their latent continuous laws differ. For n=2,
 A_0 approximately 0.000457735094,
 A_2 approximately 3.85314606709;
latent variance is 1 for Model A versus A_0+A_2 approximately 3.85360380218.
Their ordinary entropy rates are
 sigma_A=8pi^2,
 sigma_B_n=8pi^2 n^2.
Thus the compatible entropy is unbounded above as n grows, despite known
nonzero exposure, no sensor noise and arbitrarily many samples at this FIXED
spacing. Each member is a finite-dimensional, uniformly elliptic model, but
no common uniform bound on diffusion coefficients or frequencies is imposed
across the sequence. The ambiguity is a sampling obstruction, not a nullspace
of the full continuous exposed law.

Implementation: exact_sampling.py; tests: test_exact_sampling.py; numerical

The analytical proof establishes all-time Gaussian-law equality. Floating-point

4. ADDITIVE NOISE: WHAT DENSE ACCESS DOES AND DOES NOT GIVE
Suppose observations are indexed by a countable dense set D of times,
 W_t=Z_t+eta_t, with Z continuous, eta_t iid, independent of Z, finite first
moment, and known mean b. For any t, choose distinct t_j in D with t_j->t.
Then, almost surely,
 (1/N)sum_{j=1}^N W_{t_j}->Z_t+b,
by continuity of Z and the iid strong law. Subtracting b recovers Z_t. Apply
this construction on a countable dense target set and extend continuously.
Thus arbitrarily dense fresh independent fixed-variance point observations
can recover the clean exposed path under this precise, infinitely informative
access model. The noise distribution need not otherwise be known; knowing its
mean and the independence/integrability contract suffices. An unknown mean
leaves an additive constant ambiguity, not automatically all centered dynamics.
The finite-first-moment assumption is a simple sufficient condition, not a
necessary condition for every known-noise model.

For population law deconvolution, a different sufficient condition is available:
if the additive independent noise's known joint characteristic function is
nonzero for every finite tuple and frequency vector, then divide the observed
joint characteristic function by it. A known Gaussian noise process has this
property, even if temporally correlated. This identifies the latent process
law at population level but does NOT pathwise separate two independent
continuous Gaussian processes from a single observed sum.

Conversely, unmodeled continuous sensor noise creates exact ambiguity even with
known exposure. Let X and N be independent centered OU processes with the same
decay rate and measure W=B_delta X+B_delta N. Here the clean exposed signal is
B_delta X and the independent detector noise is the continuous stationary
process B_delta N. The complete W law depends only on the sum of X and N's
variances. Any positive allocation of total variance between them produces the
same complete observation law. This is an unknown sensor-noise amplitude
example, not a counterexample when the whole noise law is known.

A continuous white-noise channel dO_t=Z_t dt+nu dW_t is not a continuum of
independent point readings of fixed variance. Dividing its interval increment
by the interval length gives noise variance nu^2/Delta, which increases as
sampling is refined. The dense averaging argument for fixed-variance point
noise cannot be imported into that physically different resource model.
Finite exposure, sensor bandwidth, per-reading energy, read time and heat must
be declared before comparing finite protocols.

5. UNKNOWN EXPOSURE IS NOT AUTOMATICALLY NONIDENTIFIABLE
For the earlier compact smooth uniformly elliptic class, a nonconstant centered
smooth detector has strictly positive power spectral density at all real
frequencies. Let u=(i omega-L)^-1 f on mean-zero L2(pi). The spectral gap makes
this resolvent well-defined, and
 S_Y(omega)=2 Re<f,u>=2 integral (grad u)^*D(grad u) d pi>0.
Equality would force u constant by connectedness and positive D, and therefore
f constant, contrary to the assumption. Thus a known boxcar SHAPE with unknown
positive width delta has exposed spectral density
 S_Z=|H_delta|^2 S_Y,
whose exact zero set is precisely {2pi k/delta:k!=0}. Its first positive zero
identifies delta at the ideal population level. This assertion needs the
specified diffusion/spectral positivity class and an uncorrupted or correctly
deconvolved continuous exposed law. Finite records do not observe exact zeros;
unknown correlated detector noise can fill them. Width estimation and stable
inversion remain separate tasks. The population_theory agent independently
checked this resolvent argument and owns its integration.

6. FINITE-DATA INSTABILITY
Even where the continuous-law inverse is injective, H_delta has discrete zeros
and decays at high frequency. There is no uniformly bounded inverse on all
mixing stationary signals without spectral restrictions. One can concentrate
continuous spectra arbitrarily close to a transfer zero while retaining no
spectral atoms; exposed power tends to zero although latent power stays fixed.
That is approximate loss and poor conditioning, not exact law nonidentifiability.

Root separately owns a stronger compact-diffusion finite-access construction:
fixed-gamma drifting cosine-circle signals at increasingly large |omega| have
boxcar variance O(omega^-2) and physical ordinary entropy omega^2/gamma.
For finitely many windows with independent Gaussian read noise of variance nu^2,
convexity of KL bounds divergence from pure noise by n Var(boxcar)/(2nu^2),
even when latent exposed readings are correlated. That obstruction should be
integrated only after its independent review. It is not tested or claimed as
part of this countermodel implementation.
