ESTABLISHED INGREDIENTS
1. Hidden-model-compatible entropy optimization: Ehrich2021 and Nitzan2023.
   These precede the present program. Optimizing a selected hidden architecture
   is not equivalent to solving every hidden realization. Busiello2019 explains
   microscopic current loss under coarse graining; Buchholz1994 is the standard
   exact/ordinary lumpability reference (metadata verified, full text not read).
2. One resolved edge can recover entropy in a ring: Harunari et al2022 SecV.2,
   Eqs57-58. Asymptotic identification does not give finite-sample robust upper
   confidence bounds when reverse rates may approach zero. The fixed four-state
   finite-budget quantitative obstruction here has not been located verbatim;
   absence from this focused search is not a priority certification.
3. Scalar reversible correlations have positive spectral measures. Calibration
   of mean and same-hidden-state replicate response narrows this familiar class.
   Dechant2023/Shiraishi2023 are broad correlation baselines; Raux2026 uses
   cross-correlation asymmetry, so its numerator vanishes for one observable.
   Its shift optimization already uses means and must not be claimed as new.
4. Variance sum rule: DiTerlizzi et alScience2024 and NJP2024. NJP Sec4 Eq35
   gives the switching-trap correlation. Their curvature criterion Eq15 already
   diagnoses strong nonequilibrium. A finite-lag calibrated confidence procedure
   may improve access/statistics but does not discover curvature detection.
   Force-assisted VSR is a different-access benchmark, not a matched scalar test.
5. Composite-null e-variable/RIPr duality: Larsson,Ramdas,Ruf2025 (Secs2-5);
   constraint-generated hypotheses: sameauthors2026 Thm2.2,Thm3.1,Cor3.3,Sec7;
   Safe testing Grunwald et al2024. Products require conditional null validity,
   not stationarity alone. Finite-dimensional optimization is established.
6. Markov Hoeffding: Fan,Jiang,Sun2021 Theorem1 Eqs3-4, stationary chain and
   absolute mean-zero L2 contraction rho<1, bounded time-varying functions;
   tail exp[-2n eps^2(1-rho)/(1+rho)] for range[0,1]. No reversibility needed.
   Path-block observations require their own valid block-chain contraction.
   Published PDF's common-function right-gap sharpening is labelled Theorem3;
   the base theorem suffices. Paulin2015 is already in cumulative references.

ACTIVITY-CALIBRATED DIVERGENCE BOUND
Normalize stationary directed edge fluxes Pij=pi_i qij/A and R=Ptranspose.
Then JS(P,R)=d/A and half-Jeffreys(P,R)=[KL(P||R)+KL(R||P)]/2=sigma/A.
The sum convention would instead be2sigma/A; state the convention explicitly.
Crooks2008 TechnicalNote004v2 Eq1 gives
  JS <= min{halfJeffreys/4, log[2/(1+exp(-halfJeffreys))]}.
Correct primary PDF is https://threeplusone.com/pubs/on_jensenshannon.pdf.
The older /Crooks-inequality.pdf endpoint returned an HTML access challenge;
that response is preserved and accurately labelled, not represented as a PDF.
Falasco,Esposito,Delvenne2022 AppendixC Eqs97-100 recovers this nonlinear
comparison within a thermodynamic-response/speed-limit treatment.
Guntuboyina,Saha,Schiebinger2014 Theorem2.1 gives general sharp-f-divergence
optimization using finite-support distributions; it is an important prior
framework, but its inspected examples do not state the proposed exact
JS-to-half-Jeffreys binary envelope. Sason2015 Props3-4 separately minimize
JS and Jeffreys at fixed TV; two such lower bounds alone do NOT establish a
sharp comparison between them. Yamano2014 conference abstract discusses
Crooks/binary comparisons; full manuscript download failed. Do not use that
abstract to assert either overlap or non-overlap of the exact formula.

Current candidate sigma>=A e(jinverse(d/A)), with j=.5[(1+x)ln(1+x)+
(1-x)ln(1-x)] and e=xln[(1+x)/(1-x)], is a symmetric-divergence specialization
proved directly in this release. Literature search did not settle historical
priority of this precise envelope. Operational use with a calibrated activity
upper bound and certified record-KL lower bound must be presented separately.
Uniform-bias-ring full-path equality does not imply endpoint-data tightness.

POSITIONING
Strongest defensible advance is an explicit chain from access and calibration
to checkable finite-record certificates and equally explicit inference limits.
Do not claim a new universal thermodynamic law, first correlation detector,
first log-optimal test, universal total-dissipation reconstruction, or empirical
validation from simulated models. New conditional theorems and implementations
can be assessed on their own precise scope without those claims.


OVERDAMPED DIFFUSION TRANSFER AUDIT (2026-10-12)
Established ingredients, not a new entropy formula: Girsanov1960 (ciGirsanov1960; DOI10.1137/1105027) gives the drift-change exponential likelihood. Its primary publisher abstract was read and metadata verified; the complete paywalled article was not accessed. With equal stationary initial density and noise covariance 2D, the expected log likelihood to b_s=D grad log pi is t times integral pi v^T D^-1 v /4. Seifert2012 (ciSeifert2012; DOI10.1088/0034-4885/75/12/126001), author full text arXiv:1205.4176, Eq35 and Sec2.6.2 establish the entropy functional and multidimensional setting. The manuscript supplies its own regularity assumptions and deduction, restricted to even-variable overdamped diffusion; finite CTMC jump activity has no automatic continuum transfer.
The Gaussian sign formula is classical: ciVanVleck1966, DOI10.1109/PROC.1966.4567, publisher metadata verified. Original full text not accessed. Formula was independently verified in Eq13 of the primary full-text XML for Sensors11,6066-6087(2011), DOI10.3390/s110606066; reference8 cites Van Vleck and Middleton. The resulting rotational Ornstein-Uhlenbeck example is an analytic illustration, not empirical validation.
Three necessary citations were added to phase and current cumulative bibliography; archived editions were unchanged. No physical data contract is established by these citations.

Final example refinement: the worked diffusion example uses a bounded sine observable and a Gaussian moment-generating-function identity. The unused Van Vleck bibliography entry was removed from phase and release; its verified source audit remains. Two diffusion references are included.
