1. What is calibrated
Let X have stationary law pi. A noninvasive binary reading Y in{-1,1} has fixed
conditional mean f_i=E[Y|X=i]. Define
  m=sum pi_i f_i, A=sum pi_i f_i^2, u=m^2, 0<=u<=A<=1.
For two readings with conditionally independent noise at the SAME UNCHANGED
hidden state, E[Y^(1)Y^(2)]=A, so their agreement probability is(1+A)/2.
A is not E[Y^2], which is identically1. Within-pair readings are generally
marginally dependent through X; treating them as2N independent observations is
incorrect. Independent same-state pairs across records suffice for the API.

For instantaneous detectors m and A agree under Q and its additive reversible
reference S, since they use the same pi and f. They need not agree for nonlinear
finite-exposure path functionals. The previous disjoint-block contract therefore
requires separate reference calibration in that case. A deterministic binary
state detector already has A=1. A trusted observation of f(X) gives A directly
through f(X)^2; this is additional access and bypasses the binary-only obstruction.

1a. Upper calibration from a noisy analog channel
For bounded Z in[-1,1] produced by a fixed memoryless noninvasive channel,
 f_i=E[Z|X=i], m=E Z, A=E f(X)^2 <= B=E Z^2,
with B-A=E Var(Z|X). Distinct-time product correlations equal the desired hidden
observable correlations when endpoint noise is conditionally independent.
The same pi and detector channel under Q and S imply the same m and B.
A valid upper bound B_U therefore supplies A<=B_U without same-state twins.
A lower bound on B does NOT supply a lower bound on A. For binary Z, B=1 and
this shortcut is vacuous. For Z=X/4+epsilon/2 with independent fair signs,
A=1/16 but B=5/16. Two random binary encodings of the same noisy analog Z have
product expectation B, not A, because they share epsilon.
analog_vertices accepts any valid simultaneous mean/Bupper bounds, including
ones obtained with a dependence-aware calibration. analog_calibration_summary
is explicitly restricted to iid calibration records and uses separate Hoeffding
radii for bounded[-1,1] means and bounded[0,1] squares. It must not be applied
to an untreated dependent trajectory. This corollary does not transfer arbitrary
nonlinear exposure second moments from Q to S.

2. Exact calibrated comparison class
For deterministic commensurate positive lags k Delta, the CLOSED correlation
class of finite stationary reversible chains with fixed(m,A) is exactly
  C_k = u+(A-u) integral_[0,1] x^k mu(dx),
  q_k = (1+C_k)/2,
where mu is a probability measure. The result includes all finite menus of
positive integer k. Atom0 is a closure point corresponding to arbitrarily fast
relaxation. Detector noise affects A but does not alter the positivity proof.

Necessity is the established reversible spectral theorem. To prove sharpness,
first take any finite atomic mu. If A=u, a constant detector f=m suffices.
Otherwise m<1 and the two detector values
  a=(m-A)/(1-m), 1
lie in[-1,1]. Let r=(m-a)/(1-a). In every spectral block use stationary weights
(r,1-r), detector(1,a), and generator
  Q_x=(-log x / Delta)(1 pi-I).
Its mean is m, its second moment A, and its correlation is
m^2+(A-m^2)x^k. Mix blocks with the atomic weights. Positive atoms have finite
rates; replace zero atoms by small positive atoms for arbitrary accuracy.
Any finite moment vector has an atomic representation with at mostK+1 atoms,
by the finite-dimensional convex hull theorem. Thus at most2(K+1) block states
suffice before any simplification. Adding epsilon(1 pi-I) makes the full model
irreducible without changing m,A,pi, and converges to the desired moments as
epsilon goes to zero. The closed class is therefore sharp even if physical
comparison models are required to be irreducible.

Clock note: a compatible random-clock family preserves this calibrated cone as
an outer class. It need not realize every point of the deterministic-lag cone;
the sharpness statement is specifically for commensurate deterministic lags.

3. Uncertain calibration and support certificates
A simultaneous interval m in[mL,mU] maps exactly to u in[uL,uU], with uL=0 if
the interval crosses zero and otherwise its smallest endpoint square; uU is the
largest endpoint square. Together with A in[AL,AU], the feasible parameter set is
that rectangle intersected with0<=u<=A<=1. calibration_vertices returns exact
rational vertices. No physical model is inferred if the confidence region is
empty; that is reported as an incompatible calibration region.

For coefficients c_k and p_c(x)=sum c_k x^k, the support of the correlation class
at fixed(u,A) is
  u sum c_k+(A-u) max_{x in[0,1]} p_c(x).
For agreement probabilities, add(sum c_k)/2 and divide the correlation support
by2. Optimizing over the parameter polygon reduces to its vertices. Equivalently
the full calibrated class is the convex hull of q(u,A,x) at polygon vertices and
x in[0,1]: mixing vertices produces an effective(u,A), and its residual measure
is reweighted by A-u. All weights are nonnegative. The API certifies the
continuous polynomial maximum using rational Bernstein subdivision; a grid is
used only for a lower comparison, not as the upper certificate.

Without a relaxation-rate upper bound, fixed-A classes are nested in A at fixedu.
Extra variance can be put at x=0. Therefore an upper A calibration sharpens
positive-lag inference, while a lower A alone does not force more visible signal.
A lower bound on spectral x (an upper relaxation-rate bound) changes this fact.

For iid same-state pair records, counts(++,+-,-+,--) give
 mhat=(n++-n--)/N,
 Ahat=(n+++n---n+--n-+)/N.
Each is an average of N bounded[-1,1] variables. A common radius
 sqrt(2 log(4/alpha)/N)
gives a simultaneous confidence rectangle by Hoeffding and a union bound, without
assuming within-pair independence except where it identifies A. Calibration
failure probability must be added once to the subsequent inference error.

4. A test not requiring a negative correlation or revival
At lags t,2t, x-x^2<=1/4 gives the sharp population bound
  q(t)-q(2t) <= (A-m^2)/8.
A single reversible two-state block with x=1/2 attains it. Calibrating A therefore
can detect decay that is too steep even when both correlations stay positive and
strictly decrease. This is a consequence of the calibrated spectral class, not
a claim that monotone decay universally signifies dissipation.

Synthetic finite-bias example: four-state cycle with clockwise rate3, reverse
rate1, uniform stationary law and conditional detector means
(1/2,1/2,-1/2,-1/2). Then m=0,A=1/4 and
 C(t)=(1/4)e^(-4t)cos(2t).
At t=7/40 and2t, exact series bounds certify q(t)-q(2t)>
0.03473386533798, exceeding the calibrated reversible limit1/32 by more than
0.00348386533798. Both correlations are positive. The uncalibrated contrast
ceiling1/8 does not detect this example. The true model entropy rate is2 log3,
which is not asserted to equal the record-inferred lower bound. All are population
calculations; no fabricated samples or claimed experimental sensitivity.

5. Exact finite-menu nonidentifiability of A
For every K>=1 there are finite IRREDUCIBLE reversible models with identical
binary-pair probability laws at every integer lag1,...,K and different A.
Let x_j=j/(K+2), j=1,...,K+1, and signed coefficients
 v_j=(-1)^j binom(K+1,j).
For1<=k<=K, sum v_j x_j^k=0 by the vanishing(K+1)st finite difference of a
degree-k polynomial. But sum v_j=-1. Split the positive and negative coefficients
and multiply them by s=(2^(K+1)-1)^(-2). The resulting positive spectral measures
have equal positive moments and different total masses.

An explicit irreducible realization of each measure uses independent symmetric
telegraph coordinates Z_j with decay x_j^t and the bounded stochastic detector
 f(Z)=sum_j sqrt(s|v_j|) Z_j.
The sum of absolute detector amplitudes is at most
 sqrt(s) sum|v_j|<=1, so f lies in[-1,1]. Independent coordinates make the
correlation exactly the desired spectral sum. All coordinate rates are positive
and finite, hence their product chain is irreducible. Detector mean is zero.
The full stationary binary-pair law is determined by C(t):
 P(Y0=a,Yt=b)=(1+ab C(t))/4.
Thus every amount of independent sampling at the unchanged finite pair menu is
incapable of identifying A. Higher-order temporal records, known detector shape,
true same-state replicate access, or further model restrictions can distinguish
the models. The theorem does not equate their full output trajectories.

A small stronger-scaled example has high spectrum weights3/16 atx=1/4 and1/16
atx=3/4 (A=1/4), versus weight3/16 atx=1/2 (A=3/16). Both give C1=3/32 and
C2=3/64. They require respectively4 and2 hidden states, are irreducible, and
have all exit rates below1. Consequently even a finite rate cap does not imply
exact identification from a fixed finite positive-lag menu.

6. Uniform finite-precision obstruction, beyond a fixed menu
Compare iid fair binary outputs with a reversible two-state chain having detector
means +/-sqrt(A) and correlation A e^(-lambda t). Their binary-pair TV distance
is A e^(-lambda t)/2. For N independent pair records, each at lag>=t_min>0,
transcript TV is at most N A e^(-lambda t_min)/2. Thus fixed order-one differences
in A can be hidden at arbitrary finite precision by increasing lambda, even
though every model has zero stationary entropy production. With N=1000,t_min=.1,
A=1,lambda=200, the exact upper bound is below1.031e-6.
The statement extends to any finite independent clock menu with no atom at zero:
E e^(-lambda T) tends to0 by dominated convergence. An atom at genuine same-state
zero time is different. Exact noiseless knowledge of an entire analytic correlation
function on an interval is also different; no finite-data claim is made for that
idealized access.

7. What a kinetic cap repairs
For any stationary finite homogeneous CTMC, including nonreversible ones,
  A-C(t) = E[(f(X_t)-f(X_0))^2]/2 >=0.
If every exit rate is at most Lambda, a first-jump bound gives
  0<=A-C(t)<=2(1-e^(-Lambda t)).
This provides a rigorous interval for A from a short delayed-pair confidence
interval, without assuming the reversible null that is later tested. An average
stationary jump-rate bound J also gives A-C(t)<=2Jt. Rate information repairs
uniform approximation as t approaches zero; it does not assert exact finite-menu
identifiability. A bound on energy dissipation alone supplies neither kinetic
cap: the arbitrarily fast reversible countermodels dissipate no stationary heat.
Converting a separately measured activity bound into an energetic constraint
requires a stated microscopic energy-per-event assumption.

8. Calibration failures that can mislead inference
If Y1=Y2 is the same noisy bit, the product is1 even when f=0 and A=0.
More seriously, conditionally anti-correlated readout noise can underestimate A.
For hidden means +/-1/2, use conditional joint laws(++, +-, -+, --)
 (1/2,1/4,1/4,0) and(0,1/4,1/4,1/2).
Both readers have the correct marginal mean, but their product expectation is0
instead of A=1/4. Pooled calibration looks feasible and can incorrectly exclude
an equilibrium positive correlation. Conditional independence is a physical
premise, not something verified by the marginal count table alone. Sequential
measurements with unbounded unknown hidden motion or measurement backaction do
not implement the same-state protocol.
