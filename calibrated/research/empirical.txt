EXPLORATORY EXPERIMENTAL REANALYSIS PLAN
This plan is written before the new held-out correlation calculations. Earlier
source and whole-file descriptive audits have already been performed, so this
is explicitly exploratory, not a prospectively blinded physical experiment.

Fixed split: first10000 samples for transform/OU design; remaining39999 for
evaluation. Nominal dt=10microseconds. Fixed lag grid in sample units:
0,1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128,256. No grid extension or lag selection from results.

For the simultaneous n1 and lower-noise reference: subtract each channel's
training mean and use a common scale equal to twice the square root of their
average training variance. Bounded transform is tanh(centered_position/scale).
For n2, use its own training mean and twice its training standard deviation.
No fitted threshold, scale or center will use evaluation observations.

Primary diagnostic model: both simultaneous channels equal the same physical
position plus zero-mean, temporally white measurement errors independent of
each other and the particle. Under this explicit unit-gain model, the channel
difference has zero positive-lag covariance; nonzero-lag auto/cross covariances
coincide; zero-lag cross covariance equals latent position variance. The source
does not establish these assumptions. Quantify residual autocorrelation and
cross-channel lag asymmetry, with eight contiguous evaluation blocks as a
sensitivity analysis. Block ranges are not confidence intervals or independent
replications. Blocks never contribute pairs spanning their boundaries.

Fit one naive OU covariance to the symmetrized training cross covariance at
lags1,2,4,8,16 by least squares of log covariance against nominal time, only if
all are positive and fitted decay rate positive. Keep the fit regardless of
evaluation performance. Evaluate at every declared positive lag; report errors
normalized by evaluation cross covariance at zero. This is a model diagnostic,
not a certified mixing bound or noise correction.

At each fixed channel transform, compute positive-lag covariance using the
evaluation-wide mean, and the observed zero-lag variance as an upper-only
population surrogate for the hidden detector variance. Report finite-menu
nonnegativity, monotonicity at doubling lags, upper drop g(k)-g(2k)<=v/4,
and centered log-convexity g(k)g(4k)>=g(2k)^2, plus block sensitivity.
These are plug-in descriptive checks under a stationary reversible overdamped
Markov model and memoryless detector. No p-values, alpha levels, iid intervals,
or dissipation confidence numbers will be manufactured from this trajectory.

Exposure accounting:39999 evaluation samples span nominal.39998seconds;
paired detectors observe the same exposure, not twice the physical duration.
All overlapping lag pairs are statistically dependent. Training/calibration
time is an additional nominal.1seconds. The n2 trajectory is a separate
experimental record; its sampling offset relative to n1 is undocumented.

The source data remain immutable and retain CC-BY-4.0 attribution to Bryan IV,
Basak, Bechhoefer and Presse, Zenodo DOI10.5281/zenodo.6680673. Results will
state the precise remaining requirements for valid thermodynamic inference.

MATHEMATICAL CORRECTION BEFORE INTERPRETATION
The originally written g(k)g(4k)>=g(2k)^2 line above is invalid for geometric
lags, as a pure exponential immediately shows. It was caught during analytic
review after the first calculations, before any empirical claim. The correct
weighted Holder inequality is g(k)^2 g(4k)>=g(2k)^3, since2k=(2/3)k+(1/3)4k.
Code and tests use only this corrected expression. No lag, transformation or
data split changed. This correction is preserved here rather than concealed.
