1. Scope and first physical-access challenge

For a stationary hidden CTMC and fixed noninvasive binary endpoint channel, let
g(i)=E[Y|X=i], m=E_pi[g], and A=E_pi[g^2]. The additive reversibilization shares
pi and g, hence both m and A. This is the valid reason calibration can narrow
the reversible comparison class. It does not verify the physical detector model.
Two conditionally independent readouts of the SAME unchanged state estimate A
through their sign product. Squaring one retained bit always gives1. Backaction,
detector memory, or an uncontrolled delay can change the measured object.

Positive-time endpoint pairs do not automatically supply this zero-lag access.
The exact two-lag example from the spectral branch survives independent matrix
exponentiation: a four-state independent-telegraph product with detector
g=(sqrt(3)Z1+Z2)/4, eigenvalues1/4 and3/4 per unit time, has A=1/4; a two-state
telegraph with g=sqrt(3)Z/4 and eigenvalue1/2 has A=3/16. Both are irreducible
equilibrium processes of mean0 with C1=3/32,C2=3/64. Every binary pair law at
these two lags agrees. Their multi-time laws need not agree. Arbitrarily fast
additional modes also show why approximate positive-time calibration requires
either a rate bound or explicit finite-delay control.

The sharp closed cone construction checks out. For m<1, A in[m^2,1], take a
two-valued detector {a,1}, a=(m-A)/(1-m), stationary mass r=(m-a)/(1-a) at1,
and transition rates lambda*r and lambda*(1-r). Its covariance is
m^2+(A-m^2)exp(-lambda*t). Mixtures of such blocks realize finite spectral
mixtures with common m,A. Handle m=1 separately. Zero modes/reducible blocks
and infinite-rate atoms can be closure points, not exact finite irreducible
realizations. The code/manuscript must maintain that distinction.

Independent test construction: an eight-state driven cycle with Fourier damping
0.2 and angular frequency0.4 has calibrated A=1/2 and positive two-lag
correlations violating C2>=C1^2/A. Those same correlations have an exact
two-state reversible fit if A is left unknown. Thus the proposed calibration
has a concrete mathematical benefit; it is not merely a change of notation.

2. Information relaxation versus the physical entropy minimum

The calibrated KL projection is a lower-bound relaxation. Its optimizing
comparison point need not be the additive reversibilization of a physical model
that reproduces the observed data under the same pi, detector and interventions.
A primal feasible pair brackets the information optimization only. It supplies
neither an upper bound on physical entropy production nor a proof that the
thermodynamic lower bound is physically attainable.

There is a simple strong-driving obstruction even before endpoint coarsening.
For a fully observed four-state cycle of total activity kappa and clockwise
probability p, its path KL rate to additive reversibilization is
  d=kappa[p log(2p)+(1-p)log(2(1-p))] <= kappa log2,
but physical entropy production is
  sigma=kappa(2p-1)log[p/(1-p)].
As p tends to1, d remains bounded while sigma diverges. The linear sigma>=4d
bridge is locally sharp near equilibrium but has unbounded strong-driving slack.
An improved cone optimizer cannot remove this loss by itself.

The thermodynamic branch's dual checks out for compact convex probability sets
P and calibrated reversible class K: any positive score h gives
  inf_{p inP,q inK} KL_w(p||q) >= inf_P E_p logh - log sup_K E_q h.
For an interior minimizer h_y=p*_y/q*_y, first-order optimality in q makes
sup E_q h=1, and optimality in p makes inf E_p logh=KL_w(p*||q*).
Boundary attainment requires limits. Empty P needs an explicit inconsistency
status, not an entropy claim. Numerical optimization proposes witnesses; exact
polynomial support, logarithm enclosures and linear minimization certify them.

3. Candidate additional physical calibration: nonlinear activity bridge

Independent review derived and sent this candidate to the thermodynamic branch.
Let a_e,b_e be stationary opposite edge fluxes; put w_e=a_e+b_e and
x_e=|a_e-b_e|/(a_e+b_e). Define
  j(x)=((1+x)log(1+x)+(1-x)log(1-x))/2,
  e(x)=2x atanh(x).
Then activity kappa=sum w_e, path KL rate d=sum w_e j(x_e), and
sigma=sum w_e e(x_e). On(0,1), j'(x)=atanh(x), and
  de/dj=2+2x/((1-x^2)atanh(x)).
This derivative is increasing: differentiating its nonconstant ratio has sign
((1+x^2)atanh(x)-x)>0. Thus F=e composed with j^{-1} is convex, increasing,
and F(0)=0. Jensen gives sigma>=kappa*F(d/kappa). If an independently justified
activity UPPER bound K>=kappa is available, the perspective decreases with its
first argument, giving sigma>=K*F(d/K). A certified record KL lower bound J
and independent chosen mean path duration T imply d>=J/T, hence
  sigma >= K*e(j^{-1}(J/(K*T))).
This strictly strengthens4J/T for positive feasible arguments, approaches it as
K grows, and is sharp at the full-path level when all active edges have the same
absolute asymmetry. The four-state cycle realizes equality. Arguments exceeding
log2 signal incompatible calibration/model assumptions; equality needs explicit
handling of vanishing reverse rates/infinite entropy. No activity cap follows
from coarse readouts alone. This calibration is additional physical access.

Independent numerical tests check the inequality on100 randomized edge mixtures,
its monotonic weakening under a looser activity cap, exact equal-bias saturation,
and the uncalibrated large-K limit. These are supporting checks, not the proof.

4. Total-dissipation identification boundary

The access branch's four-state parity example is substantive and sound. Each
physical jump changes the observed parity: no invisible jumps or independent
spectator are needed. At fixed activity kappa, graph, dimension and stationary
distribution, the entire observed process is the same symmetric two-state
telegraph process for all clockwise probabilities p. Yet entropy spans0 to
arbitrarily large values. The detector is deterministic, so A=1 is already
known perfectly. Therefore zero-lag calibration cannot universally identify total
dissipation; its value is to tighten lower bounds for informative records.

Breaking lumpability by giving four states distinct known exits r_i leaves
pi_i proportional to1/r_i independent of bias. Coupling to the one-direction
limit on any stopped exposure at mostL gives TV<=r_max*L*(1-p). This supports
the uniform finite-upper-confidence obstruction even for resolved states.
For direction-count repair, a fixed number of jumps gives Bernoulli marks;
conditioning on the random number of jumps in a fixed exposure is not generally
binomial when exits differ. The access branch was alerted to this distinction.

5. Predictable sequential contrasts

Root's proposed sequential contrast e-process checks algebraically. Predictable
coefficients a,b, nonnegative stake lambda and calibrated bias budgets allow
  S=sum lambda(a.Y-b-bias)-sum lambda^2||a||^2/8,
  H=sum lambda*sum_k |a_k|sqrt(t_k).
For true stationary entropy sigma, exp(S-H*sqrt(sigma/8)) is a supermartingale
provided EACH outcome has the required conditional mean envelope, including
conditioning on earlier outcomes within the same batch. Marginal stationarity
or an unconditional vector mean is insufficient. Ville at the single true
sigma gives the anytime lower bound8[(S-log(1/alpha))_+/H]^2; no union over
all candidate sigma is needed. H=0 abstains.

Learning a and stakes from completed past data is allowed. Physical Q and its
stationary entropy must stay fixed, or a different cumulative target must be
proved. If calibration intervals update using the same history, add their
simultaneous failure probability and stop the comparison supermartingale at
the first predictable invalid calibration/support event. Simply conditioning
the proof on a good calibration event can invalidate the martingale argument.

6. Common-block dependent records

The sampling branch uses a deterministic common block duration H, an externally
calibrated hidden-state L2 contraction rho<1, and a schedule fixed before the
evaluated data. For each bounded block statistic Z_i, write
Z_i=g_i(X_i)+D_i, where conditional on the full hidden past at the start state,
E[D_i]=0 and D_i has conditional range length1. The established Fan--Jiang--Sun
bound controls the state-function part with factor A=(1+rho)/(1-rho).
Conditional Hoeffding controls the D part with factor1. Holder with exponents
p=1+1/sqrt(A),q=1+sqrt(A) gives total factor pA+q=(sqrt(A)+1)^2.
The algebra checks. The common-block schedule and fixed functions are essential;
an outcome-adaptive schedule is not established by this argument.

If the initial law is within beta of stationarity in TV, the entire future
record law is within beta of the stationary reference, so beta is paid once.
The hidden contraction calibration has its own failure budget. Observed
autocorrelation cannot replace that hidden-state calibration.

A shared hidden-state endpoint at the boundary of two blocks is allowed. With
a stochastic detector, however, reusing a previously retained noisy endpoint
bit can violate the stated g_i(X_i) conditional-mean property; fresh conditional
detector noise or a calibrated augmented state is required. A deterministic
detector already has no separate private noise to reuse. The conservative
Holder factor tends4, not1, when rho tends0; no sharp IID-limit claim is made.

Independent transfer-matrix calculations verify the block-record MGF envelope
for a biased two-state CTMC with noisy endpoint readouts and mixed-sign weights.
A stationary slow-state control exceeds the naive IID MGF upper bound by over
a factor ten. The new count sampler was checked against a separately assembled
four-state matrix exponential for the agreement probability, final-sector
probability and their joint probability after padding;6000 seeded draws agree
within six worst-case binomial standard deviations.
